## How long after breast augmentation can I swim?

An [article on the ASPS website by Dr. Cindy Wu](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/considering-breast-augmentation-for-beach-season-heres-when-you-should-plan-your-surgery) says most surgeons want you to wait at least a month before immersing in a pool or ocean. The rule matters more than the number: a [UW Medicine patient handout](https://healthonline.washington.edu/document/health_online/pdf/Breast-Augmentation.pdf) says not to take a bath, sit in a hot tub or go swimming until your incisions are fully healed, and [University of Utah Health](https://healthcare.utah.edu/plastic-surgery/breast/recovery) says the same about a bathtub, pool or hot tub.

You will often see this quoted as 4 to 6 weeks. The only written range we found comes from a [UW Health handout on breast reconstruction incisions](https://patient.uwhealth.org/healthfacts/8525), which is a different operation, so treat it as a rough guide. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm), whose page covers cosmetic breast surgery in general, lifts and reductions included, gives no number: it says to wait until stitches and drains are out and your surgeon says it is OK.

Showers come much sooner. The UW Medicine handout allows them 48 hours after surgery, washing very gently with mild soap. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/aftercare-recovery) gives a wider range of 1 to 7 days, so go by what your surgeon told you.

## Does the type of water change the wait?

No. None of the sources we read gives a pool, the ocean, a hot tub or a bath its own number of weeks, so one rule covers all of them: not until your incisions are fully closed and your surgeon agrees.

## How long after breast augmentation can I swim in the ocean?

The same as everywhere else: at least about a month, and until your incisions are fully closed and your surgeon agrees. Dr. Wu's article puts pools and the ocean together under "at least a month".

This matters in Florida more than in most places. A [CDC report](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10843064/) says Vibrio vulnificus, a waterborne bacterium, can infect open wounds through contact with salt water or brackish water, and it advises keeping open wounds out of that water. A [study of Florida cases from 1981 to 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8627070/), so an old one, found that wound infections from this family of bacteria clustered in the warmer months.

Keep that in proportion. None of these sources is about surgical incisions, and none says a closed scar is at risk. They are general advice about open wounds, and that is exactly why a fully closed incision is the line to wait for.

Once you are cleared, think about what the water asks of your chest as well. Surfing and paddling on a board mean lying on your front and pushing with your arms. [Sientra's patient labeling](https://sientra.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MDC-0010-R6-Sientra-Patient-Educational-Brochure-Augmentation-1.pdf) for its silicone implants says that after healing you should be able to return to normal activities, including sports, while avoiding situations that put a lot of pressure on your breasts.

## How long after breast augmentation can I swim in the pool or hot tub?

The same rule applies to both: at least about a month, and until your incisions are fully closed and your surgeon agrees. We found no source saying that chlorine makes a pool safe any sooner, and none that sets a separate, later date for a hot tub, so if you're unsure about the heat of a hot tub, ask your surgeon when you ask about swimming.

## How do I know my incisions are ready for swimming?

Your surgeon decides, and it's worth asking at a visit rather than judging for yourself. No source we read gives a visual checklist, but these are reasonable questions to run through first:

- **Is the whole incision closed?** Nothing should be open or gaping. An incision that opens is a reason to call right away.
- **Is it dry and calm?** MedlinePlus says to check daily for signs of infection: redness, pain or drainage.
- **Are the stitches and strips finished?** The UW Medicine handout keeps Steri-Strips on until they fall off, and has you remove them after 2 weeks if they are still on. Buried stitches can take longer: [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/15709-incision-care), writing about surgical wounds in general, says absorbable stitches can last for a few months. See [when breast augmentation stitches dissolve](/when-do-breast-augmentation-stitches-dissolve).
- **Has your surgeon said yes?** Dr. Wu's article says to get clearance to swim after your surgery, and this is the step that counts.

[Where the incisions go](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#placement) also changes what a swimsuit rubs against, so mention yours when you ask.

## Which water activities are safe during recovery?

Before you're cleared, none that put your chest or incisions in the water. We found no source that approves wading, water aerobics or sitting on a pool step during this time, so we don't suggest them. Sitting in the shade by the water with your incisions dry is fine.

After you're cleared to get in, being in the water and swimming laps are two separate permissions. Laps work your arms and chest over and over. An [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/back-to-active-returning-to-exercise-after-breast-augmentation-recovery) says strenuous activities and upper body movements should be avoided for at least six weeks, and [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation) says returning to sports may take up to 6 weeks. So you might float at week 5 and still be waiting on laps. Our [week-by-week exercise plan](/blog/when-can-you-work-out-after-breast-augmentation) shows where swimming fits with everything else.

## How long after breast augmentation can I wear a bathing suit?

No source gives a date, so here is what they do say. University handouts keep a surgical bra on around the clock for 3 to 4 weeks, off only to shower, and your surgeon sets the length. University of Utah Health moves you to a soft, supportive sports bra after that. So a swim top comes no earlier than the point your surgeon lets you out of the surgical bra.

The UW Medicine handout says no underwire until your incisions are fully healed, so that rules out a wired swim top for now. A soft, wire-free top with good coverage is the easier choice, and it shades new scars from the sun. Our guide to [when you can go without a bra](/when-can-i-sleep-without-a-bra-after-breast-augmentation) covers the rest of the bra timeline.

## How do I protect new scars from the sun?

Keep them covered, or use SPF 30 or higher whenever you are outside. Sientra's labeling lists sun exposure among the things to avoid for the first month after surgery. After that, MedlinePlus says to protect scars with a strong sunblock of SPF 30 or higher whenever you are out in the sun. Dr. Maman's article gives the reason: UV rays can permanently darken fresh scars, even after you feel fully healed.

South Florida's sun is year-round, so this is a daily habit here and not a summer one. Some practical advice, not from a study: a long-sleeved rash guard or a loose shirt works well, and a hat and shade help. Put sunscreen on only once the incision has closed and your surgeon agrees, and reapply it after you get out of the water.

No source says exactly when you can stop. ASPS says incision lines [may take several months, even a couple of years, to fade](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/results), so keep protecting them while they are still pink or dark, and read our guide to [how breast augmentation scars fade](/blog/breast-augmentation-scars-miami-timeline) for what to expect.

## How far ahead should I plan surgery before a beach trip?

At least a month ahead if you only need to get in the water, and longer if you care how you look in a swimsuit. Dr. Wu's article says to plan surgery at least a month before a vacation, but it also says implants normally still sit high after the first month and settle over the next 2 to 3 months. Our [drop and fluff timeline](/blog/breast-implants-drop-fluff-timeline) goes through this. University of Utah Health says you begin to notice your final results around three months, with subtle changes through 12 months, and an [article on the ASPS website by Dr. Eric Mariotti](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-does-drop-and-fluff-mean-after-breast-augmentation) says the whole process can take 3 to 6 months.

If you're [coming to Miami from another state](/fly-in-consultation), factor in the trip home too. Dr. Karlinsky tells you, in writing, how many nights to stay in Miami before you fly home.

## Frequently asked questions

### When can I take a bath after breast augmentation?

At least about a month, and not until your incisions are fully closed and your surgeon agrees. It's often quoted as 4 to 6 weeks, from a breast reconstruction handout. A UW Medicine patient handout groups baths with hot tubs and swimming, and an article on the ASPS website by Dr. Daniel Maman says soaking too soon can raise your risk of infection. Showers come sooner, about 2 days after surgery.

### What happens if I swim too early?

The main concern is infection at an incision that hasn't closed. If you got in the water before you were cleared, don't panic. Watch for redness, heat, drainage or increasing pain, and call your surgeon if you notice any of these or a fever. Tell them about the swim either way, since it helps them judge what they're seeing.

### Can I swim with stitches or Steri-Strips?

Not until your surgeon clears you. MedlinePlus says to stay out of the water until stitches and drains are removed and your surgeon says it is OK. As general knowledge, dissolving stitches can remain under healed skin for a while, so your surgeon's clearance is what counts, not whether the material has fully absorbed.

### Is swimming good exercise after breast augmentation?

Yes once you are fully cleared, but it isn't an early activity. No study we found shows that swimming speeds up recovery after breast augmentation. Walking is the early exercise; laps come later because they use your arms and chest. An article on the ASPS website says upper body movements should wait at least six weeks.

### When can I go to the beach after breast augmentation?

You can sit on the beach well before you can swim, if you feel up to it and keep your incisions covered and out of the sun. Sientra's patient labeling says to avoid sun exposure for the first month. Stay dry and in the shade, keep your support bra on as instructed, and save the water for after your surgeon clears you.

### Is the wait the same after a breast lift or reduction?

Ask your surgeon, because those operations can involve more incisions. MedlinePlus, which covers cosmetic breast surgery in general, gives one rule for all of them: no baths, hot tubs or swimming until stitches and drains are out and your surgeon says it is OK. It gives no number of weeks for any of them.
