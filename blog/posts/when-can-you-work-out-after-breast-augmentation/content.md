## When can you work out after breast augmentation?

In stages, starting with a walk on day one, but after that the sources don't line up. The dates below are guides, not rules, and your surgeon can move any of them. The evidence is thin: the stages come mainly from bylined articles on the ASPS website, plus a few hospital handouts, and we found only one randomized trial that tested exercising early.

| What | When | Who says so |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light walking | From the first day | [Article on the ASPS website by Dr. Catherine Huang-Begovic](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/exercise-after-breast-augmentation): helps circulation and helps prevent blood clots |
| Harder cardio, such as treadmill or uphill walking | After about 2 weeks | The same article |
| Sports and anything that raises your pulse | Not in the first month | [Sientra's patient labeling](https://sientra.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MDC-0010-R6-Sientra-Patient-Educational-Brochure-Augmentation-1.pdf) for its silicone implants |
| Most exercise, except heavy lifting | At about a month | The Huang-Begovic article only |
| No strenuous activity | For 3 to 4 weeks | [University of Utah Health](https://healthcare.utah.edu/plastic-surgery/breast/recovery) |
| No strenuous exercise or heavy lifting | For 3 to 6 weeks | [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm), on cosmetic breast surgery in general, lifts and reductions included |
| No strenuous activity or upper body movements | For at least six weeks | [Article on the ASPS website by Ariel Frankeny](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/back-to-active-returning-to-exercise-after-breast-augmentation-recovery) |
| Heavy lifting | At least 4 weeks | [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation); other sources range from 2 to 6 weeks |
| Sports, if you are active in them | Up to 6 weeks | Cleveland Clinic |
| Running | About 6 to 8 weeks, when fully recovered | Cleveland Clinic |
| Push-ups and chest work | No single date | Frankeny: at least six weeks. Huang-Begovic: all exercises at six weeks, but try to avoid strenuous chest exercise such as push-ups, especially under the muscle |

When sources differ like this, your surgeon's instructions settle it. If you haven't been given any, ask before you do anything strenuous.

## Why does exercise have to wait after breast augmentation?

Because exertion raises your blood pressure, and early on that can cause bleeding, as MedlinePlus explains. That is why it also warns against overstretching your arms during those weeks, and why Sientra's labeling lists activities that raise your pulse or blood pressure among the things to avoid for the first month.

An article on the ASPS website by [Dr. John Cook](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/will-breast-implants-get-in-the-way-of-exercise) adds that going back to the gym too soon could make swelling worse, break stitches open and make scars look worse. Our page on [what recovery involves](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#recovery) has the full picture.

## Exercise after breast augmentation, week by week

The sources give stages, not exercise lists, so where we name a specific machine or class, that's our reading of the stage. Your surgeon's word wins. If you had a breast lift or another procedure at the same time, ask for your own plan rather than assuming these dates carry over.

### Days 1 to 7: walk and rest

Walk a few minutes every few hours. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/aftercare-recovery) says this reduces the risk of blood clots in your legs, and [University of Utah Health](https://healthcare.utah.edu/plastic-surgery/breast/recovery) has you start the night of your surgery. Short walks around the room count.

### Weeks 2 and 3: harder walking

From about 2 weeks, you can start harder cardio, such as treadmill or uphill walking, according to the Huang-Begovic article. The Frankeny article says patients can begin walking at a faster pace and add light exercise at that point. University of Utah Health still says to avoid strenuous activity and repetitive arm motions for three to four weeks, so keep your arms out of it. It also has you begin stretching and range-of-motion exercises for your upper body at three weeks.

### Weeks 4 and 5: where the sources split

At about a month, most patients can resume most exercise except heavy lifting, the Huang-Begovic article says. That is the earliest view. Sientra's labeling rules out sports that raise your pulse for the first month, MedlinePlus says no strenuous exercise for 3 to 6 weeks, and the Frankeny article says strenuous activity waits at least six weeks. So ask before you step up. If your surgeon agrees, go lighter than you did before surgery and change one thing at a time, such as distance, speed or weight.

### Week 6 and after: building back up, with your surgeon's OK

At six weeks, patients are generally completely healed and can resume all exercises, the Huang-Begovic article says. Dr. Cook's article agrees that in most cases patients can resume all forms of exercise within six weeks, while the Frankeny article treats six weeks as the minimum for strenuous activity. Get your surgeon's clearance first, and build back up to your old weights over several sessions. Full recovery takes about 6 to 8 weeks on average, according to Cleveland Clinic, so feeling a bit behind at week 6 is normal.

## What exercise can I do at 4 or 5 weeks?

Mostly lower-body and cardio work, if your surgeon agrees. Few sources name specific exercises, so this table is our reading of their stages and nothing more. Only one article says most exercise can return at about a month, and the others are more cautious about anything strenuous. Take it to your follow-up. For the rest of that week, see [what to expect at 5 weeks post-op](/weeks-post-op-breast-augmentation-what-to-expect).

| Exercise | Around week 4 or 5 | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Treadmill and uphill walking | Often allowed | Named at about 2 weeks in one article |
| Stationary bike, easy | Often allowed | Low impact, arms relaxed |
| Bodyweight squats and lunges | Ask first, start light | "Most exercise" in one article; others hold strenuous work longer |
| Leg press and light leg machines | Ask first | Bracing can be more work than it looks |
| Running | Wait | About 6 to 8 weeks, see below |
| Planks | Wait | Loads your chest and shoulders |
| Push-ups, chest press, flyes | Wait | At least six weeks in one article, and no single date |
| Heavy barbell lifts | Ask first | Heavy lifting is at least 4 weeks, and limits vary |
| Swimming | Only once incisions are closed | See [our swimming guide](/how-long-after-breast-augmentation-can-i-swim) |

## When can I run after breast augmentation?

About 6 to 8 weeks, with a good supportive bra and your surgeon's clearance. Cleveland Clinic says to avoid intense or jarring activities such as running until you've fully recovered, and it puts full recovery at about 6 to 8 weeks on average. The Frankeny article says more strenuous exercise can start once the four-to-six-week mark has passed, so your surgeon may clear you a little sooner. Start with short stretches of running mixed with walking, and stop if your breasts ache or swell afterward.

## When can I lift weights after breast augmentation?

Heavy lifting waits at least 4 weeks, according to Cleveland Clinic, but the sources disagree on both the date and the weight limit. Your surgeon sets yours.

| Source | Limit |
| --- | --- |
| [UW Medicine patient handout](https://healthonline.washington.edu/document/health_online/pdf/Breast-Augmentation.pdf) | Nothing heavy for 2 weeks |
| University of Utah Health | Nothing over 10 pounds for 3 to 4 weeks |
| MedlinePlus | No heavy lifting for 3 to 6 weeks |
| Cleveland Clinic | No heavy lifting for at least 4 weeks |
| [Article on the ASPS website by Dr. Paul Vitenas](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/four-tips-for-a-healthy-recovery-after-a-breast-augmentation) | No heavy lifting for six weeks, including children under 20 pounds |

If you have a toddler at home, ask specifically about picking up your child, since that's the lift that's hardest to avoid. Light upper-body work comes after leg work, and Dr. Cook's article says to avoid chest and shoulder exercises while you're healing.

## When can I do planks, push-ups and chest press?

No source gives a single date, so your surgeon decides. The Frankeny article says to avoid strenuous activities and upper body movements for at least six weeks. The Huang-Begovic article says patients can resume all exercises at six weeks, and also advises trying to avoid strenuous chest exercise such as push-ups, especially with implants under the muscle. No source we read names planks, but a plank loads your chest and shoulders, which Dr. Cook's article says to avoid while healing. If you've had implants [placed under the muscle](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#placement), expect your surgeon to be more careful here. Our [push-up guide](/when-can-i-do-push-ups-after-breast-augmentation) covers that one exercise and implant displacement in detail.

## Which exercises should you avoid with breast implants long term?

None of the sources we read bans any exercise for life. Dr. Cook's article says there will be no need to give up running, lifting weights or other forms of exercise, and that implants should not prevent an active lifestyle. Sientra's patient labeling says that after healing you should be able to carry on normal activities including sports, while avoiding situations that put a lot of pressure on your breasts or may cause trauma.

The one real debate is heavy chest work with implants under the muscle. Dr. Huang-Begovic recommends trying to avoid strenuous chest exercise such as push-ups, because contracting the muscle can press the implant down and out over time, which is the worry behind [bottoming out](/blog/breast-implant-rippling-bottoming-out). That is one surgeon's view, not a study. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/detailed-procedure-information) also notes that an implant under the muscle may move when you flex your chest. If you lift seriously or compete, raise this at your consultation, because it can affect [whether implants go over or under the muscle](/blog/breast-implants-over-or-under-the-muscle).

## Is it safe to exercise early after breast augmentation?

One trial suggests that starting early did no harm, but it isn't a reason to skip your surgeon's clearance. In [a randomized trial of 225 women (Basile and Oliveira, 2022)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34936606/), one group started exercising 1 week after surgery and the other followed standard restrictions. Complications were 6.9% with early exercise and 7.5% with standard restrictions, and scar quality was comparable at a year.

It's a single trial from one institution, and the abstract doesn't say how long the comparison group waited. Worth knowing, and worth asking your surgeon about if you're keen to get moving, but it doesn't replace their instructions for you.

## What bra should I wear to work out after breast augmentation?

A soft, supportive bra, starting only when your surgeon lets you out of the surgical one. University of Utah Health keeps you in a surgical bra for three to four weeks, then moves you to a soft, supportive sports bra. Dr. Huang-Begovic and Dr. Cook both say to wear a supportive bra whenever you exercise, and the UW Medicine handout says no underwire until your incisions are fully healed. Look for wide straps and a firm band, in a style you can get on without raising your arms high. See [when you can sleep without a bra](/when-can-i-sleep-without-a-bra-after-breast-augmentation) for the rest of the bra timeline.

## When should you stop exercising and call your surgeon?

Stop when something hurts, swells or pulls, and call if it doesn't settle. The UW Medicine handout says if doing something hurts or pulls on your incisions, stop what you are doing, and Dr. Huang-Begovic tells patients to stop anything that causes swelling or discomfort and give it more time. Tiredness and sore legs after your first workouts are normal. New symptoms in your breasts are not.

| Ease off and rest | Stop and call your surgeon |
| --- | --- |
| General tiredness | Sudden swelling, especially on one side |
| Sore legs or back muscles | A breast that gets firmer, with pain or pressure |
| Mild ache that settles with rest | An incision that opens, or redness, heat or drainage |
| A little more swelling that eases by morning | Fever, or pain your medicine doesn't help |

Go to the emergency room for chest pain, trouble breathing or coughing up blood, and call right away for pain, swelling or warmth in one leg. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/16911-deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt) and [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/deepveinthrombosis.html) describe these as possible signs of a blood clot.

## Walking in the Florida heat, and flying home

In South Florida your early walks can mean heat and sun. Walk early or indoors, and keep new scars covered: MedlinePlus says to protect them with SPF 30 or higher whenever you are out in the sun.

If you came from another state, those walks matter for the trip home too. MedlinePlus says sitting still for a long time can make you more likely to get a blood clot, and Cleveland Clinic's general advice is to stand up and walk at least every half hour on a long flight. Dr. Karlinsky tells you, in writing, how many nights to stay in Miami before you fly home.

If you're still deciding, tell your surgeon about the sport or training you do, because it can shape your implant and placement choices. You can [book a consultation](/free-consultation) at Alluring Plastic Surgery. If your surgery was elsewhere, your own surgeon's instructions come first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I walk on the treadmill after breast augmentation?

From about 2 weeks, according to an article on the ASPS website by Dr. Catherine Huang-Begovic, which names walking on a treadmill or walking uphill as the harder cardio that can start then. Begin flat and easy, keep your arms relaxed, and get your surgeon's approval first. Before then, short walks around your home are enough to keep your circulation moving.

### Can I do squats 5 weeks after breast augmentation?

Possibly, with your surgeon's OK. One article on the ASPS website says most patients can resume most exercise at about a month, and bodyweight squats would fall inside that. MedlinePlus says no strenuous exercise for 3 to 6 weeks, and another article says at least six weeks. A loaded barbell is another matter, since limits on heavy lifting vary. Start light.

### What does the American Society of Plastic Surgeons say about exercise after breast augmentation?

The society's [own recovery page](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/recovery) gives no schedule. It says to resume exercise and normal activity according to your surgeon's directions. The week-by-week figures people often credit to ASPS come from bylined articles on its website, not the society itself, and they differ. One allows most exercise at about a month and all exercise at six weeks; another holds strenuous activity for at least six weeks.

### How soon can I do arm or ab exercises after breast augmentation?

University of Utah Health starts upper body stretching and range-of-motion exercises at three weeks. Beyond stretching, one article on the ASPS website says upper body movements should wait at least six weeks, while another allows most exercise at about a month. No source sets a date for ab work specifically, so ask your surgeon which moves to hold off on.

### Will chest workouts move my implants?

They can, especially with implants under the muscle. The Aesthetic Society says an implant under the muscle may move when you flex your chest, and one surgeon writing on the ASPS website says repeated strenuous contraction can press it down and out over time. That's her experience, not a study. Ask your surgeon how much chest work your placement allows.
