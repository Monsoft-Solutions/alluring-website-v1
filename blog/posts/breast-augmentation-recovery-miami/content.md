## How long is breast augmentation recovery?

Three numbers answer this, and they measure different things: typically 1 to 2 weeks to recover ([The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/aftercare-recovery)), about 6 to 8 weeks on average for full recovery ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation)), and as long as 3 to 6 months for implants to drop and fluff ([Dr. Eric Mariotti, in an article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-does-drop-and-fluff-mean-after-breast-augmentation)).

Those are different finish lines, which is why answers online seem to disagree. Treat them as guides, because your surgeon sets your own dates. Our breast augmentation page has a shorter summary of [what breast augmentation recovery involves](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#recovery).

| Finish line | Usual time | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Back at a desk job | A few days to a week | The Aesthetic Society |
| Most usual daily activities | 1 to 2 weeks, with no complications | [Sientra's patient labeling](https://sientra.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MDC-0010-R6-Sientra-Patient-Educational-Brochure-Augmentation-1.pdf) |
| Back at a physical job | At least 3 weeks | Cleveland Clinic |
| Full recovery | About 6 to 8 weeks | Cleveland Clinic |
| Final results | Start to show around 3 months; the process takes 3 to 6 months | [University of Utah Health](https://healthcare.utah.edu/plastic-surgery/breast/recovery); Dr. Mariotti's article |
| Scars faded | Several months, even a couple of years | [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/results) |

## Breast augmentation recovery timeline, week by week

Here is the whole recovery in one table. The marks come from university hospital handouts, society pages and surgeon-written articles on the ASPS website. None is a rule, and the sections below say where sources disagree.

| --- | --- | --- |

| Days 1 to 5 | Most pain in the first 48 hours; acute pain eases after 1 to 5 days | Walk every few hours; shower from about 2 days, if your surgeon agrees |
| Week 1 | Swelling, bruising, implants sitting high | Desk work; driving at about 1 week, some sources say at least 2, once you're off opioids and your surgeon clears you |
| Week 2 | Most swelling gone by about now | Harder cardio from about 2 weeks; sex after at least 2 weeks |
| Weeks 3 to 4 | Most remaining swelling gone within a month | Side sleeping after 3 to 4 weeks; support bra day and night for 3 to 4 weeks, or 2 to 4 by one source; most exercise at a month, except heavy lifting |

## What to expect after breast augmentation in the first 48 hours

The first 48 hours are usually the hardest, and each day after should get easier. The Aesthetic Society says you'll typically have the most pain in that window, and [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/recovery) says acute pain typically subsides after 1 to 5 days, with soreness and swelling lasting a few weeks. [Another article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/back-to-active-returning-to-exercise-after-breast-augmentation-recovery) says the first week is the most uncomfortable, especially with an implant under the muscle, so ask how [placement under or over the muscle](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#placement) would change your first days.

Your chest may feel tight, and raising your arms may be hard. Three things matter now:

- **Sleep propped up.** On your back at a 25- to 45-degree angle, using wedge pillows or a recliner, The Aesthetic Society says.
- **Walk.** A few minutes every few hours, to lower the risk of blood clots in your legs.
- **Take it easy.** ASPS says you can bleed into the pockets around the implants for the first several days, so hold back until you have permission to do more.

Take only the pain medicine your surgeon prescribes or approves, and ask before taking aspirin or ibuprofen, as [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm) advises on a page covering cosmetic breast surgery in general. Our guide to [breast augmentation pain](/blog/breast-augmentation-pain-management) covers the timeline, ibuprofen and nerve pain.

Text reads: Sleeping in the First Days. On your back, propped up, 25 to 45 degrees. Wedge pillows or a recliner both work. Source: The Aesthetic Society.](IMAGE:sleep-position)

## Breast augmentation recovery day by day: the rest of week 1

By the end of the first week, many women are back at a desk and close to driving.

- **Showers.** A [UW Medicine patient handout](https://healthonline.washington.edu/document/health_online/pdf/Breast-Augmentation.pdf) and University of Utah Health allow them at 2 days. The Aesthetic Society's range is 1 to 7 days, so go by what your surgeon says.
- **Stitches, glue and tape.** External sutures come out in about a week, and glue or tape falls off in a week or two, The Aesthetic Society says. Buried stitches are slower: see [when breast augmentation stitches dissolve](/when-do-breast-augmentation-stitches-dissolve).
- **Driving.** About 1 week, some sources say at least 2, and never on surgery day or while taking opioids. An article on the ASPS website says many women can drive after 7 to 10 days, and University of Utah Health says most people are ready within one week. MedlinePlus says at least 2 weeks, on a page that also covers lifts and reductions. Wait until your surgeon clears you. More on [when you can drive after breast augmentation](/how-long-after-breast-augmentation-can-i-drive).
- **Work.** Cleveland Clinic says at least 3 days off, and the UW Medicine handout says most people take 1 or 2 weeks.
- **Your support bra.** Around the clock, as your surgeon instructs, ASPS says.

## Weeks 2 to 4: when do swelling and soreness ease?

Most swelling goes down within about 2 weeks, an article on the ASPS website says, and The Aesthetic Society says most of what's left is gone within a month. Looking uneven for now is common, because each breast may heal at a different rate, University of Utah Health says. Our [swelling and asymmetry FAQ](/blog/swelling-and-asymmetry-after-breast-augmentation-faq) goes through what's normal.

The sleep and bra rules start to lift here:

- **Side sleeping.** A UW Medicine handout keeps you on your back for the first 3 to 4 weeks, and University of Utah Health says to wait 3 to 4 weeks before side sleeping. Your surgeon may ask for longer. Sources don't agree on a date for stomach sleeping, so wait for your surgeon's OK. See [when you can sleep on your side](/when-can-i-sleep-on-my-side-after-breast-augmentation).
- **Going without a bra.** Two university handouts keep a support bra on 24 hours a day for 3 to 4 weeks, and MedlinePlus, on a page that also covers lifts and reductions, says night and day for 2 to 4 weeks. Your surgeon sets yours. Beyond that, no source we trust gives a date for going braless at night, so wait until your surgeon clears you. Underwire waits until your incisions are fully healed. See [when you can sleep without a bra](/when-can-i-sleep-without-a-bra-after-breast-augmentation).
- **Arms and lifting.** University of Utah Health says to avoid repetitive arm motions for 3 to 4 weeks and to begin upper-body stretching at 3 weeks. Cleveland Clinic says no heavy lifting for at least 4 weeks.

## Weeks 4 to 8: when can you exercise and swim again?

Most exercise returns at around a month, soaking waits at least about a month, and no source gives one date for push-ups. In an [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/exercise-after-breast-augmentation), Dr. Catherine Huang-Begovic has patients walk right after surgery, add harder cardio after two weeks and resume most exercise except heavy lifting at a month. She says patients can resume all exercises at six weeks, and advises avoiding strenuous chest exercise such as push-ups, especially with implants under the muscle. [Another article on that site](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/back-to-active-returning-to-exercise-after-breast-augmentation-recovery) says to avoid strenuous and upper-body movement for at least six weeks. Your surgeon decides. Our [week-by-week exercise plan](/blog/when-can-you-work-out-after-breast-augmentation) and [push-ups guide](/when-can-i-do-push-ups-after-breast-augmentation) have the detail.

Keep out of baths, pools, hot tubs and the ocean until your incisions are fully closed and your surgeon agrees. That's at least about a month: in an [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/considering-breast-augmentation-for-beach-season-heres-when-you-should-plan-your-surgery), Dr. Cindy Wu says most surgeons will want you to wait that long before immersing in a pool or ocean. Here's more on [how long before you can swim](/how-long-after-breast-augmentation-can-i-swim). If you're around the five-week mark now, read [what to expect at 5 weeks post-op](/weeks-post-op-breast-augmentation-what-to-expect).

Cleveland Clinic says to avoid running and other jarring activity until you've fully recovered. That's stricter than some of the articles, so ask before you run.

## Months 3 to 12: when do implants drop and soften?

Implants sit high at first and settle slowly. University of Utah Health says you begin to notice your final results around three months, with subtle changes through 12 months, and Dr. Mariotti's article says the whole process takes 3 to 6 months. Our [drop and fluff timeline](/blog/breast-implants-drop-fluff-timeline) goes week by week, and our post on [where the scars sit and how they fade](/blog/breast-augmentation-scars-miami-timeline) covers your incisions.

Massage isn't a shortcut. A [2017 literature review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29348783/) concluded that the data don't support breast massage to prevent [capsular contracture](/blog/capsular-contracture), so do [implant massage](/how-often-to-massage-breast-after-augmentation) only if your surgeon prescribes it.

Recovery ends, but implants still need looking after. [The FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/things-consider-getting-breast-implants) they aren't lifetime devices, and for silicone implants with no symptoms [it recommends](https://www.fda.gov/media/131885/download) a first ultrasound or MRI at 5 to 6 years, then every 2 to 3 years. See [how long breast implants last](/blog/how-long-do-breast-implants-last) and [breast implant safety](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#safety), and see your surgeon promptly about any new change in a breast, even years later. Our post on [breast implant illness](/blog/breast-implant-illness-symptoms) covers late symptoms.

## Breast augmentation recovery tips with a source behind them

These are small things, and each comes from the sources above:

- **Set up before surgery.** Cleveland Clinic suggests a home recovery area with your medicine, clean gauze and loose button-down shirts. University of Utah Health adds zip-up hoodies.
- **Line up help.** Have someone stay the first night at least, Cleveland Clinic says, and expect to need help with everyday activities for a few days, MedlinePlus says.
- **Walk early.** University of Utah Health says to start the night of surgery, to help with swelling, blood clots and constipation.
- **Eat well and drink fluids.** MedlinePlus says this promotes bowel movements and prevents infection.
- **Don't smoke, and skip alcohol on pain medicine.** MedlinePlus says smoking slows healing.
- **Ask before using ice or heat.** The UW Medicine handout allows wrapped ice packs for the first 2 to 3 days. MedlinePlus says no ice or heat unless your surgeon says it's OK.
- **Don't stretch your incisions.** MedlinePlus says to limit activity so you don't, and Sientra's labeling says to avoid jerky movements for the first month.

## Normal, or call your surgeon?

Most of what you feel in the first weeks is normal. This table separates the two, and if you're unsure, call.

| What you notice | Usually normal | Call your surgeon right away |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pain | Worst in the first 48 hours, easing each day | Severe pain your medicine doesn't help, or pain that keeps getting worse |
| Swelling | On both sides, mostly gone within a month | Sudden swelling on one side, or a breast growing firmer with pain and pressure |
| Shape | Uneven, high or tight at first | A new change in shape, size or feel, even years later |
| Skin and nerves | Tightness, itching, tingling or shooting pains for the first few months | Spreading redness or heat |
| Incisions | Thin pink or red lines | Pus, bad-smelling drainage or an incision that opens |
| Temperature | No fever | Fever or chills. Use the number your surgeon gave you: the UW Medicine handout says higher than 100.5°F, MedlinePlus 100°F or higher |

Sudden one-sided swelling matters because [the FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/risks-and-complications-breast-implants) a collection of blood or fluid near the implant can cause swelling, bruising and pain.

Go to the emergency room for chest pain, trouble breathing or coughing up blood, which [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/16911-deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt) lists as symptoms of a clot that has reached the lungs. Call right away about leg swelling, pain or warmth, which [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/deepveinthrombosis.html) lists as common signs of a clot in the leg.

## Recovering in Florida, or flying home after surgery

In Florida, plan around sun and water. If you came from another state, plan for the trip home too.

- **Sun.** Sientra's labeling says to avoid sun exposure for the first month. After that, MedlinePlus says to protect scars with SPF 30 or higher whenever you're out in the sun. South Florida sun runs all year, so make it a daily habit. As practical advice, use sunscreen only on fully closed incisions.
- **Water.** Pool, ocean, bay and hot tub all follow the rule above.
- **Heat.** We found no study on breast augmentation incisions, and studies after other breast surgery disagree: [one of 460 implant-based reconstructions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34928243/) linked the hot season to more infection, and [one of 808 breast reductions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39407998/) found no significant rise in warmer months. So there's no heat rule to give you, beyond keeping incisions clean and dry as MedlinePlus advises.
- **Flying home.** No guideline we found sets a number of days, and surgeons differ. Dr. Victoria Karlinsky, MD, FACS, tells you, in writing, how many nights to stay in Miami before you fly home. MedlinePlus says sitting still for a long time makes a clot in the leg more likely, and Cleveland Clinic's general advice, not written for surgery patients, is to stand and walk at least every half hour on a long flight. Tell your surgeon about any history of clots. Readers in other states can start with a [fly-in consultation](/fly-in-consultation).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long after breast augmentation can I lift my arms?

No source gives one date. The Aesthetic Society says raising your arms may be hard at first. University of Utah Health says to avoid repetitive arm motions for 3 to 4 weeks and to start upper-body stretching at 3 weeks. MedlinePlus says not to overstretch your arms for 3 to 6 weeks, on a page covering cosmetic breast surgery in general. Your surgeon sets your limit.

### What helps reduce swelling after breast augmentation?

Your support bra, sleeping propped up and short walks are the steps with a source behind them. ASPS says a support bra minimizes swelling, and University of Utah Health says extra pillows and walking can help. Sources disagree on ice, so ask your surgeon first. Swelling that appears suddenly on one side is a reason to call.

### How long is recovery for a breast lift with augmentation?

It may take longer than implants alone. Sientra's labeling says adding a lift might cause more scarring and may lengthen your recovery time. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23298-breast-lift), writing about a lift, says women can usually wear a regular bra about 8 weeks after surgery. Ask your surgeon for dates for your own surgery, because a combined operation won't follow this timeline exactly.

### Can I pick up my kids after breast augmentation?

Not for the first weeks, and your surgeon sets the limit. Cleveland Clinic says no heavy lifting for at least 4 weeks. University of Utah Health's limit is 10 pounds for 3 to 4 weeks. In an [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/four-tips-for-a-healthy-recovery-after-a-breast-augmentation), Dr. Paul Vitenas says no heavy lifting for 6 weeks, and he counts picking up children. Arrange help before surgery.

### Can I combine breast augmentation with other procedures?

Often, yes, but more surgery at once can mean a longer recovery. A [mommy makeover](/procedures/mommy-makeover-miami) combines breast and body procedures, and [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation) says a lift can often be done at the same time as an augmentation. Sientra's labeling cautions that doing multiple procedures in one surgery isn't always the right call. Your surgeon decides what's sensible for you.
