## How long after a BBL can I sit?

Give it at least two weeks before any sitting on your buttocks, and expect to need a pillow until about week eight. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23308-brazilian-butt-lift) sets both limits. In between, sit only in short stretches, only on a pillow, and only after your surgeon says you can. Surgeons differ on the details, so your own surgeon's instructions come first.

| When | What the sources say | Who says it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 2 weeks | No sitting or lying on your buttocks | Cleveland Clinic |
| First 6 weeks | Keep any sitting to about 10 minutes at a time | A plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS |
| After 10 to 14 days | Work, driving or the movies, if you sit on a pillow | The Aesthetic Society |
| About 8 weeks | Most people still need the pillow | Cleveland Clinic |
| At least 8 weeks | No direct pressure on your buttocks | The Aesthetic Society |

![Timeline card titled When can you sit after a BBL? First 2 weeks: no sitting or lying on your buttocks. First 6 weeks: keep any sitting to about 10 minutes at a time. About 8 weeks: most people still need the pillow. Your surgeon clears you. Sources: Cleveland Clinic, ASPS, The Aesthetic Society.](IMAGE:timeline)

These sources don't line up exactly, and it helps to see where they differ:

- **The strict end.** An [ASPS article by Kamran Azad, MD](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/recovering-from-a-brazilian-butt-lift) says not to sit directly on your buttocks for at least eight weeks.
- **The first two weeks.** [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/butt-lift/aftercare-recovery) says many surgeons do not allow sitting in the first 10 to 14 days, except for using the toilet and eating.
- **The shorter end.** ASPS's [buttock enhancement recovery page](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/buttock-enhancement/recovery) asks you to avoid prolonged sitting for about two weeks, and adds that some surgeons prefer longer.

No source we read tested one schedule against another. Until your surgeon clears you, follow the stricter line.

## Why can't you sit after a BBL?

Because pressure can kill grafted fat while it is still settling in. [A plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/six-things-you-need-to-know-about-recovering-from-a-brazilian-butt-lift) calls the fat "very susceptible to pressure necrosis, or pressure that kills off the fat cells" during the time it is establishing its own blood supply. ASPS's recovery page says plainly that prolonged sitting "will damage the fat."

The result you are protecting is shape as well as size. The same ASPS article says patients who don't keep to the sitting limits are "at risk for asymmetrical or quickly diminishing results."

Fat that dies this way is called fat necrosis. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/24187-fat-necrosis) says it may feel like a fatty lump or a hard nodule. Lying on your back counts as pressure too, so sleep on your stomach or side. Here is [how to set up your bed after a BBL](/how-to-sleep-after-bbl).

## How do you sit on a BBL pillow?

Put the pillow under the backs of your thighs, so your thighs carry your weight and your buttocks stay clear of the seat. The Aesthetic Society describes a soft pillow beneath your hamstring muscles "but not touching your butt." ASPS's recovery page describes the same placement: behind the legs, to help raise the buttocks and avoid pressure on the fat grafts.

![Diagram of a stylized, fully clothed woman seen from the side, seated with a cushion under the backs of her thighs. Labels: under the backs of your thighs; buttocks clear of the seat; about 10 minutes at a time for the first 6 weeks. Sources: The Aesthetic Society, ASPS.](IMAGE:pillow)

The placement comes from the societies. The steps around it are common sense:

1. **Set the pillow down first.** Place it on the chair, then lower yourself onto it slowly, using your arms.
2. **Check where your weight lands.** Under the backs of your thighs, with your buttocks clear of the seat.
3. **Set a timer.** About 10 minutes at a time for the first 6 weeks is the limit the surgeon interviewed by ASPS gives. Then stand up and walk.
4. **Take it with you.** An ASPS article describes a discreet pillow small enough to carry to a restaurant.

Pillows come in different shapes. ASPS's 2022 article describes a triangle-shaped pillow that helps "offset the pressure onto their thighs," and the Azad article mentions a donut or inflatable pillow. Whichever you have, the test is the same: your weight on your thighs, not on the grafted fat. If you can't tell, ask your surgeon to check how you're sitting.

## Can you drive after a BBL?

Yes, after about 10 to 14 days. The Aesthetic Society says you can drive at that point as long as you use a pillow, so there is no pressure on your buttocks.

Before then, you are a passenger. For the ride home and your first follow-up visit, The Aesthetic Society says to lie on your stomach in the back seat. Ask your surgeon how to do that safely, including the seat belt.

Once you are driving, keep trips short and don't drive while your pain medication makes you drowsy. On longer drives, build in stops. In [ASPS's article on traveling after a BBL](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/boarding-groups-added-complications-from-traveling-after-a-brazilian-butt-lift), a surgeon advises walking for 5 to 10 minutes for every hour you sit, which also helps lower your risk of blood clots. If you are heading home to another state, read [how many nights to stay before you fly](/blog/flying-after-bbl-tips).

## Can you work at a desk after a BBL?

Yes, with some changes. Cleveland Clinic says you should be able to go back to work between 10 and 14 days, and if you usually sit at a desk for long periods, it advises a standing desk until you have completely healed. Some patients go back sooner: an ASPS article says patients return to work between days 7 and 10, and that those who sit at a desk all day need the BBL pillow or other cushioning.

What usually helps, though no study has tested it:

- **Stand when you can.** Raise your laptop on a counter or a stack of books, and take calls on your feet.
- **Sit in short stretches.** Use the pillow, set a timer, and get up when it goes off.
- **Tell your employer early.** Ask for a standing setup before your surgery date, not after.
- **Check the lifting.** Cleveland Clinic says to avoid heavy lifting or strenuous activity for one month, so a job with lifting needs a later return date.

## Can you sit on the toilet after a BBL?

Yes. It is the one exception the sources make. The Aesthetic Society says that even surgeons who don't allow sitting early on make room for the toilet and for eating. The surgeon interviewed by ASPS says "just go to the bathroom, it's fine," but asks you to stay within the same 10-minute window.

Constipation makes that window harder to keep. Fiber and fluids help, and our guide on [what to eat after a BBL](/how-to-feed-the-fat-after-bbl) lists what to reach for. Wiping can also be harder while your movement is limited. In ASPS's [article on BBL smell](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/is-bbl-smell-real-heres-what-you-need-to-know), Dr. Michael Keyes recommends a bidet if you have difficulty wiping, because poor hygiene is one cause of [odor during recovery](/why-do-bbl-stink).

## What if you sat on your BBL by accident?

Stand up and don't panic. The warnings in the sources are about pressure that lasts: the surgeon interviewed by ASPS warns against "undue or prolonged pressure," and ASPS's recovery page talks about prolonged sitting. No source we read says one short sit ruins a result, and none says exactly how much is too much.

So note how long it was and tell your surgeon at your next check-in, or sooner if you were on it for a long time. Don't try to treat it yourself. Then go back to your plan.

## When should you call your surgeon?

Call for a lump that hurts or grows, pain that is extreme or long-lasting, or any sign of infection. Trouble breathing, chest pain or an unusual heartbeat is an emergency: [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/buttock-enhancement/recovery) says to seek medical attention immediately.

| What you notice | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Bruising and swelling that slowly ease | Usually normal, according to The Aesthetic Society |
| Pain that decreases after 1 to 2 weeks | Usually normal, according to Cleveland Clinic |
| A lump that hurts or seems to grow | Call your surgeon |
| Pain that is extreme or long-lasting | Call your surgeon |
| Fever above 101°F, spreading redness, or discharge or odor from an incision | Call your surgeon the same day |
| Leg pain, swelling and warmth | Seek medical care right away |
| Trouble breathing, chest pain or an unusual heartbeat | Seek medical attention immediately. Call 911 |

The infection signs come from [Cleveland Clinic's page on surgical wound infection](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/surgical-wound-infection), and the leg and breathing signs from its [page on pulmonary embolism](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/17400-pulmonary-embolism), which says to call 911 for those symptoms.

## Frequently asked questions

### When can I sit without a BBL pillow?

Cleveland Clinic says you won't be able to sit without your pillow for approximately 8 weeks, and The Aesthetic Society says to avoid direct pressure on your buttocks for at least 8 weeks. Treat week 8 as the earliest time to ask, not an automatic end date. Your surgeon decides at your follow-up visit.

### Can I sit on the toilet after a BBL?

Yes. The Aesthetic Society notes that many surgeons who don't allow sitting in the first 10 to 14 days still make an exception for the toilet and for eating. A plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS says going to the bathroom is fine, as long as you keep it to about 10 minutes at a time.

### When can I drive after a BBL?

The Aesthetic Society says you can drive after 10 to 14 days if you use a pillow, so there is no pressure on your buttocks. Stop and walk on longer drives, and don't drive while your pain medication makes you drowsy. Ask your surgeon before your first trip at the wheel.

### Is the sitting rule after a BBL a myth?

No. The reason is real: a plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS says grafted fat is very susceptible to pressure necrosis, and ASPS's recovery page says prolonged sitting will damage the fat. What isn't settled is the exact number of weeks. Sources range from about 2 weeks to at least 8, and none we read compared them.

### I sat on my BBL by accident. Did I ruin it?

No source we read says one short sit ruins a result. The warnings are about prolonged pressure, and none says exactly how much is too much. Stand up, note how long it was and tell your surgeon. Call sooner if you notice a lump that hurts or grows, a fever or spreading redness.

### How do I maintain my BBL results long term?

Keep your weight steady. Cleveland Clinic says to maintain a consistent weight to preserve the effects of a BBL, and ASPS says the transferred fat can grow or shrink as you gain or lose weight, like other fat in your body. Cleveland Clinic calls the results semi-permanent. Your surgeon can tell you which habits matter most for you.
