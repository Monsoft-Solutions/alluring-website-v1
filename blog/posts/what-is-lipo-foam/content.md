## What is lipo foam?

Lipo foam, also written lipofoam, is a soft, flexible sheet of foam you wear directly on your skin, under your compression garment, after liposuction. It spreads the garment's pressure evenly over the treated areas and cushions seams, hooks and creases that could otherwise press into swollen skin. It comes as flat sheets or pre-cut pads, and your surgeon tells you where to place it and for how long.

Lipo foam is not a treatment in itself. It works only together with your garment, and the garment is the part the societies name: [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/liposuction/aftercare-recovery) says you wear a compression garment over the treated areas for 4 to 6 weeks.

## What does lipo foam do?

Lipo foam turns the uneven squeeze of a garment into steady, even pressure. A garment presses hardest along its seams, zippers, hook-and-eye closures and wherever the fabric folds, and in the first days after surgery that can leave dents in swollen, numb skin. A layer of foam softens those pressure points.

What surgeons expect it to help with:

- **Smoother skin.** Even pressure while the skin settles onto the tissue underneath.
- **Less bruising and bleeding.** Firm, even compression over areas that were treated.
- **Comfort.** A soft layer between tender skin and a stiff garment.
- **Fewer garment marks.** Foam fills hollows and edges where the garment would otherwise dig in.

The research behind those expectations is old and small. A [1993 report in Aesthetic Plastic Surgery](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8430530/) described foam under a pressure garment after liposuction as giving "less waviness in the skin surface, a more comfortable recovery, and less blood loss and ecchymoses" (ecchymoses are bruises). The report describes no controlled comparison with patients who went without foam.

## Is lipo foam necessary?

Not always. Some surgeons use foam for every liposuction patient, some only for certain areas or techniques, and some don't use it at all. In an [ASPS article on liposuction recovery](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/eight-ways-to-make-liposuction-recovery-a-breeze), one surgeon describes giving supplemental foam to place under the garment to patients whose procedure included athletic sculpting.

The evidence can't settle it either way. A [2023 review in Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery Global Open](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37753333/) found that "no RCTs or comparative studies have been conducted to evaluate the efficacy of compression" after body contouring, even though surgeons use it widely. We found no trial that tested lipo foam against no foam.

So: if your surgeon sends you home with foam, wear it as instructed. If your surgeon doesn't use it, don't add it on your own without asking, because foam changes how your garment fits.

## What are lipo boards: ab board and back board?

Lipo boards are firmer, curved panels worn on top of the foam and under the garment. An ab board covers the abdomen; a back board covers the lower back and sometimes the flanks. They are thin, semi-rigid plastic or dense foam, shaped to follow the body.

Boards are meant to hold a flat, even surface over a large area and stop the garment from bunching at the waist when you sit or bend. You are most likely to be given one after Lipo 360 or a tummy tuck, where a large area of the trunk is treated. We found no published study of boards, so their use rests on surgeons' experience.

| | Lipo foam | Lipo board (ab or back) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Soft, flexible foam sheet or pad | Firm, curved panel |
| Where it goes | Directly on the skin | On top of the foam |
| What it does | Evens out and cushions pressure | Keeps a large area flat, stops bunching |

## How to wear lipo foam and boards

Follow the placement your surgeon or nurse shows you before you go home, and ask them to show your helper too. The general order is skin, foam, board, garment.

![Layering diagram of lipo foam and boards: clean, dry skin, then lipo foam, then an ab board or back board if your surgeon uses one, then the compression garment. The garment is worn 4 to 6 weeks over the treated areas, according to The Aesthetic Society; foam and boards as your surgeon directs.](IMAGE:layers)

1. **Start with clean, dry skin.** Pat the areas dry after showering. Don't put lotion, oil or powder under foam unless your surgeon says you can.
2. **Lay the foam flat.** Smooth it over the treated area with no folds, rolls or wrinkles. Cut it to fit curves if your surgeon allows, and keep cut edges from overlapping.
3. **Ask about your incisions.** Some surgeons want foam over the small incisions and some want gauze or a gap there. Do what yours says.
4. **Add the board, if you have one.** Center it over the foam so its edges rest on foam, not on bare skin.
5. **Close the garment evenly.** Fasten it from the bottom up, then run your hands over it to find creases or bunched foam.
6. **Recheck after every bathroom trip** and every time you get up from bed. Foam slides, and a folded edge left for hours can leave a deep mark.

## How long do you wear your foams and boards after lipo?

There is no published standard, so it's your surgeon's call. The Aesthetic Society's figure is for the garment: 4 to 6 weeks over the treated areas. Foam and boards are often part of that time rather than all of it, and surgeons differ on whether they stay on day and night and for how many weeks.

The published examples are few. In a [2002 report in Dermatologic Surgery](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12472493/), a girdle with foam built into it was "kept on for two to three days" after liposuction. That was one surgeon's device, not a rule, but it shows how short some foam protocols are.

Ask your surgeon three things before you go home:

- How long do I wear the foam, and how long the board?
- Day and night, or only part of the day?
- What should I use once the foam comes off?

Write the answers down. Your surgeon may change the plan at your follow-up visits as your swelling goes down.

## When can you stop wearing foams after lipo?

Stop when your surgeon tells you to, not when the foam starts to feel unnecessary. Swelling can look settled while the skin is still adjusting. The Aesthetic Society says swelling mostly goes down within 2 to 3 weeks, with slight swelling for up to 4 months, and [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11009-liposuction) says the final result can take 3 to 6 months to show.

The one exception: if the foam or board is hurting your skin, take it off and call your surgeon the same day rather than waiting for your next appointment.

## Skin care under lipo foam: blisters, pressure marks and rashes

Foam sits against your skin for long stretches, so check the skin every time you take the garment off. Foam can occasionally irritate skin: the 2002 report says Reston foam, a medical foam applied directly to the skin, "can occasionally cause allergic dermatitis, bullae formation or postinflammatory hyper-pigmentation." In plain words: a rash, blisters, or darker patches that linger after the skin heals. The 2023 review also notes that one surgeon observed, in 2006, that excessive or poorly distributed compression could cause skin folding and bulging.

| Usually normal | Call your surgeon |
| --- | --- |
| Faint lines or a grid pattern from the foam that fade once the garment is off | A deep dent or line that doesn't fade |
| Mild itching as skin heals | A spreading red rash, hives or intense itching |
| Numb patches over treated areas | Blisters, open or weeping skin |
| Some leakage from incisions in the first several days | Redness, heat, pus or fever |
| Tightness that eases when you adjust the garment | Pain that gets worse under the board or garment |

To protect your skin:

- **Keep foam dry.** Replace it when it is soaked with fluid or sweat.
- **Smooth every edge.** Most marks come from folds, not from the foam itself.
- **Let skin breathe briefly** when your surgeon allows garment breaks, and check the areas you can't see with a mirror or a helper.
- **Don't cover a blister** with more foam. Leave it for your surgeon to look at.

If itching is the main problem, our guide to [itching after lipo](/how-to-reduce-itching-after-lipo) explains what helps.

## Can you wash lipo foam?

Check the manufacturer's instructions and ask your surgeon. Many foam sheets are sold for single use, and the foam loses its spring once it has been soaked and flattened. Because it sits over healing incisions, replace any sheet that is soiled with drainage rather than rinsing and reusing it. A board can usually be wiped down; ask your surgeon what to clean it with.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does lipo foam help with swelling?

It may, as part of compression. Foam spreads the garment's pressure so the whole treated area is compressed evenly, and a 1993 report described less bruising and bleeding when foam was used under a pressure garment. No controlled study has measured swelling with and without foam. Walking, your garment and time do most of the work: The Aesthetic Society says swelling peaks around 48 hours after surgery.

### Can lipo foam tighten loose skin?

No. Foam helps skin settle smoothly while it heals, but it doesn't remove or tighten skin. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/liposuction) notes that liposuction itself removes fat without tightening skin. If loose skin is your main concern, a procedure that removes skin, such as a tummy tuck, may suit you better, and a surgeon can tell you at an exam.

### Is lipo foam the same as a faja?

No. Faja is the Spanish word many people use for the compression garment itself. Lipo foam is a separate soft layer that goes under the faja, directly on the skin, and a lipo board goes between the two. You can have a faja without foam, but foam only works with a garment over it to hold it in place.

### Do you wear lipo foam at night?

Often, yes, if your surgeon wants foam on day and night in the early weeks. Some surgeons keep foam on only while you are up and moving. Whatever the plan, check for folds before you go to sleep, because a crease you lie on for hours is the most likely to leave a mark. Our [sleep guide](/how-to-sleep-after-liposuction) covers positions and garments at night.

### Is foam used after chin liposuction?

After chin or neck liposuction, compression usually comes from a chin strap or small wrap rather than body foam, and whether a foam pad goes under it is up to your surgeon, as is how long you wear it. Our guide to [chin lipo recovery](/how-long-does-it-take-to-recover-from-chin-lipo) explains the chin strap and what to expect.

### Where can I get lipo foam?

Ask your surgeon first. Some practices provide foam and boards on surgery day, and others give you a list to buy ahead. If you buy your own, choose foam made for post-surgical use and boards sized for your body, and bring them to your pre-op visit so the team can check the fit before you need them.
