## How long does pain last after breast augmentation?

The worst of it usually lasts 1 to 2 days. After that the pain changes more than it disappears: soreness for a few weeks, and sharp or tingling feelings that can come and go for the first few months. Tell your surgeon if those don't fade. Pain that gets worse instead of better is a reason to call.

## How painful is breast augmentation?

Most people describe tightness, pressure and soreness, and it's often worse with implants under the muscle. The Aesthetic Society says the surgery stretches the breast tissue and can be painful, especially when your implants are underneath the pectoral muscle. An [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/back-to-active-returning-to-exercise-after-breast-augmentation-recovery) quotes one surgeon saying the first week is typically the most uncomfortable, with pressure on the chest as well as incisional pain, and another saying that under the muscle, the muscle has been stretched to accommodate the implant.

Placement isn't chosen for comfort alone. ## What pain medicine do you get after breast augmentation?

Usually a prescription for the first days, then something you can buy without one, and your surgeon decides which. The UW Medicine handout says you will be given a prescription and should take it exactly as prescribed, and that after the first day you may need less or none of it. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation) tells you to have your prescribed medicine and/or acetaminophen (Tylenol) ready at home.

We don't list doses on this page, on purpose. The right amount depends on your health and your other medicines, and it's on your prescription label.

Things worth knowing:

- **Surgeons often combine medicines.** In a [2019 survey of ASPS members](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31044107/), across three operations including breast augmentation, 80% of those who answered used non-narcotic add-ons to manage pain. The response rate was only 12%, so treat that as a rough picture.
- **Many prescription pills already contain acetaminophen.** That same survey found the opioids most often prescribed were combined with it. Ask before adding Tylenol on your own.
- **A numbing nerve block is one option during surgery.** In [a randomized trial of 73 women (Aarab and colleagues, 2021)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34195767/), adding a chest-wall nerve block lowered pain and opioid use over the first 5 days. Ask your surgeon before surgery whether it applies to you.
- **Opioids and driving don't mix.** Don't drive while taking them. Here is [when you can drive after breast augmentation](/how-long-after-breast-augmentation-can-i-drive).
- **Take medicine the way MedlinePlus says.** Its page, which covers cosmetic breast surgery in general, lifts and reductions included, says to take pain medicine with food and plenty of water and not to drink alcohol with it.

## Can I take ibuprofen after breast augmentation?

Only with your surgeon's approval, and the answer varies by surgeon because sources disagree. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm) says not to take aspirin, aspirin-containing medicines or ibuprofen without your surgeon's approval. On the other side, [a 2016 review of 4 randomized trials, not specific to breast augmentation (Kelley and colleagues)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27018685/), found no significant difference in bleeding, with pain control equivalent to narcotics. An [ASPS press release on that review](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/press-releases/ibuprofen-doesnt-increase-bleeding-risk-after-plastic-surgery) says the available evidence suggests ibuprofen does not increase bleeding after plastic surgery. The review's authors say further large studies are needed.

Keep before and after surgery apart. Cleveland Clinic's advice to avoid aspirin and certain anti-inflammatory drugs because they can increase bleeding is part of its instructions for getting ready for surgery, and your surgeon may set a different plan for afterward. No source we read gives a day when ibuprofen becomes fine, so ask when, as well as whether. A [2020 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7203369/) says medicines in this class should be used with caution in older patients and those prone to bleeding, so mention your health history and anything else you take.

## How do you relieve nerve pain after breast augmentation?

Often with time, and with your surgeon's help if it isn't fading. Sharp, shooting or tingling feelings are common: University of Utah Health says they can last the first few months, and the UW Medicine handout calls sharp, shooting pain normal for 2 weeks. Nipple and skin sensation can swing the other way too. [Allergan's labeling for Natrelle saline implants](https://allergan-web-cdn-prod.azureedge.net/actavis/actavis/media/allergan-pdf-documents/labeling/natrelleus/salineimplants/m711rev10_web.pdf) says changes range from intense sensitivity to no feeling, and that while some can be temporary, they can also be permanent, so tell your surgeon about them.

No source we read has tested a home remedy for these zaps, so be wary of anything that promises a quick fix. What's reasonable:

- **Tell your surgeon what you feel and where.** It helps them separate nerve pain from swelling or something else.
- **Avoid what sets it off.** If doing something hurts or pulls on your incisions, stop, as the UW Medicine handout says.
- **Keep your support bra on as instructed.** ASPS says to wear it around the clock as your surgeon instructs.
- **Ask about medicine aimed at nerve pain.** More on this below.

If pain lasts, doctors have more options. A [2020 review of chronic pain after cosmetic breast surgery](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7203369/) lists over-the-counter pain relievers, nerve-pain medicines and physical therapy first, then nerve blocks for those who need more. Lasting pain does happen. In [a small Danish survey of one surgeon's patients (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20727797/), 95 women answered at an average of 31.8 months after surgery. In all, 44.2% reported some pain from the operation, and 9.5% reported moderate to severe pain. That isn't a national rate, but it's a reason not to dismiss pain that doesn't go away.

## Is gabapentin used after breast augmentation?

Sometimes, and your surgeon decides. No source we read says how often. Gabapentin is a medicine commonly used for nerve pain. The 2020 review says gabapentin or pregabalin may provide some relief for nerve pain after cosmetic breast surgery, and also says there is conflicting evidence on how well they work. A [2009 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18712436/) gave gabapentin with celecoxib before under-the-muscle augmentation, and reported less pain and opioid use than celecoxib alone, compared with an earlier group of patients rather than in a randomized trial. If you've been prescribed it, ask how long to stay on it and how to come off it, and follow your label.

## What helps pain without medicine?

A few simple things, though not all have strong evidence behind them.

- **Cold, if your surgeon agrees.** The UW Medicine handout allows ice packs for the first 2 to 3 days, wrapped in a towel or cloth, for 20 minutes each hour. MedlinePlus says not to apply ice or heat unless your surgeon says it's OK, so ask first. Skin that feels numb can't warn you it's too cold.
- **Sleeping propped up.** The Aesthetic Society suggests your back at a 25- to 45-degree angle for the first days, on wedge pillows or in a recliner. See [when you can sleep on your side](/when-can-i-sleep-on-my-side-after-breast-augmentation) for what comes next.
- **Walking.** The Aesthetic Society says to walk a few minutes every few hours, mainly to lower your risk of blood clots.
- **Not smoking.** MedlinePlus says smoking slows healing and increases your risk of complications and infection.

In South Florida, take those early walks outside the midday heat and keep new scars covered.

## What pain is not normal after breast augmentation?

Pain that gets worse rather than better, that your medicine doesn't help, or that comes with other changes. The Aesthetic Society says to contact your surgeon if pain is extreme or long-lasting. The direction matters more than any single day.

| Usually normal | Call your surgeon |
| --- | --- |
| Tightness and soreness that ease each day | Severe pain your medicine doesn't help |
| Brief zaps or tingling in the first months | Pain that increases, with redness, swelling or drainage |
| One side sorer for a few days | One breast suddenly swelling, or firm with pain and pressure |
| Sore chest muscles when you move | Fever: use the number your surgeon gave you |
| Itching as you heal | A new change in shape, size or feel, even years later |

Fever thresholds differ: UW Medicine says higher than 100.5°F, and MedlinePlus says 100°F or higher. Go to the emergency room for chest pain, trouble breathing or coughing up blood, which [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/16911-deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt) lists among the signs of a clot that has moved to the lungs. If swelling is what worries you, see [swelling and asymmetry after breast augmentation](/blog/swelling-and-asymmetry-after-breast-augmentation-faq).

Later pain can have other causes. [What to expect week by week](/weeks-post-op-breast-augmentation-what-to-expect) covers soreness in the following weeks. The FDA describes capsular contracture, [a hardening around the implant](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/risks-and-complications-breast-implants), as something that can be painful, and our guide to [capsular contracture](/blog/capsular-contracture) explains it. The FDA also says to see your surgeon promptly about [any abnormal changes](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/things-consider-getting-breast-implants) in your breasts or implants.

If your surgery was elsewhere, your own surgeon's instructions come first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which pain medication works well after breast augmentation?

There's no single right choice, and no source we read ranks them. Opioids are often used in the first days, but a 2020 review says they should be limited because of the risk of dependence. Many surgeons add non-narcotic medicines. What suits you depends on your health and other medicines, so ask your surgeon what they've chosen and why.

### Can I take Tylenol after breast augmentation?

Often yes, but check with your surgeon first. Cleveland Clinic lists prescribed pain medicine and/or acetaminophen, the drug in Tylenol, among the things to have ready at home. Many prescription pain pills already contain acetaminophen, so adding Tylenol on top can mean taking more than you realize. Your surgeon or pharmacist can tell you what's safe together.

### How long should I take gabapentin after breast augmentation?

Only as long as your surgeon prescribes. We give no schedule here because no source we read sets one for this surgery, and the right length depends on why you were given it. Don't stop or change it on your own without asking. If you weren't told when to stop, call your surgeon's office or ask your pharmacist.

### How can I tell normal tightness from abnormal pain?

Look at the direction. Normal tightness improves a little each day and responds to your medicine. Abnormal pain gets worse over time, isn't helped by your medicine, or comes with redness, swelling, drainage or fever. A breast that suddenly swells or turns firm is different from general soreness. If you can't tell, call. Asking costs nothing, and your surgeon would rather hear from you.

### Is it normal for one breast to hurt more than the other?

For a while, yes. University of Utah Health says you may see some asymmetry because each breast may heal at a different rate, and the same can be true of soreness. It's not normal if one side suddenly swells, becomes firm, or the pain keeps rising. MedlinePlus says to contact your surgeon if you notice sudden swelling of your breast.

### Is it normal to still have pain 3 months after breast augmentation?

Some twinges are common, but steady pain deserves a visit. University of Utah Health says shooting pains and tingling can last the first few months. A 2020 review defines chronic post-surgical pain as lasting 3 or more months. Tell your surgeon about pain at this stage. It may be nothing serious, but there are more options once they know.
