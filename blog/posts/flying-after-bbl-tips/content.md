## When can I fly after a BBL?

Plan to stay near your surgeon for 7 to 10 days if you are flying in for a BBL, and fly home only after you have been seen at follow-up and cleared. None of the society pages we read sets a single day for flying. [ASPS's article on traveling after a BBL](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/boarding-groups-added-complications-from-traveling-after-a-brazilian-butt-lift) says to stay in the area for at least 4 to 5 days, or as long as possible. A surgeon quoted there explains that an infection typically shows up 3 to 5 days after surgery. It also says most surgeons advise against travel in the days after surgery.

So 4 to 5 days is a minimum for staying close. It isn't a date to fly.

## How many nights should I stay in Miami after a BBL?

Alluring asks patients flying in from another state to plan on 7 to 10 days in Miami, so you can be seen at follow-up and cleared before you fly. That is the practice's own planning figure, not a society's guidance. Your exact number of nights comes in writing, with your surgery, pre-op and follow-up dates.

Here is what each stage means for your flight:

| After surgery | What the source says | What it means for your flight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 3 to 5 | An infection typically shows up in this window (a surgeon quoted in ASPS's article) | Stay close to your surgeon |
| At least 4 to 5 days | Stay in the area, or as long as possible (ASPS's article) | A minimum for staying close, not a date to fly |
| 7 to 10 days | Seen at follow-up and cleared (the practice's planning figure) | The window to plan your flight around, once you are cleared |
| At least 2 weeks | No sitting or lying on your buttocks ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23308-brazilian-butt-lift)) | A flight in this window needs a seating plan from your surgeon |
| First 6 weeks | About 10 minutes of sitting at a time (a plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS) | Get up as often as you are allowed |
| About 8 weeks | Most people can't sit without a BBL pillow (Cleveland Clinic), and you avoid direct pressure on your buttocks ([The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/butt-lift/aftercare-recovery)) | Take your pillow on any flight |

The [BBL recovery guide](/how-long-to-recover-from-bbl) and the [BBL page's recovery timeline](/procedures/brazilian-butt-lift-bbl-miami#recovery) cover the rest of those weeks.

## What should I book, and when?

Wait until your dates are in writing before you buy a ticket. Then:

- **Choose a ticket you can change.** Recovery can take longer than you planned, and you want to be able to move your date without pressure to fly.
- **Choose the shortest trip you can.** ASPS's article says the longer a person spends flying, the higher the risk of blood clots.
- **Ask for an aisle seat.** It makes it easier to get up and walk. This is common sense, not a study finding.
- **Bring someone if you can.** The Aesthetic Society says having help after surgery will increase your healing.
- **Keep the essentials with you.** Carry your pillow, your medication, your written instructions and your surgeon's contact details on board.

## How do I sit on a plane after a BBL?

Use a BBL pillow, get up as often as you are allowed, and ask your surgeon before you travel how to sit. A flight home at 7 to 10 days falls inside the first 2 weeks, when Cleveland Clinic says not to sit on your buttocks at all. A plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS explains why it matters: "If you go and sit on them for a couple of hours on a plane, you're at risk of killing the cells."

You also can't plan to lie down or stand for the whole flight. In ASPS's article, a flight attendant says "the seatbelt sign is a federal regulation," so passengers have to sit when it is on. She describes a captain telling a passenger who wanted to lie down for the entire flight, "No, you can't travel unless you can sit up."

No source we read gives a seating method for flights. That makes this a question for your surgeon: which pillow, how to position it, and whether your flight length changes your date. Check with your airline too, since a cushion may have to fit its seat and safety rules.

## How do I lower the risk of a blood clot on the flight?

Long sitting raises the risk of deep vein thrombosis, a clot in a deep vein, which can travel to the lungs. ASPS's article says clots can develop in the days and even weeks after travel, so watch for symptoms after you land too. These steps come from its surgeons and the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/blood-clots/risk-factors/travel.html):

- **Walk.** Get up and walk for 5 to 10 minutes for every hour you sit, a plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS advises.
- **Drink water.** The same surgeon says to remain well hydrated.
- **Ask about compression stockings.** Another surgeon in the article has his traveling patients wear them. The CDC says some people may benefit from graduated compression stockings.
- **Move your legs in your seat.** The CDC says to move your legs frequently on long trips and exercise your calf muscles.
- **Follow your garment instructions.** Ask whether to wear your [compression garment](/blog/bbl-compression-garment-timeline) on the flight.

## When should I delay my flight?

Delay it if you haven't been cleared, or if any of these appear. Changing a ticket is easier than handling a complication away from your surgeon.

| What you notice | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| You haven't been seen at follow-up or cleared | Wait, and move your flight |
| A fever above 101 degrees Fahrenheit, thick cloudy discharge, spreading redness, an incision that feels hot, or a noticeable odor from the incision | Call your surgeon before you travel |
| Swelling, pain or tenderness in one leg, or skin there that is red and warm | Seek medical care right away |
| Breathing trouble, chest pain or an unusual heartbeat, coughing up blood, lightheadedness or fainting | Seek medical attention immediately. On a plane, tell the crew |

The infection signs are from [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/surgical-wound-infection), which says symptoms typically develop 3 to 7 days after surgery. The clot signs are from the [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/blood-clots/about/index.html).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I fly 5 days after a BBL?

It isn't a date to plan around. ASPS's article says to stay in the area for at least 4 to 5 days, or as long as possible, so day 5 is the low end of staying close, not clearance to fly. The practice's plan is 7 to 10 days, so you can be seen at follow-up first. Your surgeon clears you.

### Do I need a pillow on the plane?

Take one unless your surgeon tells you otherwise. Cleveland Clinic says not to sit on your buttocks for at least 2 weeks, and that most people can't sit without a BBL pillow for about 8 weeks, so a flight home at 7 to 10 days falls inside both. Ask your surgeon how to sit, since you must stay seated when the seatbelt sign is on.

### What about a long flight?

A longer flight means longer sitting. ASPS's article says more time flying raises the risk of blood clots. The CDC says anyone traveling more than 4 hours can be at risk, and lists surgery within the past 3 months as a risk factor. Tell your surgeon your full travel time before you set your date.

### Who gives me my dates?

The practice confirms your surgery, pre-op and follow-up dates in writing and tells you how many nights to stay in Miami, so you can buy your ticket around a fixed calendar. Flights and where you stay are yours to arrange. Your surgeon clears you to fly once you have been seen at follow-up.

### Can I start with a virtual consultation?

Yes. You can start with a virtual consultation if you live in another state, and have your surgery, pre-op and follow-up dates in writing before you buy a ticket. Your surgeon still examines you in person before surgery, and your plan is confirmed at that exam. Florida's rule for office surgery sets that exam no later than the day before.
