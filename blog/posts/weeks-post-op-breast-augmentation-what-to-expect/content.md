## What should I expect 5 weeks post-op after breast augmentation?

At five weeks, swelling and discomfort are usually much better. Your implants are starting to settle into a more natural position, scars continue to fade with proper care, and most patients can gradually return to normal activities, though chest work and heavy lifting still need your surgeon's OK. You'll likely notice better mobility and less pain, but some tightness or nerve sensitivity may persist.

This phase is a turning point in the healing process, with noticeable improvements and lingering questions. Many patients feel unsure about what's normal at this stage: Is this swelling still okay? When can I resume workouts? Is this sensitivity expected?

## What Physical Changes Happen 5 Weeks After Surgery?

While every patient's recovery differs slightly, most patients see clear changes by now. Most of the swelling has gone, which lets your breasts begin settling into a more natural shape as the implants soften.

Most patients have less discomfort and better mobility, enough to resume light daily activities. Strenuous exercise and chest work still wait. It's also common for breasts to look somewhat high or firm: [an article on the ASPS website by Dr. Cindy Wu](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/considering-breast-augmentation-for-beach-season-heres-when-you-should-plan-your-surgery) says it is normal for implants to still sit higher on your chest wall after the first month.

This settling, known as the "drop and fluff" process, continues for months. Implants start out high and firm. [University of Utah Health](https://healthcare.utah.edu/plastic-surgery/breast/recovery) says you begin to notice your final results around three months, with subtle changes through the 12 months after surgery, and [an article on the ASPS website by Dr. Eric Mariotti](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-does-drop-and-fluff-mean-after-breast-augmentation) says the whole process can take three to six months.

![Illustration of implant settling at week 1, week 3, week 6 and weeks 12 and later, with the implant sitting lower and softer at each stage.](IMAGE:drop-fluff)

For the full drop and fluff timeline, week by week, see [when implants drop and fluff](/blog/breast-implants-drop-fluff-timeline). If one side looks higher than the other right now, that is often normal too: our [swelling and asymmetry FAQ](/blog/swelling-and-asymmetry-after-breast-augmentation-faq) covers it.

Scarring remains visible but should continue fading with proper care. [ASPS says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/results) incision lines may take several months, even a couple of years, to fade. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm), writing about cosmetic breast surgery in general, says to use sunblock of SPF 30 or higher on scars whenever you are out in the sun. [Our scars guide](/blog/breast-augmentation-scars-miami-timeline) goes further.

Regular follow-up appointments matter at this point, to check your progress, look at implant symmetry and talk through any lingering discomfort.

## What Pain and Sensitivity Are Normal at 5 Weeks?

By week five, most patients report a marked decrease in pain, though recovery varies by individual. [ASPS says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/recovery) acute pain typically subsides after 1 to 5 days, and soreness and swelling can last a few weeks, so by now it should be easing rather than building.

Many patients still feel some tightness around the implants as surrounding tissues adjust. Nerve sensitivity usually improves with time but can linger. University of Utah Health says you may feel shooting pains, tingling or other strange sensations in your skin for the first few months. [Allergan's labeling for Natrelle saline implants](https://allergan-web-cdn-prod.azureedge.net/actavis/actavis/media/allergan-pdf-documents/labeling/natrelleus/salineimplants/m711rev10_web.pdf) says the range of changes runs from intense sensitivity to no feeling in the nipple or breast.

Most people can now begin light movement under guidance, regaining range of motion in the chest and arms.

Pain needs differ. Some patients no longer need anything, and others still want something for comfort. Take only what your surgeon prescribes or approves. MedlinePlus says not to take aspirin, aspirin-containing medicines or ibuprofen without your surgeon's approval. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/aftercare-recovery) says to contact your surgeon if pain is extreme or long-lasting. There is more in our guide to [breast augmentation pain and nerve pain](/blog/breast-augmentation-pain-management).

## What Activities Are Safe at 5 Weeks Post-Op?

At the five-week milestone, many patients are ready to ease back into daily activities, with some caution. [An article on the ASPS website by Dr. Catherine Huang-Begovic](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/exercise-after-breast-augmentation) says that at a month most patients can resume most exercise except heavy lifting. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation) advises no heavy lifting for at least 4 weeks, and says returning to sports may take up to 6 weeks. Other sources range wider, so your surgeon sets your limit.

Light physical activities such as walking are encouraged to promote healthy circulation. Stop immediately if any activity causes discomfort.

Avoid exercises that work the chest muscles. No source gives a single date for push-ups. [An article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/back-to-active-returning-to-exercise-after-breast-augmentation-recovery) says to avoid strenuous and upper-body movement for at least six weeks. Dr. Huang-Begovic's article says patients can resume all exercises at six weeks, and advises avoiding strenuous chest exercise such as push-ups, especially with implants under the muscle. Your surgeon decides, as [our push-ups guide](/when-can-i-do-push-ups-after-breast-augmentation) explains.

![Card titled Week 5 Activity: Usually OK vs Not Yet. Usually OK with your surgeon's clearance: walking, light lower-body work and daily activities. Not yet: push-ups and chest work, ask your surgeon; heavy lifting, ask your surgeon; running and jarring activity, until fully recovered. Sources: Cleveland Clinic and articles on the ASPS website.](IMAGE:activity-card)

Continue to wear your supportive bra during activity, and stop anything that hurts or swells.

If you are searching for what changes at 6 weeks post-op, Dr. Huang-Begovic's article says that at six weeks patients are generally completely healed and can resume all exercises, and Cleveland Clinic puts full recovery at about 6 to 8 weeks on average. Before you resume strenuous workouts, check with your surgeon, and see [the full exercise plan after breast augmentation](/blog/when-can-you-work-out-after-breast-augmentation).

## How Should I Sleep at 5 Weeks After Breast Augmentation?

By five weeks you have usually moved past the strictest sleep rules. [A UW Medicine patient handout](https://healthonline.washington.edu/document/health_online/pdf/Breast-Augmentation.pdf) says to sleep on your back for the first 3 to 4 weeks, and University of Utah Health says to wait three to four weeks before sleeping on your side. Your surgeon may ask for longer, so check before you change.

### Back Sleeping Transition Challenges

Back sleeping can be a major adjustment, especially for lifelong side or stomach sleepers. If your surgeon still wants you on your back, use supportive pillows to raise your upper body and improve comfort. A body pillow can help keep you from rolling over during sleep.

Many patients find a gradual adjustment eases both the discomfort and the worry about harming their result. If sleep positioning remains difficult, ask your surgeon for suggestions.

### Supportive Pillow Arrangements

University of Utah Health says extra pillows to prop you up can help with swelling. The Aesthetic Society suggests wedge pillows with an incline design or a recliner chair for the early days or whenever swelling is significant.

![Line diagram of a clothed figure in a plain high-neck top lying on her back, titled How to Sleep After Breast Augmentation. Labels point to a wedge pillow under the upper body, a pillow under the knees, and a body pillow alongside to stop rolling. A note says to ask your surgeon when to change position.](IMAGE:sleep-setup)

For added comfort, place a pillow under your knees to take pressure off your lower back. This is common practice rather than something studied.

### Side Sleeping Considerations

Side sleeping may be back on the table at five weeks. The UW Medicine handout and University of Utah Health keep you on your back for the first 3 to 4 weeks and allow side sleeping after that, and your surgeon may ask for longer. University of Utah Health suggests alternating sides at first, to help with swelling.

Stomach sleeping comes last. The UW Medicine handout groups it with side sleeping after 3 to 4 weeks, and University of Utah Health says not until your surgeon says it's okay, so the sources don't agree on a date. Ask before you try it. Everyone heals differently, and [our side sleeping guide](/when-can-i-sleep-on-my-side-after-breast-augmentation) has more.

## What Bra Should I Wear 5 Weeks After Surgery?

Proper support still matters. Two university handouts keep a support bra on around the clock for 3 to 4 weeks, MedlinePlus says night and day for 2 to 4 weeks, and your surgeon sets the length. After that, University of Utah Health says you can move into a soft, supportive sports bra, which is where many women are at five weeks. Choose something that doesn't restrict circulation or movement.

Avoid underwire bras until your incisions are fully healed, as the UW Medicine handout says. [One surgeon quoted in an article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/what-to-expect-after-your-breast-augmentation-surgery) waits about two to three months, so ask yours. Bras with adjustable straps and front closures make room for any leftover swelling. If you had a breast lift as well, [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23298-breast-lift) says women can usually wear a regular bra about eight weeks after a lift.

Wondering about nights? Those first weeks are the minimum. No source we read gives a date for going braless at night, so wait until your surgeon clears you. [Our guide to sleeping without a bra](/when-can-i-sleep-without-a-bra-after-breast-augmentation) has the detail.

For clothing, choose loose, comfortable options like button-downs or zip-up tops that don't require raising your arms. Soft fabrics reduce irritation as your breasts settle.

## What Follow-Ups and Red Flags Should I Expect?

Your surgeon sets your follow-up visits, and schedules differ, so ask what yours is. At these visits your surgeon checks the incision sites, looks at implant position and watches for signs of a problem, such as inflammation, discharge or firmness that could mean [capsular contracture](/blog/capsular-contracture).

You should be seeing real progress by now: less discomfort and greater mobility. Your final result will still take several more months.

### Routine Check-Up Protocol

Follow-up appointments matter for long-term recovery. The Aesthetic Society says that if you have external sutures, your surgeon removes them in about a week. Many stitches dissolve instead, as [our stitches guide](/when-do-breast-augmentation-stitches-dissolve) explains, so your first visit may simply be a check of your incisions.

Your surgeon also looks for signs of infection, such as redness, swelling or unusual discharge. Keep talking openly and report persistent discomfort or visible changes. [The FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/things-consider-getting-breast-implants) that if you notice any abnormal changes in your breasts or implants, you need to see your surgeon or health care provider promptly.

### Recognizing Warning Signs

While most recoveries go smoothly, spotting a problem early matters. Here is how the common worries at five weeks sort out.

| Usually normal at 5 weeks | Call your surgeon |
| --- | --- |
| Mild swelling after a busy day | One breast suddenly swells |
| Tightness that is easing | Firmness that grows, with pain and pressure |
| Implants sitting a little high | A breast that shifts suddenly |
| Tingling or brief shooting pains | Severe pain your medicine doesn't help |
| Fading redness along the scar | Spreading redness, heat or pus |
| Feeling tired after activity | Fever or chills, or an incision that opens |

![Card titled 5 Weeks Post-Op: Call Your Surgeon If. Fever or chills; spreading redness or heat; pus or bad-smelling drainage; an incision that opens; one breast suddenly swells; severe pain your medicine doesn't help. A last row says chest pain or trouble breathing means the emergency room. Sources: Cleveland Clinic, MedlinePlus and UW Medicine.](IMAGE:call-card)

Fever thresholds differ between hospitals, so use the number your surgeon gave you. [The FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/risks-and-complications-breast-implants) most infections after surgery appear within a few days to a week, but infection is possible any time after surgery.

Go to the emergency room for chest pain, trouble breathing or coughing up blood, which [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/16911-deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt) lists as signs of a blood clot in the lung. Don't hesitate to report concerns. Prompt attention can keep your recovery on track.

## Frequently asked questions

### What can I expect at 5 weeks after breast augmentation surgery?

At five weeks most swelling and discomfort have eased, since The Aesthetic Society says most residual swelling resolves within a month. Your implants are settling into a more natural position and scars continue to fade with proper care. Mobility is usually better, though some tightness or nerve sensitivity may still be there.

### Is swelling and tightness normal at 5 weeks post breast augmentation?

Some leftover swelling and tightness are normal at five weeks as your body heals. Mild firmness or a high breast position can continue for months during drop and fluff, which an article on the ASPS website says can take three to six months. New or one-sided swelling is a reason to call your surgeon.

### When can I resume exercise after breast augmentation?

At five weeks you may be walking and doing light lower-body work with your surgeon's OK. One article on the ASPS website says to avoid strenuous and upper-body movement for at least six weeks, another says patients can resume all exercises at six weeks, and Cleveland Clinic advises no heavy lifting for at least 4 weeks. Your surgeon decides.

### What type of bra should I wear 5 weeks after breast augmentation?

A soft, supportive sports or recovery bra without an underwire. University of Utah Health says you can move into a soft sports bra after three to four weeks in a surgical bra, and a UW Medicine handout says no underwire until your incisions are fully healed. Adjustable straps and front closures help with comfort.

### How should I sleep at 5 weeks after breast augmentation surgery?

University instructions keep you on your back for the first 3 to 4 weeks and allow side sleeping after that, so side sleeping may be possible now if your surgeon agrees. Alternate sides at first. If your surgeon wants you on your back for longer, a wedge pillow keeps your upper body raised and comfortable.

### What follow-up appointments or warning signs should I be aware of at 5 weeks post-op?

Your surgeon sets your follow-up schedule, so ask what yours is. Between visits, watch for sudden swelling on one side, spreading redness, heat, fever, pus or bad-smelling drainage, an incision that opens, or severe pain your medicine doesn't help. Contact your surgeon right away if any of these appear.

### Is nerve sensitivity or discomfort still normal at 5 weeks?

Yes, some is. University of Utah Health says shooting pains, tingling or other strange sensations can last for the first few months. Acute pain should be long gone, since ASPS says it typically subsides after 1 to 5 days. Ask before taking any pain medicine, and tell your surgeon if symptoms persist or get worse.

### How long does it take for breast implants to fully settle after surgery?

University of Utah Health says you begin to notice your final results around three months, with subtle changes through the 12 months after surgery. An article on the ASPS website says the whole drop and fluff process can take three to six months, and that smaller or textured implants can take longer. Regular follow-ups help track your progress.
