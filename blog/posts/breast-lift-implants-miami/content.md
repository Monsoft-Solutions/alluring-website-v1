## When do you need a breast lift with implants?

You may need both when your breasts have dropped and you also want more fullness than your own tissue gives you. A lift moves the breast and nipple higher and removes stretched skin. Implants add volume. Neither does the other's job, so ask which change you want:

- **Your breasts have dropped, and you want more fullness:** a lift with implants.
- **Your breasts have dropped, and you like your size:** a lift alone.
- **Your breasts haven't dropped, and you want more fullness:** implants alone.

[The American Society of Plastic Surgeons](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift) (ASPS) says a lift "does not significantly change the size of your breasts or round out the upper part of your breast." Your surgeon decides at your exam, and [the signs surgeons look for](/procedures/breast-lift-miami#candidate) are on our breast lift page.

## When aren't implants alone enough?

Implants alone may not be enough when the breast has dropped, because they add volume without moving the nipple or removing stretched skin. Lawrence Iteld, MD, [writing on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-are-the-benefits-of-combining-breast-augmentation-and-a-breast-lift), says "augmentation on its own isn't enough to address sagging breasts." [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift) says a surgeon may recommend a lift "if breast implants alone are unlikely to achieve the contour you desire."

[ASPS's candidate list](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/candidates) names the signs, including nipples that fall below the breast crease when unsupported or that point downward. Our guide to [checking whether you need a lift](/blog/breast-lift-candidate-checklist) shows how to look at home, and [breast augmentation vs a breast lift](/blog/breast-augmentation-vs-breast-lift) compares the two.

## When is a breast lift alone enough?

A lift alone is usually enough if you like your size and want your breasts higher and better shaped. The Aesthetic Society says: "If you are happy with your breast size, but not the way your breasts look, you may benefit from a breast lift." Our guide to [a breast lift without implants](/blog/breast-lift-without-implants) covers who it suits and what it can't do.

## Should a lift and implants be one surgery or two?

Surgeons differ, and no study we read settles it. ASPS says implants "can be placed at the same time or as a second procedure, depending on the experience of the surgeon." [Cleveland Clinic's surgery center](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/cosmetic-plastic-surgery/procedures/breast-lift) says the two are generally done together, with some patients having two separate surgeries. Two surgeons on the ASPS website make each case:

- **One surgery.** Iteld says patients "only have to undergo one surgery and one recovery period," which saves time and expense.
- **Two surgeries.** [Jaime Schwartz, MD](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/misconceptions-about-breast-augmentation-and-lift), points out that one operation makes the breast larger and the other makes it smaller. Depending on your goals and how far your breasts have dropped, Schwartz writes, "it may be safer to stage the procedures," which can lower the risk of skin breakdown, reduced nipple sensation or loss of the nipple.

Three studies measure different things:

| Study | Who it covered | What it found | Its limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Ali and colleagues, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34699429/) | A national surgical database, 2005–2018: 1,508 lifts alone, 534 lifts with implants | Complications within 30 days: 2.5% of lifts alone, 1.5% with implants. Reoperations: 1.4% and 1.5% | 30 days only |
| [Khavanin and colleagues, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25347628/) | A review of 23 studies of a lift with implants in one surgery | Complications of any kind: 13.1%. Another operation: 10.7%, from the 13 studies reporting it. Most common single complication: sagging again, 5.2% | Most followed patients for under 1 year |
| [Calobrace and colleagues, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22965238/) | One practice: 332 lifts with implants in one surgery | Complications: 22.9%. Reoperations: 23.2% (20.0% in first-time cases, 30.9% in repeat surgeries), against 10.2% after a lift alone | One practice, including repeat surgeries |

The rates aren't directly comparable: Ali counts 30 days, Khavanin's studies averaged 16 to 173 weeks, and Calobrace's abstract gives no follow-up period. Calobrace's team concluded the combined rate "was not more than additive," meaning about what you'd expect from adding the two operations' risks together. In Ali's database, combined surgery didn't carry higher odds of complications or reoperation once other factors were accounted for, although incisions reopening was most common in that group (0.6%).

None of the three compared one surgery with two, so they can't tell you which is safer for you. Ask your surgeon which they recommend and why, and if the answer is two, how long to wait in between. The sources we read give no standard gap.

## What does the FDA require for breast implants?

[The FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/things-consider-getting-breast-implants) requires that implants carry a boxed warning and that your provider review a patient decision checklist with you, under labeling it approved on October 27, 2021. You get the chance to initial and sign the checklist, and the physician implanting the device must sign it. The FDA also says:

- **Implants don't last forever.** "Breast implants are not lifetime devices." The longer you have them, the more likely you are to need them removed or replaced, and it says to assume you will need additional surgeries.
- **Silicone needs screening.** [Its guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/131885/download) recommends a first ultrasound or MRI 5–6 years after surgery, then every 2–3 years, even without symptoms.
- **Serious risks to know.** [The FDA's labeling guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/131885/download) says the boxed warning should cover breast implant-associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma (BIA-ALCL), a cancer of the immune system that occurs more often with textured implants, though it has also been diagnosed with smooth ones. "BIA-ALCL is not breast cancer," the FDA notes; it is treatable, though deaths have occurred. It should also cover systemic symptoms that some patients call breast implant illness.

## Saline or silicone implants with a lift?

The sources we read don't say either type is better with a lift, so ask your surgeon. [The FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/types-breast-implants) both have a silicone outer shell. Saline implants are filled with sterile saltwater and approved for augmentation from age 18. Silicone gel implants are approved for augmentation from age 22 and carry the screening schedule above.

## Where do the scars go with a lift and implants?

Your scars depend mostly on how much lift you need, because, as Schwartz writes, more sagging "will usually require more incisions." [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23298-breast-lift) says scars are permanent but fade a lot, and [its surgery center](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/cosmetic-plastic-surgery/procedures/breast-lift) says most of the fading happens in about 1 year. Our guides explain [the types of breast lift](/blog/types-of-breast-lift) and [how breast lift scars fade](/blog/breast-lift-scars-miami).

## How is recovery different with implants?

The sources we read give timelines for a lift, not a separate one for a lift with implants, so plan around these and ask your surgeon what changes:

- **2 to 5 days:** your chest may feel stiff and sore, [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/aftercare-recovery) says.
- **About 1 week:** many women go back to desk work and gentle activities such as walking, Cleveland Clinic says.
- **First 2 weeks:** no lifting, driving, laundry or cleaning, The Aesthetic Society advises. Discomfort, swelling and bruising last about 2 weeks, Cleveland Clinic says.
- **Up to 6 weeks:** lifting, exercise and sex may not be cleared until then (Cleveland Clinic).
- **About 8 weeks:** women can usually wear a regular bra (Cleveland Clinic).

See [what to expect 5 weeks after breast augmentation](/weeks-post-op-breast-augmentation-what-to-expect), which covers implants without a lift, and our [breast lift recovery guide](/blog/breast-lift-recovery-guide-miami).

Call 911 or go to the emergency room for new or severe chest pain, trouble breathing, or pain, redness or swelling in one leg, which [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000132.htm) says can be signs of a blood clot in the lung. Call your surgical team immediately if either breast becomes red, hot, swollen or painful, or if you have bleeding, drainage, redness or swelling at an incision, dizziness, a fever higher than 100.5°F, severe pain not helped with medicine, or nausea or vomiting. That list is Cleveland Clinic's.

## How long does a breast lift with implants last?

It is long-lasting, but the sources we read don't agree on how long, and none measures it for a lift with implants. [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/results) says results "will be long-lasting" while your breasts keep changing with aging and gravity, and Cleveland Clinic says they "should last for several years." [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/results) says results "may last longer" with augmentation, though sagging again was the most common single complication in the Khavanin review.

ASPS also says pregnancy can "minimize or reverse" a lift's improvement, and that significant weight loss can affect breast appearance. Our post on [timing after pregnancy](/blog/breast-lift-miami-post-pregnancy) goes further.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which incisions are used for a breast lift with implants?

It depends on how far your breasts have dropped. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/detailed-procedure-information) says a crescent above the areola may be used for minor sagging or alongside augmentation, a ring around the areola for mild sagging, and an anchor for severe sagging. That list describes lifts in general; only the crescent is tied to augmentation there, and the society's moderate-sagging option, the keyhole, is described for people who don't want implants. Ask your surgeon which fits a lift with implants.

### How long do implants take to settle after a lift?

Plan on months. Eric Mariotti, MD, [writing on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-does-drop-and-fluff-mean-after-breast-augmentation) about augmentation, says implants can sit high and look flat at first, and that the "drop and fluff" process can take 3 to 6 months. No source we read gives a separate timeline for implants placed with a lift, so ask your surgeon what to expect.

### What are the most common complications of a lift with implants?

In a 2014 review of 23 studies (Khavanin and colleagues), the most common were the breast sagging again (5.2%), poor scarring (3.7%), capsular contracture (3.0%) and tissue-related asymmetry (2.9%); infection, hematoma and seroma were under 2% each. The FDA says the most common local problems with implants are capsular contracture, reoperation and implant removal. Our page covers [breast lift risks and safety](/procedures/breast-lift-miami#safety).

### What are the long-term effects of a lift with implants?

Scars stay but fade, and your breasts keep changing with age. You may lose some feeling in your nipples and breasts. Cleveland Clinic's surgery center says it gradually returns over weeks or months, numbness can last up to 1 year for some, and rarely the loss is permanent. Implants need monitoring for as long as you have them, the FDA says, and you should assume more surgery at some point.

### Should I wait until after pregnancy and breastfeeding?

Yes. [The FDA's example patient checklist](https://www.fda.gov/media/131885/download) says you aren't a candidate for implants while pregnant or nursing, and ASPS says breast surgery should wait until you have finished having children. Shahram Salemy, MD, [writing on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/better-breasts-after-pregnancy-and-breastfeeding), suggests waiting up to 6 months after breastfeeding stops, so the breasts settle into their final shape. Other surgeons give different waits, so ask yours.
