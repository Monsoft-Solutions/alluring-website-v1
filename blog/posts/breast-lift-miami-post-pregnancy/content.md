## When should I get a breast lift after pregnancy?

Once three things are true: you're done having children, breastfeeding has stopped and your breasts have stopped changing, and your weight is stable. [The American Society of Plastic Surgeons](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/results) (ASPS) puts the first one plainly: "Breast surgery should be entertained after child bearing is completed."

There's no clock running against you. Karen Horton, MD, [quoted on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/post-pregnancy-plastic-surgery-what-can-you-do-now-and-what-needs-to-wait), says breast surgery should be postponed until you're certain your breasts have stopped changing, and that it's safe to wait 6 months to a year. She often suggests waiting until all children are in school before "a big surgical procedure", speaking of surgery such as a reduction or tummy tuck.

| Where you are | What it usually means | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Done having children, breastfeeding finished, weight stable | A good time to see a surgeon | Your breasts are less likely to keep changing |
| Still breastfeeding | Wait | Breastfeeding should be finished before breast surgery |
| Stopped breastfeeding recently | Wait until your breasts stop changing | The tissue keeps changing after weaning |
| Another pregnancy is likely soon | Wait | Pregnancy can minimize or reverse a lift |
| More children possible, but not soon | Talk it through with a surgeon | In certain cases surgery can still be done safely |
| Still losing weight | Wait until your weight is stable | Significant weight loss after surgery can change the result; see [a breast lift after weight loss](/blog/breast-lift-recovery-weight-loss-miami) |

Whether you need a lift at all is a separate question. [Do I need a breast lift?](/blog/breast-lift-candidate-checklist) walks you through it, and the [candidate section of our breast lift page](/procedures/breast-lift-miami#candidate) sums up who it suits.

## How long after breastfeeding should I wait for a breast lift?

Surgeons usually suggest waiting at least a few months, and they don't all give the same number. We found no rule from a medical society, so these are individual surgeons' views, published on the ASPS website:

| Surgeon | What they say about the time after breastfeeding stops |
| --- | --- |
| [Kristy Hamilton, MD](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/post-pregnancy-plastic-surgery-what-can-you-do-now-and-what-needs-to-wait) | She prefers to wait at least 3 months after weaning, because the tissue continues to change |
| Karen Horton, MD | It can take 4 to 6 months for the breasts to contract, and she says it's safe to wait 6 months to a year |
| [Shahram Salemy, MD](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/better-breasts-after-pregnancy-and-breastfeeding) | Up to 6 months, so the breasts settle into their final shape |

What all three want is the same thing: a breast that has stopped changing. Note the month you stopped nursing, and tell your surgeon whether your size or shape still seems to be shifting. Your surgeon decides at consultation.

## Does breastfeeding cause sagging?

Probably not on its own, at least in the studies we could find. Pregnancy itself seems to matter more. In a study of 132 patients, the number of pregnancies and significant weight loss were linked to sagging, but breastfeeding was not ([Rinker and colleagues, Annals of Plastic Surgery, 2010](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20354434/)). Age, body mass index, cup size and smoking were also risk factors.

An earlier study by the same team, of 93 women, found the same ([Rinker and colleagues, Aesthetic Surgery Journal, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19083576/)).

A separate study of 496 Italian mothers, not surgical patients, found women reported changes about as often whether or not they had breastfed ([Pisacane and Continisio, Acta Paediatrica, 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15499956/)).

The studies we found don't point at nursing, but they rely partly on women's own reports and can't rule it out, and [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23298-breast-lift) and [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift) still list breastfeeding among the causes. Dr. Horton notes that breasts often lose volume after having a baby even without breastfeeding.

## What if I want another baby after a breast lift?

You can still have one, but the pregnancy can undo part of the lift. ASPS says: "Changes that occur in the breasts during pregnancy can minimize or reverse the improvement a breast lift provides." Dr. Salemy recommends waiting until you don't plan to become pregnant again, and adds that "in certain cases, women who want breast enhancement but may also have future children can still undergo surgery in a safe manner."

If another baby is likely soon, waiting usually makes sense. If you're unsure, or it's years away, say so at your consultation. You're weighing the years you'd enjoy the lift against the chance of wanting a second surgery afterward.

## Can I breastfeed after a breast lift?

Many women can, but no one can promise it, and you may not make a full supply. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/detailed-procedure-information) explains that the nipples and areolas stay attached to the tissue underneath "to preserve sensation and the ability to breastfeed." Dr. Salemy [writes in a later post](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/common-myths-about-breast-lifts-debunked) that "many women have been able to breastfeed successfully after the procedure." He gives no rate. We found none for a lift alone: one small French study that pooled lifts and reductions found 41% of women breastfed successfully after surgery, against 82% whose pregnancy came before it, and its authors urge caution ([Faure and colleagues, Annales de Chirurgie Plastique Esthétique, 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38866681/)).

[The CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/breastfeeding-special-circumstances/hcp/illnesses-conditions/breast-surgery.html) says a breast lift can affect the nerves and ducts in the breast, and that most mothers who have had breast surgery can make some milk but may not make a full supply. Cleveland Clinic lists not being able to breastfeed among the possible risks, alongside the others on our [breast lift safety section](/procedures/breast-lift-miami#safety).

If you might nurse again:

- **Say so before surgery.** Dr. Salemy says your surgeon may be able to tailor the lift to preserve as many milk ducts as possible.
- **Ask for a lactation consultant after the birth.** The CDC tells health care providers to refer mothers who have had breast surgery to one.
- **Know that a partial supply still counts.** The CDC says women without a full milk supply can still breastfeed, and may need to supplement. The CDC also says babies of mothers who've had breast surgery should be carefully monitored for adequate weight gain, so tell your baby's doctor about your surgery.

## Is there a right age for a breast lift?

No. No source we read sets one, and health and timing matter more. [ASPS's candidate list](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/candidates) has no age on it: you're physically healthy, your weight is stable, you don't smoke, and your nipples fall below the breast crease when unsupported. Dr. Salemy writes that "age is not the sole determinant" and that women of various ages can benefit.

Age was one of the risk factors for sagging in the 2010 study, but a risk factor isn't a cut-off. If implants are part of the plan, the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/types-breast-implants) has approved saline implants for breast augmentation from age 18 and silicone gel implants from age 22.

## How long does a breast lift last?

For years, but no reliable source gives a set number. ASPS calls the results "long-lasting" and says your breasts can keep changing with aging and gravity. Cleveland Clinic says results should last for several years, and that pregnancy and weight changes may affect them. ASPS says keeping your weight steady and living a healthy lifestyle helps you keep your new look longer.

The data are thin. A 2014 review of 23 studies of lifts with implants found the most common complication was the breast sagging again (5.2%), but most of its studies followed patients for under 1 year ([Khavanin and colleagues, Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery, 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25347628/)), and a very small study measured breast shape 10 years after surgery, including in 5 women who had a lift alone ([Swanson, Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery Global Open, 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30276063/)). So if you see a fixed number of years quoted, ask where it comes from.

You'll see the lift right away, and ASPS says the final shape appears over the next few months. To see results, browse our [before and after photos of real breast lift patients](/gallery/breast-lift), and ask how long after surgery each was taken.

## Will a lift alone be enough after pregnancy?

A lift alone is enough if you like your size and mainly want your breasts higher. ASPS says it "does not significantly change the size of your breasts or round out the upper part of your breast." So if you miss fullness at the top, you may want volume too. Going the other way doesn't work either: Lawrence Iteld, MD, [writing on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-are-the-benefits-of-combining-breast-augmentation-and-a-breast-lift), says "augmentation on its own isn't enough to address sagging breasts."

These posts go further: [what a lift without implants can and can't do](/blog/breast-lift-without-implants), [when you need a lift with implants](/blog/breast-lift-implants-miami) and [how a lift and an augmentation compare](/blog/breast-augmentation-vs-breast-lift). Every lift also leaves scars, so read [where the scars sit and how they fade](/blog/breast-lift-scars-miami) too.

If your belly changed as well, [a mommy makeover pairs breast surgery with a tummy tuck](/procedures/mommy-makeover-miami). Dr. Hamilton says it needs, crucially, the completion of childbearing. At Alluring, when procedures such as a lift and a tummy tuck are combined in one surgery, $500 comes off the total for two procedures and $1,000 for three, and your surgeon confirms the price at consultation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get a breast lift while I'm still breastfeeding?

It's better to wait. An [ASPS article on mommy makeovers](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/mommy-makeovers-what-women-should-know-about-postpartum-surgical-procedures) says it's important that breastfeeding has already finished before breast work. If implants are part of the plan, the [FDA's example patient decision checklist](https://www.fda.gov/media/131885/download) says you aren't a candidate for breast implants while pregnant or nursing.

### How soon after having a baby can I have a breast lift?

Usually not for at least 6 months, and longer if you're breastfeeding. Kristy Hamilton, MD, quoted on the ASPS website, recommends a minimum of 6 months for most procedures, ideally 9 to 12. Karen Horton, MD, quoted in the same article, says the body keeps changing for up to a full year after delivery and recommends waiting up to that long.

### Will a breast lift make my breasts smaller?

Not noticeably. ASPS says a lift doesn't significantly change your breast size, because the surgery removes excess skin and tightens the tissue around it. If you want them smaller as well as higher, that's a reduction, which [our comparison of a lift and a reduction](/blog/breast-lift-vs-reduction-miami) explains.

### Am I too old for a breast lift at 50 or 60?

Age alone doesn't rule you out. ASPS's candidate list covers health, a stable weight, not smoking and how your breasts sit, and it gives no age limit. ASPS's [preparation list](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/preparation) says you may be asked to get lab testing or a medical evaluation first. Your surgeon decides at consultation, based on your health rather than your age.

### Can exercise or creams lift sagging breasts?

We found no evidence that they can. A lift works by removing excess skin and tightening the tissue around the breast, and exercise and creams don't remove skin. In the 2010 study, not doing regular upper body exercise wasn't a risk factor for sagging either. Our guide to [non-surgical breast lift options](/blog/non-surgical-breast-lift) looks at what else is sold for this.

### Does insurance cover a breast lift after pregnancy?

Usually not. [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/cost) says most health insurance plans do not cover breast lift surgery or its complications, so check your own plan. [Financing is available](/plastic-surgery-financing-miami) if you'd like to spread the cost, and our [guide to breast lift cost](/blog/breast-lift-cost) shows what a quote should spell out.

### How do I plan recovery with young children at home?

Line up help first. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/preparing-your-procedure) advises arranging care for young children for at least the first week, and [its recovery page](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/aftercare-recovery) says no lifting, driving, laundry or cleaning for the first 2 weeks. Cleveland Clinic says lifting may not be cleared for up to 6 weeks, so ask when you can pick up your children. Our [week-by-week recovery guide](/blog/breast-lift-recovery-guide-miami) has the details.
