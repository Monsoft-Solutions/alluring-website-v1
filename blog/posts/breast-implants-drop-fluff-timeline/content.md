## How long does it take for implants to drop and fluff?

In most cases, 3 to 6 months, according to an [article on the ASPS website by Dr. Eric Mariotti](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-does-drop-and-fluff-mean-after-breast-augmentation). So if your implants still look high and tight a month after surgery, that alone is not a problem. An [article on the ASPS website by Dr. Cindy Wu](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/considering-breast-augmentation-for-beach-season-heres-when-you-should-plan-your-surgery) calls it normal. For everything else that happens in those months, see the [breast augmentation recovery timeline](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#recovery).

## What does drop and fluff mean?

Drop and fluff is the everyday name for implants settling into place after surgery. Right after the operation, implants can ride high on your chest and look flatter than you hoped, or slightly square. Dr. Mariotti's article puts that down to tight skin and muscle, which ease over the following weeks.

The phrase covers two changes that happen together:

- **Drop:** as tight muscles relax, the implant moves to a lower, more natural-looking position on your chest.
- **Fluff:** the implant fills out the lower part of the breast, and the shape becomes smoother, softer and rounder.

![Three side-view line drawings of breast implant settling, labeled early post-op, drop and fluff, with the implant sitting lower and rounder from the first to the third.](IMAGE:sequence-diagram)

## Drop and fluff week by week

Nobody has published a week-by-week schedule that we could find, so treat this as a rough map rather than a deadline. The middle column is the usual description. The right column is what the sources actually say.

| When | What you may notice | What the sources say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Week 1 | High on the chest, tight and swollen, perhaps flat or slightly square | Normal, from tight skin and muscle (Mariotti). Pain is typically worst in the first 48 hours ([The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/aftercare-recovery)) |
| Weeks 2–3 | Swelling and bruising ease, and the implants may start to settle | Swelling and bruising should subside in 1 to 2 weeks, and muscles begin to relax (Mariotti) |
| Weeks 4–6 | Softer. Some women see a clear drop by now, and others don't yet | Most remaining swelling resolves within a month (The Aesthetic Society). Sitting high after the first month is normal (Wu) |
| Months 2–3 | The lower breast fills out and the shape rounds | After the first month, implants settle over the next 2 to 3 months (Wu). You begin to notice final results around 3 months ([University of Utah Health](https://healthcare.utah.edu/plastic-surgery/breast/recovery)) |
| Months 3–6 | The process can finish | The entire process can take 3 to 6 months (Mariotti) |
| Up to 12 months | Small changes only | Subtle changes can continue through the 12 months after surgery (University of Utah Health) |

## Drop and fluff before and after: what changes

The difference between before and after is mostly position and softness. Here is what Dr. Mariotti's article describes:

| | Before they settle | After they settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Position | High on the chest | Lower and more natural-looking |
| Shape | Flatter than you hoped, or slightly square | Smoother, softer and more rounded |
| Lower breast | Not yet filled out | Filled out |
| Size | Can change several times | May look bigger or fuller |
| Feel | Tight | Softer |

To see patients, look at [real breast augmentation results in our gallery](/gallery/breast-augmentation). When you compare any before-and-after set, check how long after surgery the after photo was taken, because a photo from the early weeks doesn't show the final shape.

Many searches ask whether the progression looks different over or under the muscle, or with high profile implants. None of the medical sources we read compares drop and fluff timing by placement or by profile, so be wary of a confident answer. See [over or under the muscle, compared](/blog/breast-implants-over-or-under-the-muscle) for how placement differs, and ask your surgeon what to expect with yours.

## What can make implants take longer to drop?

Implant weight, implant surface and your own pace of healing. If yours are slower than a friend's, Dr. Mariotti's article says that is okay, and not to panic.

- **Smaller, lighter implants** may take longer than heavier ones, because heavier implants get extra help from gravity.
- **Textured implants** may settle more gradually than smooth ones, because they have more friction against the tissue around them.
- **Your own healing** sets the pace, and it differs from person to person.

The closest thing to measurements we found is a [2026 single-surgeon study of 106 patients](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41783999/) in the Aesthetic Surgery Journal, which used 3D imaging at 6 and 12 months. The lower breast lengthened most with smooth round implants and showed almost no change with polyurethane-coated shaped ones, and implant type and volume were both linked to the change. It measured how much the lower breast lengthened, not how fast implants drop, so it gives no timeline. See [how smooth and textured implants differ](/blog/saline-vs-silicone-breast-implants).

One side can also move first. University of Utah Health says each breast may heal at a different rate, so a temporary difference is common. Our guide to [one breast sitting higher than the other](/blog/swelling-and-asymmetry-after-breast-augmentation-faq) covers what is normal and what isn't. It is also why the size you see in week 1 isn't the final look, which is worth remembering if you are checking your result against an [implant size guide](/blog/breast-implant-size-guide).

## How can I help my implants drop and fluff?

Honestly, not by much. Dr. Mariotti's article says there is little you can do to make implants drop and fluff faster, and that following your surgeon's aftercare is what helps healing go smoothly. Here is what that looks like, and what the evidence does and doesn't support:

- **Wear your support bra as instructed.** [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/recovery) says a support bra will minimize swelling and support the breasts as they heal. Our guide to [when you can sleep without a bra](/when-can-i-sleep-without-a-bra-after-breast-augmentation) has the stages.
- **Walk.** An [article on the ASPS website by Dr. Catherine Huang-Begovic](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/exercise-after-breast-augmentation) says light walking from the start helps circulation and prevents blood clots. Nothing we read says it speeds up settling.
- **Hold off on chest work.** No source gives a single push-up date. An [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/back-to-active-returning-to-exercise-after-breast-augmentation-recovery) says to avoid strenuous and upper-body movement for at least six weeks. Dr. Huang-Begovic says patients can resume all exercises at six weeks, and advises avoiding strenuous chest exercise such as push-ups, especially with implants under the muscle. Your surgeon decides.
- **Massage only if your surgeon prescribes it.** A [2017 literature review (Sood and colleagues)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29348783/) found the available data do not support massage for preventing capsular contracture, and we found nothing showing it speeds up drop and fluff. See [implant massage: when to start and how often](/how-often-to-massage-breast-after-augmentation).
- **Use a band only if your surgeon gives you one.** ASPS mentions an elastic band, called a bandeau, as a kind of support garment. Your surgeon decides whether you need it.

## When should I worry about drop and fluff?

Slow settling on its own is not an emergency. Dr. Mariotti's article says most implants drop and fluff over three to six months, and to contact your surgeon if that doesn't appear to be happening, or if you have unusual pain, bleeding, or swelling that gets worse.

| Usually normal | Call your surgeon |
| --- | --- |
| Implants that sit high and feel tight in the first weeks | Sudden swelling of a breast ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm), on a page that also covers lifts and reductions) |
| One side settling before the other (University of Utah Health) | Growing enlargement or firmness with pain and pressure ([Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation)) |
| Size and shape changing several times (Mariotti) | Fever or chills, using the number your surgeon gave you, or bad-smelling drainage ([UW Medicine handout](https://healthonline.washington.edu/document/health_online/pdf/Breast-Augmentation.pdf)) |
| Shooting pains, tingling or other strange sensations for the first few months (University of Utah Health) | Severe pain your medicine doesn't help, pus, or stitches that open (UW Medicine; Cleveland Clinic) |
| | Not settling after several months, or sooner with the warning signs (Mariotti) |

For chest pain, trouble breathing or coughing blood, go to the emergency room. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/16911-deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt) lists these among the signs of a blood clot that has traveled to the lungs.

![Two-column card titled Drop and Fluff: Normal or Call? The Usually normal column lists sitting high in the first weeks, one side settling first, and shape changing several times. The Call your surgeon column lists sudden swelling, firmness with pain and pressure, fever or chills, and drainage or an opening incision.](IMAGE:normal-or-call)

A breast that stays firm or gets firmer over time is a different thing from a slow drop, especially with pain or a change in shape. The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/risks-and-complications-breast-implants) describes capsular contracture as the hardening of the breast around the implant, and says the two most severe grades may require another operation. Read more about [capsular contracture](/blog/capsular-contracture) and [breast implant safety](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#safety), and let your surgeon feel it.

## When can I buy new bras after breast augmentation?

Once your shape has stopped changing, and no source we read gives a date for that. Dr. Mariotti's article says to give your breasts several months to settle before you spring for a new wardrobe.

Until then, stick to what your surgeon allows. A UW Medicine handout says no underwire until your incisions are fully healed, and one surgeon quoted in an [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/what-to-expect-after-your-breast-augmentation-surgery) holds off for about two to three months, until the implants have settled.

If you are still deciding on surgery, Dr. Victoria Karlinsky, MD, FACS, can walk you through the timeline at a [free consultation](/free-consultation). If you are flying in from another state, Dr. Karlinsky tells you, in writing, how many nights to stay in Miami before you fly home. Much of the settling happens after that, so ask who to call if something changes once you are back.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the signs that my implants are settling properly?

The upper breast softens, the lower breast fills out, and the implants sit lower and feel less tight. Size and shape can change several times before they stop, which an article on the ASPS website calls normal. One side may lead the other. Your follow-up visits are the place to confirm it, because your surgeon can feel what a photo can't show.

### Are there exercises that help with drop and fluff?

None that has been shown to. Light walking from the start helps circulation and helps prevent blood clots, but nothing we read says it speeds up settling. For chest work such as push-ups, no source gives a single date: one article on the ASPS website says to avoid upper-body movement for at least six weeks, another advises avoiding strenuous chest work such as push-ups, and your surgeon decides. Our guide to [exercise after breast augmentation](/blog/when-can-you-work-out-after-breast-augmentation) has the full plan.

### How long does swelling take to go down after breast implants?

Most of it goes down within about 2 weeks, according to a surgeon quoted in an article on the ASPS website, and The Aesthetic Society says most remaining swelling resolves within a month. Swelling can change how your breasts look early on, so it is hard to judge shape before then. If one side suddenly swells, call your surgeon.

### When do breast implants soften?

Gradually, as swelling goes down and muscles relax. An article on the ASPS website says implants begin to settle and soften once the first 1 to 2 weeks of swelling and bruising have passed, and the whole process can take 3 to 6 months. A breast that gets firmer over time, rather than softer, is different and worth a call to your surgeon.

### Will my implants look bigger once they drop?

They may. An article on the ASPS website says implants may appear bigger or fuller once they have dropped to a lower position and fluffed into a rounder, softer shape. Size and shape can also change as swelling and tightness ease, so give it several months before you judge the size for good.

### Can implants drop too far?

Yes, though it is a different problem from normal settling. The FDA describes implant malposition as an implant that is not in the correct position, and says shifting can be caused by gravity, trauma or capsular contracture. Implants that sit very low are a reason to see your surgeon. The FDA says a reoperation to reposition the implant may be needed. See [rippling and bottoming out](/blog/breast-implant-rippling-bottoming-out).
