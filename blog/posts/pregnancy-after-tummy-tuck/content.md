## Can you get pregnant after a tummy tuck?

Yes, women do. The evidence on mothers and babies is limited. A 2023 review of 237 patients found no deaths of mothers or newborns, though that is too few patients to rule out a rare risk, and a 2024 analysis linked a past tummy tuck to higher odds of preterm delivery. A later pregnancy can also stretch your skin and muscles again and change your result. If you become pregnant, tell your obstetrician (OB) about your surgery. If you're still planning, ask your surgeon about timing, and start with [who a tummy tuck suits, and when to wait](/procedures/tummy-tuck-miami#candidate).

Nothing we read measured whether a tummy tuck changes your chance of conceiving, because the studies start with women who are already pregnant. Don't count on surgery to prevent a pregnancy either. In [a 2022 survey of 32 women](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34622279/) who became pregnant after a tummy tuck, 15 said the pregnancy wasn't planned.

## What did the review of 237 patients find?

It found no deaths of mothers or newborns in any study, though 237 patients is too few to rule out a rare risk. Its authors concluded that pregnancy "should not be contraindicated" after a tummy tuck. This is [a 2023 systematic review by Karunaratne and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37266593/) in Aesthetic Plastic Surgery, covering 17 studies and 237 patients. We read its published abstract, not the full paper.

| What it measured | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Average time from tummy tuck to pregnancy | 3.75 years |
| Had muscle repair, where reported | 89.74% |
| Delivered by cesarean | 43.63% |
| Delivered vaginally | 56.37% |
| Average pregnancy length at delivery | 38.90 weeks |
| Preterm delivery, before 37 weeks | 9.85% |
| Low birthweight, under 2,500 g | 7.22% |
| Deaths of mothers or newborns | None in any study |
| Average follow-up | 8.5 months |

What it doesn't show matters as much:

- **Too few patients for rare events.** Deaths are rare in any pregnancy, so finding none among 237 patients can't show the risk is no higher than usual.
- **No comparison group in the abstract.** It gives rates for these women only, so it can't say whether 9.85% preterm is higher or lower than for similar women without a tummy tuck.
- **Short follow-up.** At 8.5 months on average, it says little about how the abdomen looks years later.
- **A long average gap.** With 3.75 years between surgery and pregnancy on average, it says little about getting pregnant soon after surgery.
- **Newborn deaths only.** The abstract says "neonatal" and doesn't say whether it counted miscarriage or stillbirth.
- **A mixed group.** 31.75% had previous weight-loss surgery, and 94.51% had a tummy tuck rather than another body contouring procedure.
- **Problems were recorded.** The 14 studies that documented them, covering 136 patients, list 21 fetal or newborn diagnoses or complications, 104 maternal and 96 affecting the abdominal wall or appearance. The abstract doesn't spell them out.

## Does a tummy tuck raise the risk of preterm birth?

It may. The largest study with a comparison group that we read linked a past tummy tuck to higher odds of preterm delivery, though it can't show that the surgery caused it. A smaller, less controlled one reported rates similar to the general population, so the question isn't settled.

[Cheng and colleagues](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38447678/), a team led by maternal-fetal medicine specialists writing in the American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology MFM in 2024, used health records from 68 US organizations. They compared 304 pregnant patients who had a past tummy tuck with 44,433 who didn't, then matched the groups for other factors:

- **Preterm delivery:** higher odds, with an odds ratio of 2.15 (95% confidence interval, 1.48 to 3.13), or about double the odds.
- **Preeclampsia and waters breaking early:** lower, which is also only an association.
- **Other outcomes they tracked, including stillbirth and fetal growth problems:** no significant difference, though 304 patients is too few to rule out a difference in something as rare as stillbirth.

The authors conclude that a tummy tuck "may be associated with a relative increase in the rates of preterm delivery and cesarean delivery", and that wanting a future pregnancy need not rule one out. We could read only the abstract, because the full paper is behind a paywall. It gives no actual rates, and no cesarean result after matching, so we can't say how many more preterm births or cesareans that means. It also doesn't say which factors were matched or how long after surgery these pregnancies came.

[Pimentel and colleagues, in a poster abstract in a 2016 supplement to Obstetrics & Gynecology](https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/abstract/10.1097/01.aog.0000483356.94088.2b~is-the-cesarean-delivery-rate-higher-after-abdominoplasty), looked at 99 women with a past tummy tuck at one urban hospital. They say the preterm rate was similar to that reported in the general population. But the abstract, which is all we could read, gives no figure for it, the comparison was with hospital and published rates rather than matched women, these women were older and had more children than average, and more identified as Hispanic than at that hospital overall.

The 2022 survey asked about this too. Of its 32 women, 5 (15.6%) reported a late premature birth, between weeks 35 and 37, 1 a miscarriage and 1 an emergency C-section. It surveyed only women who had a tummy tuck, recruited through social media, so it can't say whether that is more than usual.

[A 2020 literature review](https://revista.fecolsog.org/index.php/rcog/article/view/3567) in the Revista Colombiana de Obstetricia y Ginecología, an obstetrics journal, is one we could read in full, in Spanish. Its authors, a surgeon and a medical intern rather than obstetricians, found 13 case reports and case series covering 111 patients, 99 of them the Pimentel women. They say a tummy tuck could increase cervical prolapse, where the cervix slips down, and cesarean section, "although the impact on perinatal outcome is not clear". The prolapse finding rests on two single-patient reports.

We also looked for guidance from the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists on pregnancy after a tummy tuck and found none. Your OB is the right person to weigh this for your own pregnancy.

## What can change in your result?

Your skin can stretch, your scar can widen and your belly can bulge again. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/procedures/11017-tummy-tuck) says your muscles and skin can stretch after pregnancy, which can affect the outcome. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/tummy-tuck/results) says the abdominal skin could stretch out again. The 2022 survey puts numbers on it, as the women reported it themselves:

| Change after pregnancy | Of 32 women |
| --- | --- |
| New stretch marks | 16 (50%) |
| Extra abdominal skin | 12 (37.5%) |
| Wider scar | 9 (28%) |
| Abdominal bulge | 8 (reported as 25.8%) |
| New hernia | 2 (6.3%) |
| Had a revision surgery | 2 (6.2%) |

Most didn't rate the change as severe. On a scale of 1 for no effect to 10 for the worst, the average was 2.7, and only 3 scores were above 5. But these women were recruited through social media groups about tummy tucks, so they may not represent everyone. We read this survey's abstract, not the full paper.

Your scar can also change color for a while. In [a 2023 case report by Alhumsi and colleagues](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10240990/), a woman's scar darkened during pregnancy, possibly from hormones, and was lighter 4 months after delivery. The authors also describe a new bulge that wasn't there before the pregnancy, though elsewhere they report no aesthetic change other than the scar, and she was satisfied with her result.

## Should you wait until you're done having children?

The [American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS)](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/tummy-tuck) says yes if you may want more children: women considering future pregnancies "would be advised to postpone a tummy tuck". The Aesthetic Society and Cleveland Clinic advise the same. Kristy Hamilton, MD, in [an article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/post-pregnancy-plastic-surgery-what-can-you-do-now-and-what-needs-to-wait), says she strongly advises waiting "since a future pregnancy can undo the results."

The review and the 2024 analysis seem to say the opposite, but they mainly ask whether pregnancy after surgery is dangerous, not whether your result lasts. The 2022 survey did ask about results, and its authors think the advice is worth reevaluating, but it is small and self-reported. Here is how that can sort out for you:

- **You know you want more children.** Waiting is what the societies advise, and it avoids putting your result through a pregnancy.
- **You aren't sure.** To women unsure whether they're ready for surgery, Dr. Hamilton says "Consult early, decide later." A surgeon can tell you what you would gain now and what a pregnancy might change.
- **You're finished, but your body is bothering you now.** If you recently had a baby, read [how long to wait after a C-section](/blog/tummy-tuck-after-c-section). If a gap between your stomach muscles is the main problem, read [when a tummy tuck repairs diastasis recti](/blog/tummy-tuck-diastasis-recti-miami).

Even if you are sure, ask your doctor about birth control. Alhumsi and colleagues describe a mother of five, finished having children and not on the pill or an IUD, who became pregnant about a month after her tummy tuck. She had gestational diabetes, and the authors otherwise call her pregnancy uneventful and her baby healthy, but one case can't show what is typical. They urge thorough counseling on the risks of pregnancy after a tummy tuck "even if they have completed their family."

## What if you're pregnant now?

Tell your OB about your tummy tuck at your first visit, even if it was years ago. Give them the year of your surgery, whether your muscles were repaired and whether mesh was used. If you aren't sure, ask your surgeon's office for your surgery record. If your surgery was recent, tell your surgeon too.

It matters because it can change how your care is planned. [A 2011 case report from Mount Sinai Hospital](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22435285/) describes a woman who hadn't mentioned her tummy tuck before a cesarean, and notes that patients "may hesitate, or even forget, to reveal cosmetic surgeries". We read its abstract, not the full paper. There is no reason to feel embarrassed. It is simply useful for your team to know. The numbers above can't predict your own pregnancy, because the studies are small or rely on records. Your OB can tell you what they mean for you.

## What does a tummy tuck mean for delivery?

Vaginal birth is still common, but a cesarean may be more likely. In the Pimentel study, 49.5% had a cesarean, against 31.7% at the same hospital and 32.7% nationally. Among women with a past vaginal birth, 22.6% had a first cesarean, against 11.5% in published rates. These women were older and had more children than average, and the abstract doesn't say how many had already had a cesarean. The 2024 analysis also suggests a higher rate, without a matched figure. The 2020 literature review suggests the same, but 99 of its 111 patients are the Pimentel women. In the 2023 review, 56.37% delivered vaginally, and your OB decides with you based on your pregnancy.

Two points are worth raising with your delivery team:

- **Numbing.** The 2023 review notes a spinal block that reached lower than expected in 3 cases, and the 2020 literature review describes 3 patients whose block needed reinforcing during a cesarean. These may be the same reports. So mention the surgery to your anesthesia team as well.
- **Mesh.** Some tummy tucks reinforce the muscles with mesh. In [a 2025 case report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12510398/), obstetricians Ahmed and colleagues describe one such patient's cesarean, which they call feasible but technically challenging. They say surgeons "should anticipate encountering mesh", that the long-term outcomes of cutting through and re-suturing mesh during pregnancy remain unknown, and that mesh is generally recommended after childbearing.

## What to ask at a consultation

If you are still planning, take these questions with you:

- Given that I may want more children, would you advise waiting, and why?
- Would my surgery include muscle repair or mesh, and how could a pregnancy affect it?
- If I got pregnant, how long would you want me to have healed first?

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I wait to get pregnant after a tummy tuck?

No source we read sets a number, so ask your surgeon and your OB. The 3.75 years in the 2023 review is an average of what happened, not advice, and the 2020 literature review found gaps from 2 months to 13 years. Those gaps describe what happened, not what is safe, so don't treat any of them as a target.

### Will my muscles separate again if I get pregnant?

They may, but no source we read says how often. In the 2022 survey, 8 of 32 women reported a bulge and 2 a new hernia, though a bulge alone doesn't prove the muscles separated. The 2020 literature review says the repair appears to hold, but also that the question remains unanswered, and one 2023 case report describes a new bulge after delivery. Your surgeon can explain what your repair would involve.

### Can I deliver vaginally after a tummy tuck?

Many women have: 56.37% in the 2023 review. Your OB decides with you. If you have also had a cesarean, the [American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists](https://www.acog.org/womens-health/faqs/cesarean-birth) says you may be able to give birth vaginally, depending on factors such as the type and number of earlier incisions. Its page doesn't mention tummy tucks, so ask your OB about yours.

### Should I have a tummy tuck before having more kids?

The surgical societies advise against it, because pregnancy can change your result. That said, 18 of the 32 women in the 2022 survey said they would recommend having one before pregnancy, and its authors say there is room to reevaluate the advice. All of them had already gone ahead, so weigh that view with your surgeon.

### Can a tummy tuck be repeated after pregnancy?

Sometimes. In the 2022 survey, 2 of 32 women had a revision surgery. Speaking generally, not about pregnancy, [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/tummy-tuck/results) says that in some situations a single surgery may not achieve an optimal result and another may be necessary. No source we read says how often a second tummy tuck is needed after pregnancy or how well it works, so ask your surgeon what it would involve.

### Can I have a tummy tuck while pregnant?

No. [StatPearls](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK431058/), a medical reference, lists pregnancy as an absolute contraindication. If there's any chance you are pregnant, tell your surgeon. In [a 2024 case report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11666211/), a woman had surgery at about 6 weeks with nobody aware. She later had gestational diabetes and high blood pressure, and a small but healthy baby. The authors don't blame the surgery, but say that doesn't assure the same for others. The 2020 literature review describes another such surgery, complicated by a wound infection.
