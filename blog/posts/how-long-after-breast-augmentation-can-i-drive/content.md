## How long after breast augmentation can I drive?

About a week for many women, and never while you are taking opioid pain medicine. Beyond that, the sources give different dates.

Your surgeon's word overrides all of it. University of Utah Health says to get your doctor's approval at a follow-up visit before you drive. Here is what each source says, side by side:

| Source | What it says | What it covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [University of Utah Health](https://healthcare.utah.edu/plastic-surgery/breast/recovery) | Most people are ready within one week, and not until off prescription pain medicine for at least 24 hours | Breast reduction and breast augmentation |
| [Article on the ASPS website, 2023](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/back-to-active-returning-to-exercise-after-breast-augmentation-recovery) | Many women can drive after the first 7 to 10 days | Breast augmentation |
| [Article on the ASPS website by Dr. Paul Vitenas](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/four-tips-for-a-healthy-recovery-after-a-breast-augmentation) | No driving until you finish all narcotic pain medicine | Breast augmentation |
| [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm) | At least 2 weeks, with full range of motion in your arms first | Cosmetic breast surgery in general: lifts, reductions and augmentation |
| [UW Medicine handout](https://healthonline.washington.edu/document/health_online/pdf/Breast-Augmentation.pdf) | No driving for the first 24 hours, and someone else takes you home | Breast augmentation |

Driving is one part of the [breast augmentation recovery timeline](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#recovery). The rest of this guide helps you decide whether you are ready.

## Can I drive home on the day of surgery?

No. A UW Medicine handout says you will not be able to drive yourself, and that for 24 hours after surgery you should not drive or travel alone, because of the medicine you were given. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation) says to arrange for someone to drive you home and to stay with you the first night at least. Sort this out before surgery day, including how you will get to your first follow-up visit.

## Can I drive 3 days after breast augmentation?

Usually not. None of the sources we read supports driving at 3 days, so only consider it if your surgeon agrees and you are off opioid pain medicine.

Three days is still early. [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/recovery) says acute pain typically eases after 1 to 5 days. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/aftercare-recovery) says your chest may feel stiff and sore for 2 to 5 days. University of Utah Health says your shoulders may have a limited range of motion in the first couple of days, enough that you may need help getting dressed.

You may read that some women are back at work within 3 to 5 days. A surgeon says this about desk jobs in an [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/what-to-expect-after-your-breast-augmentation-surgery), and the article by Dr. Vitenas says many women are back to work and routine activities in three to five days, while still ruling out driving until you finish narcotic pain medicine. Neither gives a driving date, and sitting at a desk asks far less of your chest and arms than an emergency stop does.

## Can I drive while on pain meds?

Not on opioid pain medicine. MedlinePlus says not to drive if you are taking narcotic pain medicines, and the article by Dr. Vitenas says the same. University of Utah Health adds a margin: you should be off prescription pain medicine for at least 24 hours and able to operate a vehicle safely in traffic.

So count from your last dose, not from your surgery date. If anything else you take makes you drowsy, such as a muscle relaxant, ask your surgeon or pharmacist before you drive. Our [breast augmentation pain timeline](/blog/breast-augmentation-pain-management) explains what to expect from pain in the first days and weeks.

## What mobility do you need to drive after breast surgery?

MedlinePlus says you should have full range of motion in your arms before you start driving again, and warns that turning the wheel and shifting gears may be difficult. Run through this list before your first drive:

- **Medicine:** off prescription pain medicine for at least 24 hours (University of Utah Health).
- **Arms:** you can reach the wheel and raise your arms without pulling at your incisions.
- **Wheel:** you can make quick, full turns in both directions without pain.
- **Mirrors:** you can look over each shoulder without wincing.
- **Brake:** you can stamp on the brake, as in an emergency stop, without pain.
- **Surgeon:** you have asked, and your surgeon has agreed.

The first two and the last come from the sources above. The wheel, mirror and brake checks are our plain reading of what driving asks of you, not results from a study. Try them in a parked car with someone else there.

## Do I have to wear a seat belt after breast augmentation?

Yes, every time, as a driver or a passenger. None of the sources we read sets a special rule for seat belts after this surgery. Keep the shoulder strap across your chest, where it can protect you, rather than slipping it under your arm or behind your back. If it rubs an incision or presses on a sore spot, ask your surgeon what you can do about it, and hold off on driving until it is comfortable.

## Should I start with short trips or long drives?

Short trips. MedlinePlus says to ease into driving slowly. Make the first one a short trip in light traffic and good daylight, with someone in the car who can take over.

Long drives are a separate question, because of sitting still. MedlinePlus says [sitting still for a long time can make you more likely to get a DVT](https://medlineplus.gov/deepveinthrombosis.html), a blood clot in a deep vein, and The Aesthetic Society says it is very important to walk a few minutes every few hours to reduce the risk of clots in your legs. On a long drive, whether you are driving or riding, plan stops to get out and walk.

If you came to Miami from another state, no source we read gives a number of days before a long drive or flight home after breast augmentation. For patients of Dr. Victoria Karlinsky, MD, FACS, the answer is personal: Dr. Karlinsky tells you, in writing, how many nights to stay in Miami before you fly home. Ask her the same question if you plan to go home by car.

## When can I drive after a breast lift or breast reduction?

The same checks apply, and your surgeon may ask you to wait longer. University of Utah Health's one-week line is on a page about both reduction and augmentation, and the 2 weeks from MedlinePlus covers lifts and reductions too. If you had a [breast lift](/procedures/breast-lift-miami) with your implants or a [breast reduction](/procedures/breast-reduction-miami), ask for your own date instead of borrowing one from an augmentation guide.

## When should I call my surgeon?

Some soreness when you move your arms is expected. Other signs mean you should stop and call, whether or not you have been driving.

| Usually normal | Call your surgeon |
| --- | --- |
| Soreness in your chest when you move your arms (a surgeon quoted in an article on the ASPS website) | Sudden swelling of a breast (MedlinePlus) |
| Tightness, and skin that feels sensitive to the touch (The Aesthetic Society) | Severe pain your medicine doesn't help (UW Medicine) |
| Stiffness for the first days (The Aesthetic Society) | Stitches that open, or pus from an incision (Cleveland Clinic). Fever or chills, using the number your surgeon gave you |

For chest pain or trouble breathing, go to the emergency room. Call right away for leg swelling, pain or warmth, which [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/16911-deep-vein-thrombosis-dvt) and MedlinePlus list among the signs of a blood clot.

For what comes after driving, see [when you can work out again](/blog/when-can-you-work-out-after-breast-augmentation) and [what to expect at 5 weeks post-op](/weeks-post-op-breast-augmentation-what-to-expect). If you are still planning surgery, the [breast augmentation FAQ](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#faq) is a good next stop.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I drive 7 days after breast augmentation?

Often, yes, if you pass the checks above and your surgeon agrees. University of Utah Health says most people are ready within one week, and an article on the ASPS website says many women can drive after the first 7 to 10 days. MedlinePlus would have you wait until at least 2 weeks. If you are still on opioid pain medicine at 7 days, wait.

### Can I drive after 2 weeks?

At 2 weeks you have passed the longest wait we found, and the same checks still apply: off prescription pain medicine, full range of motion in your arms, and your surgeon's OK. If you still can't turn the wheel or brake hard without pain at this point, wait, and tell your surgeon.

### What does the American Society of Plastic Surgeons say about driving after breast augmentation?

The society's recovery page gives no driving date. It says to resume exercise and normal activity according to your surgeon's directions. Articles on its website go further: one says many women can drive after the first 7 to 10 days, and another says no driving until you finish narcotic pain medicine. Those are bylined articles rather than society guidance.

### Does it matter if my implants are under the muscle?

We found no source that gives a different driving date by placement. A surgeon quoted in an article on the ASPS website says most women feel pressure on their chest, especially when the implant was placed under the muscle. So judge by the checks and not the calendar, and see [over or under the muscle, compared](/blog/breast-implants-over-or-under-the-muscle) if you are still choosing.

### When can I go back to work?

Often before you can drive, so plan a ride. The Aesthetic Society says you may return within a few days to a week, depending on your job. Cleveland Clinic says to take at least 3 days off, and at least 3 weeks for a labor-intensive job. University of Utah Health says most people are ready to drive and return to work within one week.

### Can I ride as a passenger in the first week?

Yes, and you will need to on surgery day. Wear your seat belt, keep the trip short if you can, and take your pain medicine as prescribed, since it is only the driver who has to be off it. On a longer ride, stop to walk a few minutes every few hours, as The Aesthetic Society advises for lowering clot risk.
