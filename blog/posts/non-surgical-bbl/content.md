## Is there a non-surgical BBL that works?

Not in the way most people hope. A surgical BBL moves your own fat into your buttocks, and no injection has FDA approval to do that job: the [FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/dermal-filler-dos-and-donts-wrinkles-lips-and-more) it "has not approved injectable silicone or any injectable fillers for body contouring or enhancement." Glute training can build muscle, and padded shapewear changes your outline while you wear it, but neither adds the fat volume a BBL adds.

"Non-surgical BBL" is a sales term, not a defined procedure. We found no page from the FDA, the American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS) or The Aesthetic Society that defines it. It can mean filler, Sculptra, silicone or a device treatment, so the first question to ask is what exactly is being offered.

| Option | What to know |
| --- | --- |
| Butt fillers | The FDA recommends against them to increase the size of the buttocks |
| Injectable silicone | Not approved for any aesthetic procedure, the FDA says |
| Sculptra | Off-label on the buttocks, with small studies |
| Glute training | Builds muscle, not fat volume |
| Padded shapewear | Changes your outline only while you wear it |

## What does the FDA say about butt fillers?

The FDA recommends against them. Its [guide to dermal fillers](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/aesthetic-cosmetic-devices/dermal-fillers-soft-tissue-fillers) says "The FDA recommends against using dermal fillers or any injectable filler for body contouring and enhancement" and lists increasing the size of the buttocks among those uses. Its consumer guide is blunter: "Do not get any type of filler or liquid silicone injected for body contouring."

The reason is harm. The FDA says using injectable filler for large-scale body contouring or enhancement "can lead to serious injury, including long-term pain, infection, permanent scarring or disfigurement, and even death."

Injectable silicone is a separate concern. The FDA says it "is not approved for any aesthetic procedure including facial and body contouring or enhancement," and that silicone injections can lead to "long term pain, infections, and serious injuries, such as scarring" and "permanent disfigurement, embolism (blockage of a blood vessel), stroke, and death." [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/butt-lift/detailed-procedure-information) calls silicone injections "an unsafe and unacceptable option for buttock augmentation."

Two more warnings apply even when someone tells you the product is safe:

- **The label may not be true.** In 2017 the [FDA warned](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-warns-about-illegal-use-injectable-silicone-body-contouring-and-associated-health-risks) about "products being falsely marketed as FDA-approved dermal fillers."
- **The injector may not be licensed.** The same warning says "Silicone injections for body contouring are often performed by unlicensed and non-medical practitioners."

For any filler, the FDA also says "Do not purchase dermal filler products online, because they could be counterfeit products," and that having filler injected "should be considered a medical procedure, not a cosmetic treatment."

## Does Sculptra work as a non-surgical BBL?

Some small studies report good results, but the use is off-label, the evidence is limited and the FDA's recommendation still stands. Sculptra is poly-L-lactic acid. An [ASPS article](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/the-rising-popularity-of-non-hyaluronic-acid-fillers-what-you-should-know-about-these-biostimulators) counts it among non-hyaluronic acid fillers, and a plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS calls it "a collagen stimulator rather than a volumizer in the traditional sense" and says it is being used more often for buttock augmentation.

The FDA documents we read describe facial uses. Its [2004 approval](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpma/pma.cfm?id=P030050) was for facial fat loss in people with HIV, and its [2009 letter](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf3/P030050S002a.pdf) covers "shallow to deep nasolabial fold contour deficiencies and other facial wrinkles." A [2023 technique paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37361362/) describes gluteal augmentation as "off-label."

Here is what the published work looks like:

- **A 60-patient review.** A [2020 retrospective review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32976171/) of patients treated with 1 to 3 sessions, 4 to 6 weeks apart, concluded the product was "safe and effective," with results tied to "at least 20 vials." Looking back at past patients can't show what would have happened without treatment.
- **A literature review.** A [2020 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31188150/) reached a similar conclusion, and warned injectors to "avoid injecting deeply in the danger triangle to prevent intravascular injection into the gluteal vessels."
- **The technique paper.** It says outcomes "have, to date, been subjective clinical observations," and several of its authors disclose ties to Galderma, the company behind Sculptra.
- **Randomized trials.** We found none that tested Sculptra to enlarge the buttocks. The two small ones we found looked at [hip dips in 15 women](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39503574/) and [cellulite and skin laxity in 20 women](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36826378/).

So if someone offers you Sculptra for your buttocks, you are weighing small off-label studies against an FDA recommendation. Ask to see the sealed product, ask how many vials the plan needs, and ask what the injector does if a complication happens.

## Can exercise or a device replace a BBL?

Exercise can change the muscle, but it does a different job. In a [2023 study in Frontiers in Physiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37877099/), untrained college-aged participants trained under supervision for 9 weeks (15 to 17 sessions), and the title sums up the finding: "Hip thrust and back squat training elicit similar gluteus muscle hypertrophy." That is real muscle growth with no needle. It doesn't place fat above the muscle or take fat from your waist, so the shape it gives differs from a BBL.

Devices are harder to judge. Muscle-stimulating and heat-based treatments are marketed for the buttocks, but we found no FDA or society guidance saying they add the volume a BBL adds. The one FDA clearance we opened for this kind of device, [BTL-899](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf19/K192224.pdf), is indicated for "Non-invasive lipolysis (breakdown of fat) of the abdomen." Before you pay, ask to see the clearance for the area being treated.

For vacuum or cupping "butt lifts," our search turned up clinic marketing and no clinical study. That doesn't prove they do nothing. It does mean there is no good evidence to rely on.

## Is a surgical BBL the safer choice?

We can't tell you that. We found no study that directly compares injections with a surgical BBL, and a BBL is surgery with a small but serious risk. The most serious is a fat embolism: fat injected into or below the gluteal muscle can enter a blood vessel, which can be fatal.

A [2026 meta-analysis](https://doi.org/10.1097/PRS.0000000000012437) of 38 studies and 22,151 patients found minor complications in 3.58% of BBL patients, including uneven contours in 2.29% and seroma, a pocket of fluid, in 2.03%. Pulmonary embolism, a blood clot in the lungs, was reported separately, at 0.04%. The difference from the injections above is that a BBL uses your own fat, and surgical societies have published guidance on where it should go. The BBL page explains [what a BBL's most serious risk is and how fat placement affects it](/procedures/brazilian-butt-lift-bbl-miami#safety).

A BBL also takes time. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23308-brazilian-butt-lift) puts full recovery at 2 to 3 months and notes that it can take up to 6 months. Not all the fat stays: [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/buttock-enhancement/procedure) says patients retain 60% to 80% of the transferred fat on average, in some cases more and in others less. Our [week-by-week BBL recovery guide](/how-long-to-recover-from-bbl) shows what those months look like.

Surgery isn't right for everyone. If you want to know whether it could suit you, start with [who is a candidate for a BBL](/procedures/brazilian-butt-lift-bbl-miami#candidate). Only an exam can tell you, and your surgeon decides at consultation.

## What if I've already had butt injections?

Keep a record of what you were told was injected, when and by whom, and share it with your doctor, even if you feel fine. If you don't know what the product was, say so.

- **Breathing trouble, chest pain or an unusual heartbeat.** Seek medical attention immediately.
- **Unusual pain or vision changes.** The FDA says to "Seek immediate medical attention," and lists "a white, gray, or blue appearance of skin near the injection site" as another reason.
- **Redness, warmth, swelling that keeps growing, or fever.** These can be signs of infection. Call a doctor the same day.

If a filler caused a problem, the FDA asks you to "report the problem to the FDA." You can do that yourself, whether or not the injector does.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do butt injections last?

We found no reliable long-term figure, and the answer depends on what was injected, which is part of the problem. The longest follow-up we read is the 60-patient Sculptra review, which followed patients for 2 years. Anyone quoting a longer figure should be able to name the product and show you the study behind it.

### How much does a non-surgical BBL cost?

We found no price from the FDA, ASPS or The Aesthetic Society, so we don't quote one. Cost also depends on what is injected: the 60-patient Sculptra review used 2 to 12 vials per session, "based on the patient budget." A low price tells you nothing about what is in the syringe, so ask to see the sealed product first.

### Can you sit after bum filler?

Follow the instructions of whoever treated you. We found no FDA or society guidance on sitting after buttock filler, because the FDA recommends against the treatment itself. If you have unusual pain, a change in skin color near the injection site, or fever, get medical care rather than waiting to see how sitting feels.

### Are butt injections illegal?

It depends on what is injected and who injects it, and this isn't legal advice. The FDA titled its 2017 warning "FDA warns about illegal use of injectable silicone for body contouring and associated health risks." Separately, the FDA recommends against any filler for this use. You can check a provider's license with your state's health department before you agree to anything.

### Can filler fix dents after a BBL?

The FDA recommends against filler for body contouring, including the buttocks, and we found no exception for dents. Dents need an exam first, because the cause decides the fix. Our guide on [how dents after a BBL are fixed](/how-to-fix-dents-after-bbl) explains what the evidence covers and what to ask your surgeon.

### Is there a BBL without fat transfer?

Not strictly, because a BBL is fat transfer. The surgical option that doesn't use your fat is butt implants. Cleveland Clinic says that if you don't have enough excess body fat available, "you may be a good candidate for butt implants." Our [comparison of BBL and other body procedures](/blog/tummy-tuck-vs-bbl-miami) covers how they differ.
