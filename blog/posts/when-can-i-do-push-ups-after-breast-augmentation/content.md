## When can I do push-ups after breast augmentation?

Wait at least about six weeks before attempting push-ups, the floor one article on the ASPS website sets for upper-body movement, and then only with your surgeon's clearance, because your timeline depends on your healing and implant placement.

## Why Avoid Push-Ups After Surgery?

While push-ups might seem like a straightforward exercise, they load your chest muscles and the surrounding tissues during the early healing phase. [An article on the ASPS website by Dr. John Cook](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/will-breast-implants-get-in-the-way-of-exercise) says to avoid chest and shoulder exercises, lifting weights or any other exercise that puts pressure on your chest or back. [An article on the ASPS website by Ariel Frankeny](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/back-to-active-returning-to-exercise-after-breast-augmentation-recovery) says strenuous activities and upper body movements should be avoided for at least six weeks. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm), writing about cosmetic breast surgery in general, gives another reason to go slowly: exertion can increase blood pressure and lead to bleeding.

[ASPS says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/procedure) the implant goes either under the pectoral muscle (a submuscular placement) or directly behind the breast tissue, over the pectoral muscle (a subglandular placement). When you do a push-up, the pectoralis major contracts. Dr. Huang-Begovic gives her reason for caution: with implants under the muscle, she writes, contraction places downward and outward pressure on them, which over time can result in the implants dropping or becoming displaced. That is one surgeon's view, and the evidence here is thin. We found no study that measured whether push-ups move an implant during recovery.

![Side-view illustration of submuscular and subglandular implant placement, with an inset of a push-up showing chest muscle contraction and potential implant displacement in early recovery.](IMAGE:displacement)

[A randomized trial of 225 women at one institution (Basile and Oliveira, 2022)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34936606/) adds some perspective: starting exercise 1 week after surgery did not change complication rates, 7.5% in the control group and 6.9% with exercise. The abstract does not say whether the exercise included chest work or push-ups. It is one trial, and it is not a reason to skip your surgeon's clearance.

If anything hurts when you try, stop and tell your surgeon.

## What Exercises Are Safe Early On?

These restrictions don't mean you'll be sedentary during your breast augmentation recovery. Dr. Huang-Begovic's article says patients should start light walking immediately after surgery, which is good for circulation and helps prevent blood clots.

During the first six weeks, you can gradually add light activity with your surgeon's approval, while avoiding strenuous exercise. Focus on lower body movements and short walks, wearing a supportive bra for comfort and protection.

Always check with your surgeon before increasing activity levels, as individual recovery varies.

## How Do I Restart Chest Workouts?

Once your surgeon clears you, at the six-week mark or later, you can begin reintroducing chest exercises step by step.

Start with modified push-ups to reduce strain. Wall push-ups are the gentlest introduction, followed by incline push-ups and then knee push-ups as your strength returns. This order is common practice rather than something tested in a study, so ask your surgeon how fast to move through it.

![Step-by-step card titled Push-Up Progression After Clearance, with a clothed figure in a loose long-sleeve top and full-length leggings: 1 wall push-ups, 2 incline push-ups, 3 knee push-ups, 4 standard push-ups. A note says stop if you feel pain or swelling, and a footer says this is common practice, not from a study, and to ask your surgeon first.](IMAGE:progression)

These modifications let you rebuild strength gradually. If your implants are under the muscle, tell your surgeon how much chest work you want to do. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/detailed-procedure-information) says that with this placement the implant may move when you flex your chest muscles.

Stop immediately if you notice discomfort, swelling or unusual sensations. Checking with your surgeon before resuming any strenuous exercise gives you guidance based on your own recovery. If you had a breast reduction or lift rather than implants, the timing is its own question: see [push-ups and exercise after a breast reduction](/blog/breast-reduction-exercise-timeline-miami) or [recovery after a breast lift](/procedures/breast-lift-miami).

## Do Push-Ups Affect Implants Long-Term? Exercises to Avoid With Breast Implants

No source we read lists exercises to avoid with breast implants for the long term. Dr. Cook's article says that in most cases patients are healed and able to resume all forms of exercise within six weeks, and Dr. Huang-Begovic's says the same.

What can last is animation, when the implant moves or the breast changes shape as you flex your chest. It is linked to under-the-muscle placement. [A 2019 systematic review of four studies (Dyrberg and colleagues)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30685936/) says its incidence has yet to be established, and evidence is scarce. [A 2009 study from one surgeon's patients (Spear and colleagues)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19052809/) says patients for whom exercise is central to their daily living may be better candidates for placement over the muscle, though 82% of those who answered its survey described mild to no distortion, and nearly all patients in the study would still choose placement under the muscle. So if push-ups and chest work are a big part of your life, raise it before surgery, as [implant placement](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#placement) is a decision you make once. Our comparison of [implants over or under the muscle](/blog/breast-implants-over-or-under-the-muscle) goes further.

Always wear a supportive sports bra during push-ups to limit movement. Watch for unusual discomfort, swelling or changes in implant position, which warrant a call to your surgeon. [The FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/things-consider-getting-breast-implants) that if you notice any abnormal changes in your breasts or implants, you need to see your surgeon or health care provider promptly.

### What are implant displacement exercises?

They are a different thing from push-ups: moving the implant by hand within its pocket, usually taught alongside breast massage. [A 2017 literature review (Sood and colleagues)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29348783/) calls these techniques controversial and concluded that the available data do not support massage to prevent capsular contracture. Do them only if your surgeon prescribes them. [Our breast massage guide](/how-often-to-massage-breast-after-augmentation) has the detail.

## Frequently asked questions

### When can I safely resume push-ups after breast augmentation surgery?

Start with modified push-ups once your surgeon clears you, which depends on your healing and implant placement. No source gives a single date: one article on the ASPS website says upper-body movement waits at least six weeks, and another says patients can resume all exercises at six weeks. Always get clearance from your surgeon first.

### Why should I avoid push-ups right after breast augmentation?

Push-ups load your chest muscles while your tissues are healing. One article on the ASPS website advises avoiding strenuous chest exercise such as push-ups, especially with implants under the muscle, and MedlinePlus says exertion can raise blood pressure and lead to bleeding. We found no study measuring whether push-ups move an implant, so it is a precaution.

### What exercises are safe in the early recovery phase after breast augmentation?

An article on the ASPS website says to start light walking immediately after surgery, which helps circulation and helps prevent blood clots. Gentle lower body movement can follow with your surgeon's approval. Strenuous activities and upper body exercises, including push-ups, wait until your surgeon clears you to resume them.

### How do I gradually restart chest workouts after breast augmentation?

After your surgeon gives the okay, start with wall push-ups, then progress to incline and knee push-ups, and eventually standard push-ups. This order is common practice, not something tested in a study. Watch for any pain, swelling or discomfort, and stop immediately if you notice these symptoms.

### Does the placement of my breast implants affect my recovery timeline for exercise?

It may. A surgeon quoted in an article on the ASPS website says the first week is the most uncomfortable, and that most women feel pressure on their chest, especially if the implant was placed under the muscle. No source we read sets different exercise dates for each placement, so ask your surgeon. Tell them early if chest training is central to your routine.

### Can doing push-ups after breast augmentation affect my implants long-term?

No source we read shows long-term harm from push-ups once you are healed. With implants under the muscle, The Aesthetic Society says the implant may move when you flex your chest muscles. Wear a supportive sports bra, watch for discomfort or changes in implant position, and talk to your surgeon if you notice any.

### What are the signs that I should stop exercising after breast augmentation?

If you notice discomfort, swelling, unusual sensations or changes in implant position during exercise, stop immediately and contact your surgeon. These may mean your tissues haven't fully healed or that something needs attention. Sudden swelling on one side, or a new firmness with pain, needs a call the same day.

### Is it necessary to consult my surgeon before resuming exercise after breast augmentation?

Yes. It is essential to get your surgeon's approval before restarting any upper body or strenuous exercise. Every patient heals differently, and the published marks are guides, not rules. Your surgeon can give you personal guidance based on how your incisions and implants are healing, so ask at your follow-up visit.
