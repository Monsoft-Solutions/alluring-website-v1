## How long do you wear a compression garment after a BBL?

Plan on about two months, with the first month the strictest. [A plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/six-things-you-need-to-know-about-recovering-from-a-brazilian-butt-lift) tells patients to wear it day and night, except for showering, in the first month, then at least 12 hours a day in the second, usually overnight. That is one surgeon's schedule, not a society rule, and the one your own surgeon gives you comes first.

| When | How much to wear it | Who says it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Month 1 | Day and night, except showering, or as your surgeon directs | A plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS |
| Month 2 | At least 12 hours a day, or as your surgeon directs | The same surgeon |
| After month 2 | No schedule in the sources we read. Ask your surgeon | None |

## How good is the evidence on how long to wear it?

Thin. A [2023 review in Plastic and Reconstructive Surgery Global Open](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10519563/) found "limited high-quality evidence supporting the efficacy of postoperative compression garments." For body contouring, it says "no RCTs or comparative studies have been conducted." The review doesn't discuss gluteal fat grafting at all, so it speaks to a BBL only by analogy.

That is why the numbers you see online disagree. Here is what each source we read says:

| Source | Schedule | Written about |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS, 2022 | Day and night in month 1, at least 12 hours a day in month 2, or as your surgeon directs | BBL |
| [ASPS article by Kamran Azad, MD](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/recovering-from-a-brazilian-butt-lift), 2016 | Eight weeks | BBL |
| [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/butt-lift/aftercare-recovery) | Six to eight weeks | Butt lifts generally |
| A plastic surgeon quoted by ASPS, [2025 article](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/keep-it-snatched-why-compression-garments-are-your-best-friends-in-recovery) | Day and night for at least six weeks, then part time if needed | Plastic surgery generally |
| [ASPS blog by Daniel Maman, MD](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/the-importance-of-compression-after-plastic-surgery), 2023 | Day and night for one to three weeks, then at night for several weeks | Plastic surgery generally |

They agree on the outline: the most wear comes first, then it tapers. None of them shows what stopping a week early does to a BBL result. We found no study that measured it. So don't treat any schedule as proven, and don't stop on your own either. Ask.

## What does the garment do after a BBL?

It is there mainly for swelling, comfort and the skin over the areas that had liposuction. The Azad article says the garment helps to minimize swelling, decrease discomfort and help the skin retract, and Dr. Maman writes that constant pressure helps prevent a build-up of fluid under the skin and can minimize bruising.

Where it presses is worth asking about. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23308-brazilian-butt-lift) describes a garment around the areas that were liposuctioned. The Azad article says the garment often has the rear end cut out. The Aesthetic Society, writing about butt lifts as a group, describes tight garments that keep the buttock area compressed. Those aren't the same instruction, so ask your surgeon whether yours should press on the grafted area.

## How should a BBL compression garment fit?

Snug, not painful. In the 2025 ASPS article, a surgeon says you want it to "feel snug against the skin while still allowing for some movement," and that it "should never feel like it is cutting off your circulation." Dr. Maman says it shouldn't be so tight that it is uncomfortable or restricts breathing.

Tighter isn't better. The 2023 review cites reports that excessive or poorly distributed compression could cause venous stasis and thrombosis, which means sluggish blood flow and clots. So:

- **Too tight.** Numbness, tingling, skin color changes or deep pain mean the garment may be cutting off circulation, and they can also be signs of something else. Call your surgeon right away, and if you can't reach them, seek medical care.
- **Too loose.** Dr. Maman says to talk to your surgeon about a smaller size if the garment gets loose as swelling goes down.
- **Bunching.** Smooth out folds and ridges each time you put it back on. That is common sense rather than evidence.
- **Extra layers.** Don't add a second garment or waist trainer for more squeeze unless your surgeon asks you to.

## How do you wash a BBL compression garment?

Change it and wash it about once a day if you can. In [ASPS's article on BBL smell](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/is-bbl-smell-real-heres-what-you-need-to-know), Dr. Michael Keyes says garments "should be changed and washed regularly, ideally every 24 hours." He names moisture, heat and friction under a garment worn too long as a cause of bacteria on the skin, and suggests clean, breathable liners to absorb moisture. Our [post on BBL odor](/why-do-bbl-stink) covers the rest.

For the wash itself, the 2025 ASPS article says:

1. **Hand wash if you can.** The article prefers it, though you may need help in the first days.
2. **If you use a machine,** choose cold water and a gentle cycle, and use a delicates or garment bag.
3. **Never machine dry.** Heat can ruin the elastic, so always air dry.

## Is a faja the same as a compression garment?

In everyday speech, usually yes. Faja is the Spanish word for a girdle, and many patients use it for any garment worn after surgery. None of the sources we read compares them. What matters is the job: Dr. Maman describes compression garments as medical-grade clothing.

So a faja your surgeon gave you or approved is fine to call a faja. If you bought one yourself, or a friend passed one along, bring it to your next visit before you switch. The same goes for shapewear.

## Which symptoms need urgent care?

Trouble breathing, chest pain or an unusual heartbeat is an emergency, not a fit problem. [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/buttock-enhancement/recovery) says that if you have shortness of breath, chest pains or unusual heartbeats, seek medical attention immediately. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/17400-pulmonary-embolism) lists these among the signs of a blood clot in the lungs and says to call 911. Don't assume the garment is the cause.

![Card titled When it isn't about the garment. Trouble breathing, chest pain or an unusual heartbeat: seek medical attention immediately, call 911. Leg pain, swelling or warmth: seek medical care right away. Fever above 101°F, spreading redness or discharge: call your surgeon the same day. Numbness, tingling or skin color changes: call your surgeon right away. Sources: ASPS, Cleveland Clinic.](IMAGE:urgent)

| What you notice | What it can mean | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trouble breathing, chest pain or an unusual heartbeat | A clot or fat in the lungs | Seek medical attention immediately. Call 911 |
| Coughing up blood or fainting | A clot in the lungs | Seek medical attention immediately. Call 911 |
| Leg pain, swelling or warmth | A clot in the leg | Seek medical care right away |
| Fever above 101°F, spreading redness or discharge | An infection | Call your surgeon the same day |
| Numbness, tingling or skin color changes | The garment may be cutting off circulation, or something else | Call your surgeon right away. If you can't reach them, seek medical care |
| Odor from an incision | Can be a sign of infection | Call your surgeon the same day |
| A rash under the garment | Moisture and bacteria, or an infection | Wash more often, and call if it doesn't clear |

The infection signs come from [Cleveland Clinic's page on surgical wound infection](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/surgical-wound-infection), which says they typically develop three to seven days after surgery.

Ask four things: which garment you should wear, whether your quote includes it, how long you wear it, and whether it should press on the grafted area. Write the answers down, because your surgeon may change the plan at follow-up visits. ## Frequently asked questions

### When do I switch to a stage 2 garment after a BBL?

When your surgeon says. In a 2025 ASPS article, one surgeon describes a firmer stage 1 garment right after surgery and a less firm stage 2 later. Another puts stage 1 at 1 to 2 weeks and stage 2 at 2 to 6 weeks. The article says there are no firm guidelines, and it doesn't discuss BBLs.

### Do I sleep in my compression garment?

In the first month, usually yes: the surgeon interviewed by ASPS advises wearing it day and night, except for showering. In the second month, the same surgeon advises at least 12 hours a day and says patients can usually do that at night. Sleep on your stomach or side, as Cleveland Clinic advises. Your surgeon can change this schedule.

### How long do I wear compression socks after a BBL?

Ask at your first follow-up, and don't stop on your own. No BBL source we read sets a time. Cleveland Clinic's general guidance on preventing clots after surgery says stockings are generally worn until you are back to your baseline activity, and that the time depends on your medications, leg exam, history and clot risk.

### How many compression garments do I need?

At least two, so you have something to wear while the other is washed and dries. Dr. Michael Keyes recommends a backup garment in ASPS's article on BBL smell. You may also need a smaller size later, because a garment can get loose as swelling goes down. Ask your surgeon before you buy.

### Can I shower with my compression garment on?

No. Take it off to shower and put it back on when your skin is dry. Showering is the one exception in the schedule given by the surgeon interviewed by ASPS, and Dr. Daniel Maman writes that a garment should only be removed to bathe or shower. Ask your surgeon when you can start showering.

### What happens if I don't wear my compression garment after a BBL?

No study we found measured this after a BBL, and a 2023 review says no comparative studies exist for body contouring. An ASPS blog says a garment helps limit swelling, bruising and discomfort, so you may simply feel worse without it. If you can't tolerate yours, call your surgeon rather than stopping.
