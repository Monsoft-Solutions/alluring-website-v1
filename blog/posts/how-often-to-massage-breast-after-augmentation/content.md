## How often should you massage your breasts after augmentation?

There is no standard number: it's whatever your surgeon prescribes, if your surgeon prescribes it at all. Surgeons themselves disagree. In a 2015 survey of 1,067 surgeons, cited in a [2017 literature review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5749369/), 61.5% still recommended massage after surgery, which means many did not.

If nobody gave you massage instructions, you have not missed a step. Don't start on your own. An [article on the ASPS website by Dr. Kevin Tehrani](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-is-capsular-contracture-and-how-can-it-be-treated) puts it plainly: you should never disturb the tissue of your breasts while they are healing without first getting your surgeon's permission.

## When can I get a back massage after breast augmentation?

Ask your surgeon at about 3 to 4 weeks at the earliest. No medical source we read gives a date for a back massage, so this is reasoning and not a rule. A massage table usually means lying face down, which presses on your chest. A [UW Medicine patient handout](https://healthonline.washington.edu/document/health_online/pdf/Breast-Augmentation.pdf) says you may sleep on your side or stomach after 3 to 4 weeks, and [University of Utah Health](https://healthcare.utah.edu/plastic-surgery/breast/recovery) says not to sleep on your stomach until your surgeon says it's okay. If you aren't cleared to [lie on your side or front](/when-can-i-sleep-on-my-side-after-breast-augmentation) at night, you aren't ready for the table.

When your surgeon says yes:

- **Tell the therapist you have implants.** [Sientra's patient labeling](https://sientra.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MDC-0010-R6-Sientra-Patient-Educational-Brochure-Augmentation-1.pdf) for its silicone implants names massage therapists among the people who should know, because they could damage an implant by accident.
- **Keep pressure off your chest.** The same labeling says to guard against any strong or repeated pressure on your breasts. A seated or side-lying position avoids the face-down problem; that part is common sense, not a study.
- **Say where your incisions are.** It's fine to ask the therapist to stay away from them.

## What kinds of massage are there after breast augmentation?

Four different things get called massage after this surgery, and the answer is different for each one.

| Type | What it means | When | What the evidence says |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Implant massage | Pressing or moving the implant in its pocket at home | Only if prescribed; your surgeon sets the start | A 2017 review: the data don't support it for preventing contracture |
| Scar massage | Rubbing a healed incision with gel, cream or lotion | After stitches are out and the incision has healed, if your doctor says so | Sientra's labeling: keeps skin from drying out and may feel better |
| Back or spa massage | An ordinary massage | Ask from 3 to 4 weeks at the earliest | No medical source gives a date |
| Lymphatic massage | Light massage meant to move swelling | Ask first | We found no trial and no society or hospital guidance |

## Why do some surgeons prescribe implant massage?

Because they hope to keep the space around the implant open and soft. Your body forms a capsule of scar tissue around any implant. [Capsular contracture](/blog/capsular-contracture) is when that tissue tightens, and the FDA describes it as [the hardening of the breast around the implant](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/risks-and-complications-breast-implants), which can be painful.

Massage is meant to work against that, but surgeons don't even agree on the method. The 2017 review says some recommend displacing the implant to maintain the pocket, while others use compression to flatten it. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm), whose page covers cosmetic breast surgery in general, lifts and reductions included, says your surgeon may show you breast-massaging techniques and to do them at home if they were recommended. [Allergan's labeling for Natrelle saline implants](https://allergan-web-cdn-prod.azureedge.net/actavis/actavis/media/allergan-pdf-documents/labeling/natrelleus/salineimplants/m711rev10_web.pdf) says something similar. Neither gives a schedule.

One term causes confusion. "Implant displacement" can mean this massage technique, or it can mean an implant shifting out of place, which is the worry behind [implant displacement and push-ups](/when-can-i-do-push-ups-after-breast-augmentation).

## Does massage prevent capsular contracture?

The evidence doesn't show that it does. The [2017 literature review (Sood and colleagues)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29348783/) found 4 relevant studies with 587 patients in total. Average contracture was 31% in the massage groups and 40% without massage, which the authors call similar. Their conclusion: "The available data do not support breast massage to prevent capsular contracture." The two most alike studies in the review reached opposite results.

The Tehrani article on the ASPS website agrees that massage has not been proven effective by any major studies. You may still read that you must massage daily for life. The review doesn't back that.

Other factors show up more clearly than massage. A [2025 meta-analysis (Haas and colleagues)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39989707/) linked lower contracture rates to [placing the implant under the muscle](/blog/breast-implants-over-or-under-the-muscle) and to textured implants. Surface is a trade-off, though: of the BIA-ALCL cases reported to the [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/medical-device-reports-breast-implant-associated-anaplastic-large-cell-lymphoma) through June 2024, 73% involved textured implants and 3% smooth ones, with the surface not reported for the rest. Those are reports, not a rate, and most of the smooth-implant reports involve a prior textured implant or an unknown implant history. [How smooth and textured implants differ](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#implants) is a conversation for before surgery, not something massage changes afterward.

For scale, severe contracture (Baker grade III or IV) within 10 years of a first augmentation was [12.1% in Mentor's study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33620918/) and 13% in Sientra's. Each number covers that maker's own implants, in a study it sponsored.

## When can I start breast massage after surgery?

When your surgeon tells you to, and not before. In the studies the 2017 review looked at, massage began anywhere from 2 days to 2 weeks after surgery. That describes what those researchers did. It is not a recommended start date.

Sientra's labeling also warns that your doctor may have special directions about avoiding activities that compress or put pressure on your breasts during the first weeks. Massage of the incision itself comes after your stitches are out and the incision has healed, the same labeling says. Ask at your first follow-up visit whether massage is part of your plan. If the answer is no, that is a complete answer.

## How often should you massage breast implants if it's prescribed?

As often as your surgeon says. In the 2017 review the massage was performed twice daily and lasted 2 to 5 minutes for each breast, but those are study routines rather than a protocol to copy, and the review says more studies with standardized techniques are needed. No source we read says how many weeks or months to keep going, so ask when you can stop too.

## How do you massage breast implants?

Your surgeon has to show you, in person. We don't describe a technique here because the sources don't agree on one, and a written routine can't account for your incisions, placement or implant type.

Questions to bring to your follow-up:

- **Do you want me to massage at all?** Ask with your implant and placement in mind.
- **When do I start and stop?** Get both dates, and how many times a day.
- **Will you show me how?** Ask how firm the pressure should be.
- **What should make me stop?** Know which signs mean you should call.

One firm limit: don't let anyone squeeze a hard breast to loosen it. The FDA lists closed capsulotomy, which means manually squeezing the breast to break the capsule, among procedures not recommended for saline implants because they are known to cause rupture. It also names intense physical pressure among the possible causes of rupture for implants in general.

## When should you call your surgeon about a firm or painful breast?

Call when something changes or gets worse, rather than trying to massage it away. Tightness early on is expected. The [Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/aftercare-recovery) says your breasts may feel tight and sensitive to the touch after surgery.

| Usually expected | Call your surgeon |
| --- | --- |
| Tightness and sensitivity in the first days | New hardness, pain or a change in shape, at any time |
| Soreness that eases each day | Pain that gets worse or that your medicine doesn't help |
| Both sides feeling slightly different as they heal | Sudden swelling, especially on one side |
| Implants sitting high at first | Redness, heat, drainage or fever |

Sientra's labeling lists breast pain, hardness or abnormal shape as signs of capsular contracture. The FDA says to see your surgeon promptly for any abnormal changes in your breasts or implants. A new lump or swelling years after surgery also needs a visit, and our guide to [breast implant illness and BIA-ALCL](/blog/breast-implant-illness-symptoms) explains why.

If you are still deciding about surgery, our page on [breast implant safety](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#safety) lays out the risks in one place, and you can [book a consultation](/free-consultation) with Dr. Victoria Karlinsky, MD, FACS. If your surgery was elsewhere, your own surgeon's instructions come first.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens if you don't massage your breast implants?

If your surgeon didn't prescribe massage, the 2017 review found no clear benefit from it. Contracture rates across 4 studies were called similar: 31% on average with massage and 40% without. Those studies weren't built to show what skipping it does. If your surgeon did prescribe it, don't stop on your own. Ask first.

### Should I massage my breasts after augmentation if nobody told me to?

No. Wait for instructions. An article on the ASPS website by Dr. Kevin Tehrani says you should never disturb your breast tissue while it is healing without your surgeon's permission. If you have questions about whether massage suits you, bring them to your next follow-up visit, or call the office if the visit is a long way off.

### Do textured implants need massage?

None of the sources we read sets a rule by implant surface, so ask your surgeon instead of assuming. The FDA says you receive a device card after surgery with your implant's style, size and serial or lot number, which tells your surgeon or anyone else exactly what you have. Advice written for someone else's implants may not apply to yours.

### Can lymphatic massage help after breast augmentation?

We can't tell you that it does. A PubMed search on lymphatic massage and breast augmentation returned no studies when we ran it, and no society or hospital page we read gives guidance. That doesn't prove it's useless or harmful, only that it hasn't been tested after this surgery. Ask your surgeon before booking, and tell the therapist where your incisions are.

### Can massage help implants drop faster?

Probably not. An [article on the ASPS website by Dr. Eric Mariotti](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-does-drop-and-fluff-mean-after-breast-augmentation) says there is little you can do to speed up the [drop and fluff process](/blog/breast-implants-drop-fluff-timeline), which it says can take 3 to 6 months. University of Utah Health says you begin to notice final results around three months, with subtle changes through 12 months. Massage for this purpose is only for those whose surgeon asks for it.

### Can massage fix capsular contracture?

No source shows that it can. If a breast has become firm or painful, see your surgeon instead of working on it yourself. The FDA says grades III and IV are considered severe and may require another operation, and that it has not cleared or approved any device to treat capsular contracture or reduce how often it happens.

### Can you get a massage with breast implants years later?

Nothing we read rules it out once you have healed. Sientra's labeling says you should be able to carry on normal activities after healing, while avoiding situations that put a lot of pressure on your breasts. Tell the therapist about your implants, skip firm pressure on your chest, and mention any tenderness or new change to your surgeon.
