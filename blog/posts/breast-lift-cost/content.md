## How much does a breast lift cost?

The average cost from [the American Society of Plastic Surgeons](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/cost) (ASPS) is $6,816, which does not include anesthesia, operating room facilities or other related expenses. ASPS itself calls it "only part of the total price," so it's a starting point, not what you will pay.

We found no average for the all-in price. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/associated-costs) publishes its own average, which counts surgeon and facility fees and may leave out anesthesia, medical tests, prescriptions and garments, so the two societies' numbers measure different things. Prices vary by region and practice, and we found no reliable source for regional price ranges, so we don't quote any here.

## Why can't you compare the ASPS average with a clinic's price?

Because they count different things. The ASPS average leaves out anesthesia, operating room facilities and other related expenses, while a clinic's price is for one surgery at one practice and may bundle more lines, or fewer.

| | ASPS average | A clinic's price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | An average from ASPS's latest statistics | A price at one practice |
| Anesthesia | No | Ask |
| Operating room or facility | No | Ask |
| Other related expenses | No | Ask |

The only fair comparison is quote against quote, line by line. That includes our prices: ask us what yours covers.

## What should a breast lift quote spell out?

A useful quote lists every cost, so you can see what's in the price and what isn't. The first six lines below are [ASPS's list](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/cost) of what breast lift costs may include. The last three are questions we'd add.

| Line | What to ask |
| --- | --- |
| Surgeon's fee | Is this the full fee, and who performs the surgery? |
| Anesthesia fees | Which type, and who gives it? |
| Hospital or surgical facility costs | Where is the surgery done, and is the facility in this price? |
| Medical tests | Are lab work and mammograms in this price? |
| Post-surgery garments | Is your support bra in this price? |
| Prescriptions for medication | Are they in this price, or do you pay separately? |
| Implants, if any | Saline or silicone, and are they in this price? |
| Follow-up visits | How many, and are they in this price? |
| Revisions and complications | What happens, and who pays, if you need more care? |

Footer: cost list from the American Society of Plastic Surgeons; also ask about implants, follow-up visits, revisions and complications.](IMAGE:quote)

The medical tests line is easy to miss. [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/preparation) says you may be asked to get lab testing or a medical evaluation, and a baseline mammogram before surgery with another one after.

## What makes a breast lift cost more or less?

Mainly who does it, what's done and where. ASPS says a surgeon's fee is based on experience, the type of procedure and the office's location, and The Aesthetic Society says costs vary by surgeon, geographic area and surgical technique. Beyond that:

- **Implants.** On our list they raise the price, and saline and silicone differ. They also bring [costs later on](/blog/breast-lift-implants-miami).
- **Where it's done.** [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23298-breast-lift) says most breast lifts take place in a surgical center or hospital. Ask how the setting and anesthesia show up on your quote.
- **What else you add.** Other procedures in the same surgery add to the total.

## Why do some breast lift offers cost less?

A lower price can mean the offer counts fewer lines, describes a different surgery or comes from a different location or practice. So the price alone can't tell you why it is lower. Ask.

ASPS says the surgeon's experience and your comfort with them "are just as important as the final cost of the surgery." The Aesthetic Society is blunter: "Choose your surgeon based on quality, training, and experience—not cost." [Cleveland Clinic's surgery center](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/cosmetic-plastic-surgery/procedures/breast-lift) suggests asking about credentials, training and how many procedures a surgeon has performed, and reviewing before and after photos. You can see [our real breast lift photos](/gallery/breast-lift) and read [about your surgeon](/procedures/breast-lift-miami#surgeon).

## Does insurance cover a breast lift?

Usually not. "Most health insurance plans do not cover breast lift surgery or its complications," ASPS says, and The Aesthetic Society says insurance does not cover these costs because a lift is elective cosmetic surgery. That word "complications" matters, so ask your surgeon what happens if you need extra care.

One exception to know about comes after a mastectomy. [The U.S. Department of Labor](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/ebsa/about-ebsa/our-activities/resource-center/publications/your-rights-after-a-mastectomy) says that under the Women's Health and Cancer Rights Act, if your plan covers mastectomies, you're receiving benefits for one and you choose reconstruction, the plan must cover surgery and reconstruction of the other breast "to produce a symmetrical appearance," decided with your doctor and subject to your usual cost-sharing, with exceptions for some church and government plans. That is about symmetry after a mastectomy, not a cosmetic lift, so check with your plan.

## Can you finance a breast lift?

Yes. Financing is available, and [our financing page](/plastic-surgery-financing-miami) explains the options. ASPS notes that many surgeons offer financing plans, so it's worth asking wherever you go.

Read the terms before you sign any plan. [The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-should-i-know-about-medical-credit-cards-and-payment-plans-for-medical-bills-en-1827/) says interest on medical credit cards is often deferred, and that if you pay late or have a balance when the promotional period ends, you may face significant interest and fees. Ask when interest starts, what fees apply and what happens if you miss a payment.

## How do I compare breast lift quotes?

Put each quote in the same format, and then compare the surgeon as well as the total:

1. **Compare the same surgery.** Check the type of lift, and whether each quote has implants.
2. **Go line by line.** Use the table above, and treat a blank line as a question.
3. **Get it in writing.** That includes what's left out.
4. **Ask about the unexpected.** [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/safety) lists possible revisional surgery among the risks, so ask what it would cost you.
5. **Add what sits outside the quote.** Time off work, help at home and later costs all count. If you live in another state, add your own trip, and ask your surgeon how many nights you need to stay nearby before you're cleared to fly home.
6. **Weigh the surgeon, not only the total.** Photos, training and how you feel in the room all matter.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is a breast lift without implants?

On our list, a breast lift without implants is $5,000, and your surgeon confirms your price at consultation. ASPS's average is for "a breast lift (mastopexy)", and its page doesn't separate lifts with and without implants. Any quote still depends on what's included. Our guide explains [who a lift without implants suits](/blog/breast-lift-without-implants).

### How much is a breast lift with implants?

On our list, it is $6,000 with saline implants and $7,000 with silicone implants, and your surgeon confirms your price at consultation. Later costs count too: [the FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/things-consider-getting-breast-implants) says implants aren't lifetime devices, and that screening exams and implant removal or replacement may not be covered by insurance.

### How much does a breast lift and tummy tuck cost?

We don't publish one figure, because it depends on which of each you have. On our list, it is the price of each procedure, with $500 off the total when a lift without implants and a tummy tuck are done in one surgery, and your surgeon confirms your price at consultation. Our [mommy makeover page](/procedures/mommy-makeover-miami) explains more.

### Can I use an HSA or FSA for a breast lift?

It may not qualify. [IRS Publication 502](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p502) says you generally can't count cosmetic surgery as a medical expense, with exceptions for deformities from a congenital abnormality, an accident or trauma, or a disfiguring disease. [Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969) ties HSA spending to medical care and FSA spending to what your plan specifies. Neither addresses a breast lift, so check with your plan or a tax adviser first.

### How much time off should I budget for?

For a desk job, many women go back after about a week, Cleveland Clinic says. But [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/aftercare-recovery) says not to drive for the first 2 weeks, so plan your commute, and let your surgeon clear you. Lifting and exercise may not be cleared for up to 6 weeks, Cleveland Clinic says, and it doesn't address physical jobs. Our [recovery guide](/blog/breast-lift-recovery-guide-miami) has the detail.

### Should I budget for help at home?

Yes, if you'd pay for it. ASPS says someone should drive you home and stay with you for at least the first night after surgery. If you have young children, [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/preparing-your-procedure) advises arranging for someone to care for them for at least the first week.
