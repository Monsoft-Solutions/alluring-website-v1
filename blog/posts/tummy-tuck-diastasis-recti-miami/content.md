## Does a tummy tuck fix diastasis recti?

Usually yes, if the surgery includes muscle repair. Your surgeon stitches the stretched tissue between your stomach muscles so they sit closer together, and removes loose skin and fat in the same operation. Surgery that only removes skin leaves the gap where it is, so the first thing to ask is whether yours includes the repair. Our page explains [how a tummy tuck is done, step by step](/procedures/tummy-tuck-miami#procedure).

The [American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS)](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/tummy-tuck) describes a tummy tuck as surgery that removes excess fat and skin and, in most cases, restores weakened or separated muscles. That is what the surgery usually includes, not how often the gap stays closed, which is covered below. Tomer Avraham, MD, an ASPS member surgeon interviewed in [an article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/diastasis-recti-repair-restoring-core-strength-and-abdominal-integrity), calls the repair "an integral part of a tummy tuck".

What the repair does for strength is less settled. A [2020 systematic review in Cureus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7839807/) covered 7 studies and 497 patients. Physical function improved in all 4 studies that used one standard survey, and strength improved in 2 of the 3 studies that measured it. Its authors call the results encouraging for function and less consistent for strength, and say differences between the studies limit what they can conclude. An older [2011 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21688021/) goes further, saying patients should be told correction "is largely cosmetic".

## How do I know if I have diastasis recti?

Diastasis recti is a gap between the left and right sides of your rectus abdominis, the "six-pack" muscles. It usually forms when the linea alba, the band of tissue between them, stretches during pregnancy and doesn't fully spring back. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/22346-diastasis-recti) says it affects 6 in 10 women after childbirth and counts an abdominal gap wider than 2 centimeters. It lists these common signs:

- A bulge or pooch just above or below your belly button, even after losing your pregnancy weight
- A soft, jelly-like feeling around your belly button
- Coning or doming when you tense your stomach or lean back in a chair
- Low back pain, poor posture or trouble lifting things

The separation itself doesn't hurt, though its side effects can. To check at home, Cleveland Clinic suggests:

1. Lie on your back with your knees bent and your feet flat on the floor.
2. Lift your shoulders slightly off the ground, keeping one hand behind your head.
3. Put your other hand above your belly button, palm down and fingers toward your toes.
4. Feel for a gap between your stomach muscles and count how many fingers fit.

If you feel a gap of two or more finger widths, talk to your healthcare provider, who can confirm it by hand, or with ultrasound, a measuring tape or a caliper.

![Stylized drawing of a clothed woman lying on her back on a mat with her knees bent and shoulders lifted, one hand behind her head and the other above her belly button, with four steps beside her. A gap of two or more finger widths means talk to your healthcare provider.](IMAGE:finger-check)

## Can physical therapy fix diastasis recti, or do I need surgery?

Try physical therapy first. It may only narrow the gap a little, but it can strengthen your core, and [a post by Frank Campanile, MD, on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/what-is-diastasis-recti-and-how-can-a-tummy-tuck-help) says it also sets you up for better results from surgery later.

Sources disagree on how far therapy alone goes. Cleveland Clinic says it's possible to fix diastasis recti without surgery, with a physical therapist who knows the condition. Dr. Avraham says he isn't aware of any nonsurgical technique that can effectively repair it. The trials fall between the two:

- A [2023 review in Physiotherapy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36934466/) of 16 trials and 698 women after childbirth found that, in 6 of those trials, abdominal exercise narrowed the gap by 0.43 centimeters on average, compared with usual care. Its authors say that isn't clinically significant, but that exercise may have other physical and psychosocial benefits.
- In a [2016 trial in Surgery](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27475817/), people with symptoms were randomized between two types of repair and compared with a control group who did a 3-month training program. Strength improved in every group, the surgery groups felt a greater gain, and after training "patients still experience discomfort and pain". The 2020 review's table records 26 of the training group, 81.25%, as dissatisfied and offered surgery, and notes that the training group was followed for 3 months and the surgery groups for 12.

| Start with physical therapy | Talk to a surgeon |
| --- | --- |
| You gave birth recently and the gap may still be closing | Your symptoms remain after working with a physical therapist |
| You may want more children | You have finished having children |
| You are still losing weight | You are at or near a stable weight |
| Your separation is mild | You also have loose skin that exercise can't remove |
| | You have an umbilical hernia |

While you work on it, Cleveland Clinic advises avoiding crunches and sit-ups of any kind, planks and push-ups unless modified, and anything else that makes your stomach bulge, cone or dome. It says the exercises that help involve deep breathing and slow, controlled movements. A belly band can support your back, but it can't heal the gap.

## What happens during diastasis recti surgery?

Most often it is done as part of a tummy tuck. Dr. Avraham describes a low incision across the lower belly, through which the surgeon removes excess skin and fat and uses sutures to reposition and repair the muscles. Cleveland Clinic says a tummy tuck can take anywhere from 1 to 5 hours and is usually an outpatient procedure. Your surgeon tells you how long yours should take.

If you don't have much loose skin, a tummy tuck may not suit you. Dr. Avraham says laparoscopic and robotic techniques repair the gap from inside the abdomen, often along with a hernia repair, and that he does them with a general surgeon. He names a rare risk, injury to structures inside the abdomen, and says that is why a multidisciplinary surgical team matters. Ask a surgeon whether one applies to you.

Weight matters too. Dr. Avraham says the ideal candidate is at or near a healthy or goal weight, because a lot of fat around the organs limits the repair. [Paul Vitenas, MD](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/five-things-to-consider-before-getting-a-tummy-tuck), on the ASPS website, says to be close to your desired weight for 6 to 12 months first. The page sets out [who is a good candidate for a tummy tuck](/procedures/tummy-tuck-miami#candidate).

## Does a mini tummy tuck repair diastasis recti?

Not at Alluring: our mini and extended mini don't include muscle repair, so neither closes the gap. Elsewhere it varies. [Samir Rao, MD](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/understanding-the-different-types-of-tummy-tucks), on the ASPS website, says a mini can strengthen muscles in the lower stomach but improves only the area below the belly button. Cleveland Clinic says diastasis recti can sit above, at or below the belly button, so a lower-belly repair may not reach all of it.

Read [what a tummy tuck without muscle repair can and can't do](/what-is-a-tummy-tuck-without-muscle-repair-called), and see [which tummy tuck you need](/procedures/tummy-tuck-miami#which-tummy-tuck). Your surgeon decides at your exam.

## Does insurance cover diastasis recti surgery?

Usually not, so plan as if it won't. [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/tummy-tuck/cost) says most health insurance plans do not cover tummy tuck surgery or its complications, and no source we read gives a figure for diastasis repair on its own. Dr. Campanile's post says a repair "should be covered, at least in part" if you have health insurance, but ASPS says its blog posts are the authors' views, and that line doesn't match the society's own statement.

Before you count on anything, ask your insurer in writing whether a muscle repair or a hernia repair would be covered for you, and what happens if you have a complication. ## What is recovery like with muscle repair?

Plan on about 2 weeks off work and 6 weeks without lifting. "This is a surgery that requires real recovery," Dr. Avraham says, and he tells women to expect up to 2 weeks out of work. These timings come from [a post by Cindy Wu, MD, on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/answers-to-common-tummy-tuck-questions) and [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/tummy-tuck/aftercare-recovery). Dr. Rao gives an extended tummy tuck longer: about 4 to 6 weeks before returning to work.

| When | What to expect |
| --- | --- |
| Day of surgery | You walk, which helps keep the risk of blood clots in your legs to a minimum (Dr. Wu) |
| 7 to 10 days | You walk bent at the waist, then stand up straight (Dr. Wu) |
| About 2 weeks | Most people return to work (Dr. Wu). Someone else takes charge of small children for at least this long (The Aesthetic Society) |
| 4 to 6 weeks | You wear a compression garment (Dr. Wu), with no heavy lifting such as your children or laundry baskets (The Aesthetic Society) |
| 6 weeks | Running and lifting are avoided until now (Dr. Wu) |
| About 3 months | Swelling keeps going down until about now (Dr. Wu) |

Dr. Avraham says you could feel tightness that should improve over time, and our [guide to tightness after a tummy tuck](/how-to-reduce-tightness-after-tummy-tuck) explains what helps. Your surgeon sets your own schedule, and the full picture is in our [week-by-week recovery guide](/how-long-to-recover-tummy-tuck).

This surgery carries real risk. In an insurance database of 25,478 patients, major complications followed 3.1% of tummy tucks done alone and 3.8% of those combined with liposuction ([Winocour and colleagues, 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26505716/)). Of those major complications, 31.5% were hematomas (bleeding under the skin), 27.2% infections and 20.2% suspected or confirmed blood clots. The 2020 review counted minor problems too and found an overall rate of 17.0%, or 47 of 276 patients, most often fluid under the skin, a wound reopening or minor bleeding. It couldn't say whether adding the repair raises the rate. Read more about [how safe a tummy tuck is](/procedures/tummy-tuck-miami#safety).

## Will diastasis recti come back after surgery?

It can. How often isn't settled: the studies we read range from no recurrence at all to 40 percent, and most are small.

| Study | Who was checked | Repair | Found |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Swedenhammar, 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32299300/) | 52 patients, a median of 5 years later, by clinical exam | Self-retaining sutures or mesh | No recurrence, after one early recurrence was repaired and left out of follow-up |
| [Nahas, 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15861083/) | 12 women, 76 to 84 months later, by CT scan | Nonabsorbable sutures | No recurrence |
| [de Castro, 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23897333/) | 38 patients, 1 or 5 years later, by ultrasound | Plication during a tummy tuck | None of 18 at 5 years, 2 of 20 at 1 year |
| [van Uchelen, 2001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11335840/) | 40 women, 64 months later on average, by ultrasound | Absorbable sutures | Residual or recurrent gap in 40 percent |

The 2020 review looked at strength, function and complications, not at recurrence as such. Its background section says the rate "has been reported as high as 40%", citing that [2011 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21688021/), and warns that gains in strength may be temporary. Its complications table lists one recurrence, in the 2016 trial. The [Swedenhammar study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8551425/) followed the patients from that trial and left out that patient, who had a second repair within 6 weeks. In the de Castro study, only one of the two patients with a recurrence had any complaint. The study that found 40 percent used absorbable sutures, but none of these compared absorbable with nonabsorbable sutures, so we can't say that explains the difference. Ask your surgeon which technique you would have, and how often the gap comes back in their patients.

Pregnancy and weight changes are the other risk. ASPS advises women who may want future pregnancies to postpone a tummy tuck. Dr. Wu says results are stable with stable weight, but that weight changes or pregnancy may change them. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/tummy-tuck/results) says results should be permanent barring pregnancy or significant weight gain, though surgery can't stop normal aging. A [2023 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37266593/) of 17 studies and 237 patients who became pregnant after a tummy tuck found no deaths of mothers or newborns and said pregnancy should not be ruled out. That speaks mainly to safety, over an average follow-up of 8.5 months. The review also logged 96 abdominal wall or appearance problems, and its abstract doesn't say how many involved the repair. A [2024 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38447678/) linked a past tummy tuck to higher odds of preterm delivery. The page answers [whether you can have a baby after a tummy tuck](/procedures/tummy-tuck-miami#recovery).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can exercise close diastasis recti?

Sometimes partly. Cleveland Clinic says mild cases may improve with little effort, while the 2023 review found exercise narrowed the gap by only 0.43 centimeters on average. It can still strengthen your core, so it is worth doing with a physical therapist even if the gap stays.

### How wide is too wide for diastasis recti?

Cleveland Clinic counts a gap wider than 2 centimeters and suggests seeing a provider for more than a two-finger gap or pain. Dr. Campanile says it becomes concerning above 2.7 centimeters at 6 or more weeks after birth. No source we read sets a width that requires surgery, and the 2020 review says studies don't separate results by severity.

### How long after giving birth can I have diastasis recti surgery?

Kristy Hamilton, MD, quoted in [an article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/post-pregnancy-plastic-surgery-what-can-you-do-now-and-what-needs-to-wait), describes waiting at least 6 months after delivery, longer if breastfeeding. Another surgeon in that article recommends waiting up to a full year. Dr. Campanile says roughly half of cases resolve on their own within 12 months, which is one more reason not to rush.

### Does diastasis recti surgery cost more than a tummy tuck?

Usually the repair is part of the tummy tuck rather than a separate surgery, though Dr. Campanile says it adds complexity, cost and recovery time. Our page lists [prices by tummy tuck type](/procedures/tummy-tuck-miami#pricing). The mini and extended mini don't include muscle repair, so your surgeon confirms your type and price at your consultation.

### Can men get diastasis recti?

Yes. Cleveland Clinic says anyone can get it, and that men can develop it from heavy lifting, constipation or sudden weight changes. Dr. Rao says a standard tummy tuck can suit men and women with separated muscles. Most of the research is on women, though: 94.4% of patients in the 2020 review were female.

### Can a tummy tuck repair diastasis recti after Ozempic or major weight loss?

It can, once your weight has settled. The ASPS article lists weight fluctuations as one cause of the gap, and Dr. Avraham says the ideal candidate is at or near a healthy or goal weight. Our [guide to Ozempic and tummy tucks](/blog/ozempic-tummy-tuck-myths-miami) covers the timing, and your surgeon decides at your exam.
