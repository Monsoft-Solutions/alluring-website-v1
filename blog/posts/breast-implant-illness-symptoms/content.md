## What are the symptoms of breast implant illness?

Of the symptoms named in reports to the FDA, the most common are fatigue, joint problems, anxiety, brain fog and hair loss, and they can start anywhere from right after surgery to years later. The [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/medical-device-reports-systemic-symptoms-women-breast-implants) describes them as systemic symptoms, meaning they affect the whole body rather than one breast. Breast implant illness, or BII, is the name patients and clinicians use for this group of symptoms. It is not a formal diagnosis.

The list below comes from the FDA's review of 10,318 device reports filed between January 1, 2008 and June 30, 2024. Read it as what people report, not as your odds: the FDA says the incidence, prevalence or cause of an event can't be determined from these reports alone.

| Symptom term in the report | Share of the 10,318 reports |
| --- | --- |
| Fatigue | 41.1% |
| Joint issues (joints and joint pain) | 30.9% |
| Anxiety | 22.9% |
| Autoimmune diseases (symptoms or a diagnosis) | 22.6% |
| Brain fog ("fog" in the reports) | 22.5% |
| Hair loss | 19.6% |
| Illness | 18.7% |
| Depression | 16.9% |
| Rash | 16.7% |
| Weight (loss, gain, other) | 16.5% |

A note says these are shares of reports, not how often this happens.](IMAGE:fda-reports-chart)

[Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/23366-breast-implant-illness) also lists muscle pain and weakness, memory loss, trouble concentrating, chronic pain, dry eyes and low vision, and says people have reported more than 50 symptoms. The FDA's review found that in the 7,838 reports that gave dates, symptoms began an average of 5.6 years after surgery, with a range of 0 to 42.5 years.

## Is breast implant illness real?

The symptoms are real, and anyone who has them deserves a full evaluation. What isn't established is whether, or how, implants cause them. [The FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/risks-and-complications-breast-implants) says these symptoms "may be associated with breast implants" and that they are poorly understood.

Researchers read the evidence differently. [A 2025 systematic review of 33 studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40788544/) (Ferreira and colleagues) calls BII a real clinical entity with several causes. [A 2025 meta-analysis of 36 articles](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40391348/) (Bouhadana and colleagues) found fatigue, muscle pain or weakness and thinking problems about three times as likely in women with implants, yet opens by saying "no definitive scientific link has been established." And [a 2026 review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42881298/) in Aesthetic Plastic Surgery reports that the only pre-registered controlled cohort study, of 9,590 women, found no difference in BII symptoms between women with and without implants.

| Question | What the sources say |
| --- | --- |
| Is it a diagnosis? | No. The FDA says BII "is not recognized as a formal medical diagnosis." |
| Is there a test? | No specific test. Doctors test to rule out other conditions, such as arthritis or Lyme disease (Cleveland Clinic). |
| Which implants are involved? | All types, whatever the fill, shape or surface (FDA). |
| Who gets it? | Unknown. Experts don't know why some people get it and most others don't (Cleveland Clinic). |

## What causes breast implant illness?

Nobody knows yet. Cleveland Clinic says researchers suspect an autoimmune or inflammatory reaction to parts of the implant, such as silicone, bacteria growing on the implant surface, or a reaction to the surgery itself. The 2025 review by Ferreira and colleagues names immune responses, microbial colonization and psychological factors as hypotheses and says the mechanisms remain unclear. The FDA says it has not detected any association between silicone gel implants and connective tissue disease, breast cancer or reproductive problems.

## How common is breast implant illness?

No one has a reliable number. Cleveland Clinic says it is unclear how common BII is, and the FDA's [example boxed warning](https://www.fda.gov/media/131885/download) says individual patient risk "has not been well established." The FDA's reports can't give a rate, because that system has under-reporting, duplicates and unverified reports. Be careful with any percentage you see online: the figures in published reviews come from selected groups of patients, not from everyone with implants.

## Is it breast implant illness or something else?

Several implant problems have signs of their own, and some need a faster answer than BII does. This table can't diagnose you, but it can tell you what to bring up and how soon.

| What you notice | What it could be | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fatigue, brain fog, joint pain or rash, with no change in the breast | Reported as BII, but many other conditions cause these | See your primary doctor and tell your surgeon |
| A breast getting firmer, tighter or changing shape | [Capsular contracture](/blog/capsular-contracture) | See your surgeon |
| A saline implant losing size, at once or over several days | Saline rupture, called deflation | See your surgeon; removing it takes another operation |
| With silicone: usually nothing, sometimes hard lumps, pain or a shape change | [Silicone rupture](/blog/saline-vs-silicone-breast-implants), which is often silent | Ask about an MRI |
| Persistent swelling, a lump or pain near the implant, often years after surgery | BIA-ALCL needs to be ruled out | See your surgeon promptly |
| Fever, chills, redness, heat or drainage | Infection ([MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm), on cosmetic breast surgery in general; [Sientra's labeling](https://sientra.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MDC-0010-R6-Sientra-Patient-Educational-Brochure-Augmentation-1.pdf)) | Call your surgeon right away |
| Chest pain, trouble breathing or coughing blood | A possible blood clot | Go to the emergency room |

Don't wait on the last row: chest pain, trouble breathing or coughing blood means going to the emergency room.

![Reference card matching implant symptoms to what they could be and what to do. Fatigue, brain fog, joint pain or rash: reported as breast implant illness, see your primary doctor and tell your surgeon. A firmer breast or a shape change: capsular contracture, see your surgeon. Saline implant losing size: deflation, see your surgeon. Persistent swelling, a lump or pain near the implant: BIA-ALCL needs ruling out, see your surgeon promptly. Fever, redness or heat: infection, call right away.](IMAGE:which-problem-card)

Silicone rupture is the one you can't count on noticing. For silicone implants with no symptoms, [the FDA recommends](https://www.fda.gov/media/131885/download) a first ultrasound or MRI 5 to 6 years after surgery, then every 2 to 3 years, and an MRI if you have symptoms at any time. Saline implants have no screening recommendation, because a rupture is noticeable without one. The scans may not be covered by insurance.

## What should you do if you think you have breast implant illness?

Start with a medical evaluation, not a decision about surgery, because other conditions can cause the same symptoms.

1. **Write it down.** List your symptoms, when each one began and what changes them. Your doctor will ask about your symptoms and history first.
2. **Find your device card.** It has your implant's style, size and serial or lot number, which helps any doctor you see.
3. **See your primary care doctor.** No test diagnoses BII, so the point of this visit is to look for other causes. Stay on any treatment you already have.
4. **See your surgeon,** or another surgeon who treats implant problems. An exam looks for rupture, contracture and infection.
5. **Talk through removal only after that.** It is a real option, covered below, but it is surgery with effects of its own.
6. **Report it if you'd like it counted.** [The FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/risks-and-complications-breast-implants) encourages patients to report device-related symptoms online through MedWatch, its safety reporting program.

Having symptoms no one can explain is hard. Cleveland Clinic's advice is to find a provider who listens to your concerns and does a full evaluation. If you feel brushed off, see someone else.

## Does removing the implants help?

Many people feel better after removal, but not everyone, and no one can tell you in advance which group you'll be in. Surgeons usually treat BII by removing the implants, Cleveland Clinic says, and many offer to remove the scar capsule at the same time.

- **A 2025 review of 33 studies (Ferreira and colleagues):** 81.9% of patients reported improvement after removal.
- **[A 2024 systematic review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38636098/) (Kabir and colleagues):** 9 studies reported improvement in 83.5% of patients.
- **Reports to the FDA:** of the 785 reports that gave an outcome, 687 noted improvement and 98 noted no improvement or worsening.

These are selected groups of people who chose surgery, without blinding, so treat the numbers as encouraging and not as a forecast. The FDA's own wording is that "not all patients may experience improvement in their symptoms." Cleveland Clinic says relief may come within the first 30 days. Whether the capsule should come out too is unsettled. The 2026 review, one author's paper rated level V evidence, says no published study directly compares removal with and without capsulectomy, that the most rigorous prospective data show similar symptom improvement either way, and that capsulectomy carries a higher complication rate. It supports capsulectomy for cases such as confirmed BIA-ALCL. Ask your surgeon why they recommend what they do.

Know what removal can leave, too. The FDA says women who don't replace their implants may have dimpling, chest wall concavity, puckering or sagging. If sagging is the worry, see [how a lift and implants compare](/blog/breast-augmentation-vs-breast-lift).

## What is BIA-ALCL, and how is it different?

Unlike BII, BIA-ALCL has a diagnosis, a way to test for it and a defined treatment. It is a type of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma, a cancer of the immune system, and [it is not breast cancer](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/questions-and-answers-about-breast-implant-associated-anaplastic-large-cell-lymphoma-bia-alcl). The FDA considers an individual's risk low, but says it is serious and can lead to death, especially if not treated promptly.

The main symptoms are persistent swelling, a mass or pain near the implant, often years after surgery. [Reports to the FDA through June 2024](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/medical-device-reports-breast-implant-associated-anaplastic-large-cell-lymphoma) count 1,380 cases and 64 deaths worldwide:

- **Surface:** 73% textured, 3% smooth and 24% not specified. Patients with smooth implants have also been diagnosed.
- **How it showed up:** fluid around the implant in 48%, swelling or pain in 27%, capsular contracture in 14%, a lump or mass in 11%. A report can list more than one.
- **Timing:** a median of 8 years from the last implant to diagnosis. [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/patient-safety/breast-implant-safety/bia-alcl-summary) says 8 to 10 years on average.

Those are reports, not rates. Evaluation usually means an exam, imaging and testing the fluid or tissue around the implant. Confirmed cases are treated by removing the implant and the capsule, and ASPS says that when caught early it is curable in most patients. The FDA does not recommend removing implants in people with no symptoms, and the fill, saline or silicone, doesn't appear to be a risk factor. Our breast augmentation page covers [smooth and textured implants](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#implants).

The FDA also knows of [19 cases of squamous cell carcinoma](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/update-reports-squamous-cell-carcinoma-scc-capsule-around-breast-implants-fda-safety-communication) in the capsule from published literature, along with other lymphomas. It says the cause, incidence and risk factors remain unknown, and again it does not recommend removal without symptoms.

## How can you avoid breast implant illness?

There is no proven way. Experts don't know why some people get BII, and Cleveland Clinic points out that one way to eliminate the risk is not to get implants. Because reports cover every type of implant, no implant choice is known to prevent it, and we found no evidence that [implant massage](/how-often-to-massage-breast-after-augmentation) affects it.

If you are deciding, these steps help:

- **Read the boxed warning and checklist.** [The FDA requires](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/things-consider-getting-breast-implants) your provider to review a patient decision checklist with you, and you get the chance to initial and sign it.
- **Raise any autoimmune history.** The FDA's example checklist says autoimmune disease, or a family history of it, has not been adequately studied in people with implants.
- **Ask for the labeling.** You have the right to request the implant's patient labeling before surgery.

If you are still weighing implants, the [risks and safety section of our breast augmentation page](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#safety) covers the FDA's warnings in one place, and a [free consultation](/free-consultation) is a good time to go through the checklist line by line. If you already have implants and symptoms, start with your own doctor.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can silicone implants make you sick?

People report these symptoms with both saline and silicone implants, Cleveland Clinic says. A 2025 meta-analysis found silicone-filled implants appeared more likely to lead to BII-type symptoms, but it pooled patient-reported symptoms and says no definitive link has been established. The FDA says it has not detected any association between silicone gel implants and connective tissue disease, breast cancer or reproductive problems. So the honest answer is possibly, and no one can say how often.

### What are the signs your body is rejecting breast implants?

None of the medical sources we read uses rejection as a diagnosis. Your body normally forms a scar capsule around an implant. What people often mean is capsular contracture, where that capsule tightens, or an infection, which Sientra's labeling describes as redness, heat, swelling, pain, fever or chills. Either one is a reason to call your surgeon.

### How do you tell breast implant illness from an autoimmune disease?

You can't tell on your own, and no single test separates them. Autoimmune diseases appeared in 22.6% of the FDA's reports, and the symptoms overlap. Ask your primary doctor about testing, and about seeing a specialist if results point that way. Tell your surgeon about any personal or family history, because implants haven't been adequately studied in people with autoimmune disease.

### What are the long-term side effects of breast implants?

The FDA lists capsular contracture, rupture, breast pain, infection, changes in sensation, implant malposition and wrinkling among common local complications, and says implants are not lifetime devices. In Sientra's study, 24% of women had another operation within 10 years of a first augmentation; in Mentor's, 25.5%. Those are each maker's own studies. See [how long breast implants last](/blog/how-long-do-breast-implants-last).

### How common is BIA-ALCL?

The FDA gives no rate and says rates "are not well defined," though it considers individual risk low. ASPS estimates lifetime risk for women with textured implants at 1:2,207 to 1:86,029, based on confirmed cases and sales data, and a range that wide shows how uncertain the estimate is. Reports to the FDA through June 2024 count 1,380 cases worldwide.

### Will insurance pay to remove implants because of breast implant illness?

Don't assume it. The FDA says some insurance companies do not cover implant removal or replacement, even with complications and even if the first surgery was covered. Ask your insurer in writing what it would cover before you schedule anything.

### Should I have my implants removed if I feel fine?

Nothing we read recommends removal as prevention. For BIA-ALCL and the other capsule cancers, the FDA says it does not recommend removing implants in people with no symptoms. Keep your follow-up visits, follow the FDA's screening schedule if your implants are silicone, and report any new change promptly. You can still choose removal for personal reasons; talk it through with your surgeon.
