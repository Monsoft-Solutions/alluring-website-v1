## Am I a candidate for breast augmentation?

[ASPS's list](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/candidates) is short: healthy, breasts fully developed, realistic expectations, not pregnant or breastfeeding. It adds the usual reasons women come in: breasts that feel too small, shape and volume lost after pregnancy, weight loss or aging, or breasts that are uneven.

The quiz below turns all of that into twelve yes-or-no questions. It can't tell you whether surgery is right for you, because your surgeon decides at consultation. It can show you what to sort out first and what to ask. Our breast augmentation page has the short version of [who breast augmentation suits](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#candidate).

## Should I get a boob job? Take the quiz

Answer yes or no, and keep a count as you go.

### Part 1: Is now the right time?

Any yes here means wait for now.

1. **Are you pregnant or breastfeeding?** The [FDA's example patient checklist](https://www.fda.gov/media/131885/download) says you aren't a candidate while pregnant or nursing, and ASPS's list starts with not being pregnant or breastfeeding.
2. **Do you have an active infection anywhere in your body, or a breast cancer or pre-cancer that hasn't been adequately treated?** The FDA's example checklist says you aren't a candidate while either applies.
3. **Are you under 18, under 22 and set on silicone, or are your breasts still developing?** These are the FDA's approved ages, and ASPS's list asks for fully developed breasts.

### Part 2: Health and timing

Give yourself 1 point for each yes.

4. **Do you smoke, or have you smoked in the past?** The FDA's example checklist says active and former smokers may be at higher risk of a poor surgical outcome.
5. **Do you have a condition or take a medicine that affects healing or blood clotting?** That checklist names diabetes and connective tissue disorders among others, and says autoimmune disease hasn't been adequately studied.
6. **Did you give birth or stop breastfeeding in the past few months?** Surgeons usually want your breasts to settle first.
7. **Is your weight still changing?** Surgeons want it stable first. BMI is a conversation too, not a pass or fail: see the guide in our [BMI calculator for breast surgery](/bmi-calculator#breast-augmentation) and the BMI question below.
8. **Would it be hard to take time off and have help at home?** [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation) says at least 3 days off work, at least 3 weeks for a labor-intensive job, and someone with you the first night at least.

### Part 3: Goals and expectations

Give yourself 1 point for each yes.

9. **Without a bra, do your nipples sit below the breast crease or point downward?** Those are signs [ASPS lists for a lift](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/candidates). Only an exam can tell how much they matter for you.
10. **Are you doing this mainly because someone else wants you to?** ASPS says it's important that you're doing it for yourself.
11. **Are you counting on a perfect or permanent result?** ASPS asks for realistic expectations, and implants aren't lifetime devices.
12. **Would another surgery someday, or long-term check-ups, be a deal-breaker?** [The FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/things-consider-getting-breast-implants) you should assume you will need additional surgeries.

## What does your quiz score mean?

| Your answers | What it suggests | What to do next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Any yes in Part 1 | Not right now, and often that's temporary | Come back once it's behind you |
| 0 points | On paper, you fit ASPS's description | Read on, then book a consultation |
| 1 to 3 points | Common. These are things to plan around | Bring your yeses to your consultation |
| 4 or more points | Several things to sort out first | Ask which ones time could change |
| Yes to question 9, at any score | Worth checking whether implants alone would give you the shape you want | Ask your surgeon whether a lift would help |

## How old do you have to be to get breast implants?

At least 18 for saline and 22 for silicone, under the [FDA's approval for augmentation](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/types-breast-implants). Those are the FDA's ages, not a surgeon's policy, so ask yours. Am I too old? Not by age alone: neither the FDA's implant page nor ASPS's candidate list sets an upper age, and no source we read names an ideal one.

[ASPS's 2025 statistics](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/documents/news/statistics/2025/plastic-surgery-statistics-report-2025.pdf) count implant placements in every age group, and 36 to 45 is the largest. The counts include revisions, so some of the older women are replacing implants and not getting a first set.

## How long should you wait after pregnancy or breastfeeding?

No official rule exists, but surgeons writing on the ASPS website generally say at least 3 to 6 months after you stop breastfeeding and at least 6 months after giving birth. They don't fully agree, so here's who said what:

| Surgeon, in an article on the ASPS website | What they advise |
| --- | --- |
| [Dr. Kristy Hamilton](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/post-pregnancy-plastic-surgery-what-can-you-do-now-and-what-needs-to-wait) | A minimum of six months after giving birth, longer if breastfeeding, and at least three months after weaning before breast surgery |
| Dr. Karen Horton, same article | It can take four to six months after stopping breastfeeding for the breasts to settle in size and shape, and she suggests waiting up to a year after delivery |
| [Dr. Shahram Salemy](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/better-breasts-after-pregnancy-and-breastfeeding) | Up to six months after lactation has stopped |

What about more children? Dr. Salemy says it's recommended to wait until you don't plan to become pregnant anymore, but that women who may have future children can, in certain cases, still have surgery safely. It's a question of how long the result holds: ASPS says pregnancy may influence how augmented breasts look, and [Allergan's labeling for Natrelle saline implants](https://allergan-web-cdn-prod.azureedge.net/actavis/actavis/media/allergan-pdf-documents/labeling/natrelleus/salineimplants/m711rev10_web.pdf) says implants won't prevent sagging after pregnancy.

## Can you get breast implants after weight loss?

Yes, once your weight has been stable for a while. ASPS says augmentation can restore volume lost after weight reduction. In an [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/glp-1-and-breast-surgery-how-weight-loss-medications-are-reshaping-aesthetic-options), Dr. Matthew Kilgo typically recommends at least 3 to 6 months of documented stability, while Dr. Elise Min says there isn't one magic number of months for every patient. Whether you lost weight through diet, surgery or a GLP-1 medicine, the same questions apply, and your surgeon needs to know about any medicine you take.

Loose skin is the catch. "An implant adds volume," Dr. Min says. "It does not remove excess skin or reliably reposition a breast that has descended." She adds that a larger implant used just to fill loose skin can sometimes leave a breast that's bigger but still sits too low. Your implants, once placed, stay the same size if you lose more weight, [Dr. John Cook writes](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/will-breast-implants-get-in-the-way-of-exercise), so a stable weight comes first.

## Do implants fix sagging, or do you need a lift?

Implants add volume, and a lift raises the breast. [ASPS says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation) augmentation does not correct severely drooping breasts and that a lift may be needed along with it. If you answered yes to question 9, read our [breast augmentation vs breast lift](/blog/breast-augmentation-vs-breast-lift) guide or the page on a [breast lift](/procedures/breast-lift-miami). ASPS says both can often be done in one operation or may need separate ones, and [Sientra's labeling](https://sientra.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MDC-0010-R6-Sientra-Patient-Educational-Brochure-Augmentation-1.pdf) says combining them might cause more scarring and may lengthen recovery.

## What size breast implants should I get?

No quiz can pick your size, but our [breast implant size calculator](/blog/breast-implant-size-guide) gives a starting estimate from your band and cup. In ASPS's 2025 survey, surgeons' ranking put 300 to 400 cc first and 200 to 300 cc second, and 36 percent saw patients trending smaller. That's what surgeons use, not a recommendation for you.

Some practices offer bra sizers or 3D simulation; ask whether yours does. It's worth taking slowly: [the FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/what-know-about-breast-implants) patients can request more surgeries to change the size or shape of their breasts, and in Sientra's core study over 10 years, a change of size or style was the most common reason implants came out, at 49.1% of implants removed. Our breast augmentation page has more on [choosing implant size](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#size).

## What do implants ask of you over the years?

Some upkeep, and possibly another operation. The FDA says implants aren't lifetime devices and that their life can't be predicted, as our post on [how long breast implants last](/blog/how-long-do-breast-implants-last) explains. Its example checklist says as many as 20% of women who get implants for augmentation have them removed within 8 to 10 years, a figure the FDA calls illustrative. For silicone implants with no symptoms, the FDA recommends a first ultrasound or MRI at 5 to 6 years, then every 2 to 3 years, while saline implants have no screening recommendation.

Every implant's labeling also carries a boxed warning covering these points, plus a cancer of the immune system called BIA-ALCL and the symptoms some women report as [breast implant illness](/blog/breast-implant-illness-symptoms). Before surgery, your provider must review a patient decision checklist with you. Sientra's labeling suggests waiting 1 to 2 weeks after reading its brochure before you decide. Our page on [breast implant safety](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#safety) has the details.

## What happens at a breast augmentation consultation?

Your surgeon examines you, goes through your medical history and tells you what's realistic. Bring this checklist:

- **Your quiz answers,** especially every yes.
- **Your history:** medicines, past surgeries, any scars that healed badly, and smoking.
- **Your plans:** more children, weight goals, and how much time you can take off.
- **Your questions:** [saline or silicone](/blog/saline-vs-silicone-breast-implants), [placement over or under the muscle](/blog/breast-implants-over-or-under-the-muscle), incision, size, recovery and cost. You can also ask for the implant's patient labeling, which [the FDA says](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/breast-implant-surgery) you have the right to request.

## Frequently asked questions

### What BMI do you need for breast augmentation?

We found no society with a firm cutoff. Our calculator uses under 30. One 2026 study of [6,515 patients](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41741917/) found complications rising from a BMI of 25.2: in the first 30 days, 4.3% at that level or above and 1.3% below it (Knoedler and colleagues). That's one database study, and your surgeon decides case by case.

### Is breast augmentation OK if I'm very active?

Generally, yes, once you've recovered. In an article on the ASPS website, Dr. John Cook says that in most cases patients are healed and able to resume all forms of exercise within six weeks. Cleveland Clinic says returning to sports may take up to 6 weeks. Tell your surgeon what you do before you choose implant placement.

### Can I breastfeed after breast augmentation?

Often, but it can be harder. A [2023 review of 11 studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36752943/) found about 82% of women with implants breastfed, against about 88% without, and the review says there may be impairment in ability to breastfeed. The FDA says some women can and some cannot. If you plan to breastfeed, tell your surgeon before you choose an incision or placement, and read our post on [breastfeeding with implants](/blog/mammograms-breastfeeding-with-breast-implants).

### Can I combine breast augmentation with a mommy makeover?

Often, and your surgeon decides. A [mommy makeover](/procedures/mommy-makeover-miami) combines breast and body procedures. Sientra's labeling says combined surgery can mean more scarring and a longer recovery, and cautions that doing several procedures at once isn't always the right call. Ask whether one surgery or two stages fits your health and your time off.

### What recovery should I plan for?

The Aesthetic Society says it typically takes 1 to 2 weeks to recover, and Sientra's labeling says most usual activities return by then if you have no complications. Cleveland Clinic says full recovery takes about 6 to 8 weeks on average. Implants take 3 to 6 months to settle, an article on the ASPS website says. Our [week-by-week recovery timeline](/blog/breast-augmentation-recovery-miami) lays out driving, sleep, exercise and swimming.

### Does insurance cover breast augmentation?

Usually not when it's cosmetic. Cleveland Clinic says that in most cases it's considered elective cosmetic surgery, so insurance won't cover the procedure or follow-up visits, and may not cover implant removal later. Reconstruction can be different. You can see [saline and silicone prices](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#pricing) on our breast augmentation page, and [financing is offered](/plastic-surgery-financing-miami).
