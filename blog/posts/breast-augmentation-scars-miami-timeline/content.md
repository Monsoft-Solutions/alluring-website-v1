## Do breast augmentation scars go away?

Not completely, but they fade a lot. [University of Utah Health](https://healthcare.utah.edu/plastic-surgery/breast/recovery), writing about breast surgery scars, describes thin, slightly discolored lines after about a year. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/11030-scars), writing about scars in general, says a scar may fade so much that you can barely see it, but it never completely goes away.

Where your scar sits is settled before surgery. How it heals depends partly on your genes, and partly on things you can control, mainly sun and nicotine. We use diagrams on this page, not scar photos, because every scar heals differently. Real before-and-after photos are in our [breast augmentation gallery](/gallery/breast-augmentation).

## Where are breast augmentation scars? The three incision types

A boob job scar sits in one of three places: the crease under the breast, the edge of the areola or the armpit. ASPS lists these three [incision options](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/procedure) and says the choice depends on the type of implant, how much enlargement you want, your anatomy and what you and your surgeon prefer. You can see how they fit with implant position on our page about [where the incision goes](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#placement).

| Incision | Where the scar sits | What the sources say |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inframammary | The crease where the breast meets the body | [Sientra's patient labeling](https://sientra.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/MDC-0010-R6-Sientra-Patient-Educational-Brochure-Augmentation-1.pdf) calls it the most common incision |
| Periareolar | Around the edge of the areola | A [2018 meta-analysis of 7 studies](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28916908/) linked it to more [capsular contracture](/blog/capsular-contracture) than the crease incision (odds ratio 1.91), with no significant difference from the armpit incision. A [small 2006 study of 33 patients](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16404252/) suggested less sensory loss in the lower breast than the crease incision |
| Transaxillary | In the armpit, off the breast itself | A [2026 meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41628597/) found no significant difference from other incisions for contracture, another operation or infection. [One study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27484988/) found armpit scars were longer than crease scars |
| Belly button (transumbilical) | The navel | Not recommended. ASPS links it to a higher complication rate, Sientra's labeling says not to use it for its implants, and [the FDA](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/breast-implants/risks-and-complications-breast-implants) lists an incision site it hasn't approved, such as the belly button, among the causes of rupture |

Those comparisons come from observational studies, so they can't prove an incision caused the difference. No trial compares how all three scars look. Two cohort studies, both in Chinese patients, compare armpit with crease: [one of 163 women](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30607574/) found armpit scars scored worse at 1 and 6 months, and total scores and satisfaction evened out by 12 months. [The other, of 78 women](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27484988/) seen at least a year later, found overall scores and satisfaction equal, with more pigmentation in crease scars.

Your implant can change the length of the incision as well. [Allergan's labeling for Natrelle saline implants](https://allergan-web-cdn-prod.azureedge.net/actavis/actavis/media/allergan-pdf-documents/labeling/natrelleus/salineimplants/m711rev10_web.pdf) says saline implants typically need a smaller incision, and [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/implants) says gummy bear implants need a slightly longer one. Our guide to [saline vs silicone implants](/blog/saline-vs-silicone-breast-implants) has the rest of that choice. Adding a lift adds scars: Sientra's labeling says it might cause more scarring than implants alone, and we have a separate post on [breast lift scars](/blog/breast-lift-scars-miami).

A note says the belly-button route is not recommended.](IMAGE:incision-sites)

## Breast augmentation scar healing stages

New scars start as thin pink or red lines and fade over months. We found no study that tracks breast augmentation scars week by week, so this table gives only what the sources state. The closest is the study of 163 women above: on a scar scale where lower is better, armpit scars went from a median of 6 at 1 month to 4 at 6 months and 2 at 12 months, and crease scars went from 4 to 3 to 2. Your own timing will differ.

| When | What's typical | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First 1 to 2 weeks | External stitches come out at about a week; glue or tape falls off in a week or two | [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/aftercare-recovery) |
| First weeks and months | Thin, pink or red lines, swollen for a while | [Article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/how-to-maintain-minimal-scarring-after-breast-augmentation) by Dr. Daniel Maman; University of Utah Health |
| Several months to over a year | Healing and fading continue | [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm), on cosmetic breast surgery in general; Dr. Maman |
| About 1 year | Thin, slightly discolored lines | University of Utah Health |
| Up to a couple of years | Still fading in some women | [ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/results) |
| Long term | Faded, but it may always be noticeable | University of Utah Health |

On lighter skin a new scar is usually pink or red, and in people with dark skin scars often appear as dark spots, Cleveland Clinic says. So "fading" looks different from one woman to the next. Our [week-by-week recovery timeline](/blog/breast-augmentation-recovery-miami) puts this next to everything else that's happening.

![Vertical timeline card titled How a Breast Augmentation Scar Fades, with five stages. First 1 to 2 weeks: outer stitches come out, tape or glue falls off. First weeks and months: thin pink or red line, swollen for a while. About 1 year: thin, slightly discolored line. Up to a couple of years: still fading in some women. Long term: it fades but never completely goes away. Sources: The Aesthetic Society, University of Utah Health, ASPS and Cleveland Clinic.](IMAGE:scar-timeline)

## Why do some women scar more than others?

Genetics, nicotine and infection all play a part, ASPS says, and only the last two are partly in your hands.

Keloids are the scars worth raising before surgery. Cleveland Clinic describes them as raised scars that grow and extend beyond the injured area, and says people with dark skin, especially of African, Asian or Hispanic heritage, and red-haired people are more likely to develop them. If you've had one before, you're more likely to get another, so tell your surgeon. [The FDA](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/what-know-about-breast-implants) also suggests telling your surgeon if an earlier surgery left more scar tissue than expected.

## How do you protect new scars from the sun in Florida?

Keep them covered or, once the incision is fully closed, use SPF 30 or higher whenever you're outside. Dr. Maman's article says UV rays can permanently darken fresh scars, and that new scar tissue stays very sensitive to sunlight even after you feel fully healed. MedlinePlus and an [article on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/considering-breast-augmentation-for-beach-season-heres-when-you-should-plan-your-surgery) by Dr. Cindy Wu both name SPF 30 or higher, and Sientra's labeling says to avoid sun exposure for the first month. As practical advice, not a study finding, keep sunscreen off any incision that's still open, and ask your surgeon when to start.

For how long? No study sets an end date. Because scars keep maturing for a year or more, we'd keep this up for at least the first year. That's our suggestion, not a study finding.

In South Florida the sun doesn't take a season off, so this is a daily habit and not just a beach-day one:

- **Armpit scars.** Sleeveless tops expose them, so they need the most attention.
- **Crease and areola scars.** Clothing usually covers these, but a swimsuit that shifts or a low neckline can leave them in the sun.
- **Beach days.** Covering up counts as much as sunscreen. Water has its own rule: no ocean or pool until incisions are fully closed and your surgeon agrees, as our post on [swimming after breast augmentation](/how-long-after-breast-augmentation-can-i-swim) explains.

## How to minimize breast augmentation scars: what the evidence says

Sun protection and avoiding nicotine have the clearest reasoning behind them. Silicone products are reasonable to try if your surgeon suggests them, but the evidence is weak. Here's what each has behind it:

| What you can do | What the evidence says | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protect from sun | Dr. Maman and Cleveland Clinic both say sun can darken scars; we found no controlled trial on breast scars | SPF 30 or higher, or cover up |
| Silicone sheets or gel | A [2013 Cochrane review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24030657/) of 20 trials and 873 people found weak evidence, in poor-quality research, for people prone to bad scarring. We found no good trial on breast augmentation scars | Try it if your surgeon suggests it, once the incision is closed |
| Scar massage | Dr. Wu suggests massaging with a silicone-based gel, and Sientra's labeling says your doctor may suggest it after the incision has healed. We found no set schedule | Ask when and how |
| Not smoking | ASPS links nicotine to scar quality, and [its guidance to surgeons](https://plasticsurgery.org/documents/health-policy/resources/2024-managing-risks-of-smoking.pdf) advises stopping weeks before surgery | Your surgeon sets the date |
| Not stretching the incision | MedlinePlus says to limit activity so you don't stretch your incisions | Follow your lifting and arm limits |

Scar massage and implant massage are different things. One works on the skin of the scar, the other moves the implant, and our post on [massage after breast augmentation](/how-often-to-massage-breast-after-augmentation) covers the second. For the tape and stitches themselves, see [when breast augmentation stitches dissolve](/when-do-breast-augmentation-stitches-dissolve).

Silicone sheets or gel, weak evidence for abnormal scars in a Cochrane review of 20 trials and 873 people, worth asking about. Scar massage, common advice with no set schedule, ask your surgeon. Not smoking, nicotine affects scar quality.](IMAGE:evidence-card)

## Normal scar, or see your surgeon?

A new scar can look rough for a while, and that alone isn't a reason to worry. Call about anything in the last column.

| What you see | Usually normal | Call or see your surgeon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Color | Pink or red at first, or dark spots on darker skin | Spreading redness or heat around the incision |
| Texture | A little swollen for a while, sometimes itchy | A scar growing thick, raised or spreading past the incision |
| Incision | Closed, with tape or glue coming off on its own | Pus, bad-smelling drainage, or stitches that open |
| Over time | Slowly fading | Staying red, dark or itchy for months |

[Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation) lists discharge such as pus and rupture of your stitches as reasons to contact your provider immediately. Dr. Maman's article names thick, raised scars and scars that stay red, dark or itchy for months as reasons for a follow-up, and says silicone sheets, steroid injections or laser therapy may help. Our page on [breast implant safety](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#safety) covers the wider risks.

See your surgeon: pus or bad-smelling drainage, stitches that open, spreading redness or heat, a scar growing thick, raised or spreading. ## What to ask about scars at your consultation

Scar questions are worth asking before anyone picks an incision:

- Which incision would you suggest for my implant and my body, and why?
- Where exactly will the scar sit when I'm standing, and when I raise my arms?
- I have a history of thick scars or keloids. Does that change your plan?
- What scar care do you want me to do, and from when?

## Frequently asked questions

### How long do breast implant scars take to heal?

Longer than the incision itself. Sientra's labeling says wounds take several weeks or more to heal completely, and the scar keeps changing after that. MedlinePlus says incision scars may take several months to more than a year to fade, and ASPS says it can take even a couple of years. Everyone's timing differs, and your genes play a part.

### Can you get breast implants without scars?

No. Placing an implant takes an incision, so there's always a scar somewhere. What you can choose, with your surgeon, is where it sits. The armpit route keeps it off the breast itself, and the other two sit in the crease or at the edge of the areola. Every scar fades, and none disappears entirely.

### Which incision leaves the least noticeable scar?

No trial compares all three. Two cohort studies in Chinese patients compared armpit with crease: one found armpit scars scored worse early, and in both, overall scores and satisfaction were equal by a year or more. Neither looked at the areola incision. University of Utah Health, on breast surgery scars, says location can help conceal scars, but that they may always be noticeable.

### Do armpit scars from breast augmentation show?

They can. An armpit incision keeps the scar off the breast, but sleeveless tops and raised arms can show it. Sientra's labeling says this route gives the surgeon easier access to the chest muscle. A 2026 meta-analysis found no significant difference from other incisions for contracture, another operation or infection. How it looks is something to ask your surgeon about.

### Can breast augmentation scars be removed?

Not completely. Cleveland Clinic says treatments can reduce a scar's size or appearance, but the scar will never completely go away. Options it lists include injections, laser treatments and scar-revision surgery, which exchanges one scar for a different, more preferable one. Start with the surgeon who did your surgery, because some scars keep improving on their own for a year or more.

### When can I start silicone sheets or scar cream?

After your incision has healed and your surgeon agrees. Sientra's labeling says that after your stitches are removed and your incision has healed, your doctor may tell you to massage the site with a gel, cream or lotion. We found no source that gives a set start date for silicone sheets after breast augmentation, so go by your surgeon's instructions.
