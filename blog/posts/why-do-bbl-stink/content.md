## What is BBL smell?

BBL smell is the name social media gave to an odor some people notice while recovering from a Brazilian butt lift (BBL). Dr. Michael Keyes, a plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS for [its article on BBL smell](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/is-bbl-smell-real-heres-what-you-need-to-know), says it "is not a common or expected outcome of a BBL when performed under sterile conditions with proper postoperative care." He places the cause in recovery, not the operating room: "The BBL smell is not due to the surgery itself, but rather a consequence of the postoperative recovery."

When odor does happen, the causes are mostly ordinary: moisture, heat and friction under a compression garment, fluid draining from the small liposuction incisions, and cleaning that is harder when you can't move easily. Dr. Keyes adds that reports on social media "can sometimes exaggerate or misattribute postoperative experiences." A smell needs your surgeon's attention when it comes from an incision, or comes with pus, spreading redness or a fever, because that can be an infection.

## What causes BBL smell after surgery?

Most BBL odor comes from moisture: drainage, sweat and a garment that stays damp. Less often it is a sign of a complication.

![Card titled What causes BBL smell. Most BBL odor comes from moisture: drainage from the liposuction incisions, a garment that traps heat and sweat, hard-to-reach hygiene. Less often it is a sign of a complication: infection, an infected seroma, fat necrosis, rarely. Sources named in the footer.](IMAGE:causes)

- **Drainage from the liposuction incisions.** During liposuction, surgeons inject fluid, and some of it can seep back out afterward. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/body/liposuction/aftercare-recovery) says incisions "may be leaky or drain fluids for several days." Dr. Keyes links less drainage to "less opportunity for bacterial overgrowth or garment saturation."
- **A garment that traps heat and sweat.** [A plastic surgeon interviewed by ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/articles/six-things-you-need-to-know-about-recovering-from-a-brazilian-butt-lift) advises wearing compression day and night for the first month, or as your surgeon directs. Dr. Keyes names bacterial overgrowth on the skin "due to moisture, heat and friction, especially if patients wear the same garment for long periods without cleaning it."
- **Hard-to-reach hygiene.** [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23308-brazilian-butt-lift) says you can't sit or lie on your buttocks for at least 2 weeks, and that can make cleaning up after using the bathroom harder than usual. Dr. Keyes says it directly: "Poor hygiene during recovery, such as not wiping well, can occur due to mobility limitations." That is a practical problem with practical fixes, listed below.
- **Infection.** A noticeable odor from an incision is one of the signs of a surgical wound infection, along with thick, cloudy discharge, redness and fever, according to [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/surgical-wound-infection).
- **An infected seroma.** A seroma is a pocket of fluid under the skin. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/seroma) explains that it can collect bacteria and turn into an abscess, and says to call your surgeon if the fluid looks like pus: "thick, milky-white to yellow and stinky."
- **Fat necrosis, rarely.** When some grafted fat doesn't survive, it can form a lump or an oil cyst. Dr. Keyes says odor can rarely be a sign of early infection or fat necrosis, "though that usually presents with additional symptoms like redness, fever, increasing pain and/or a firm, tender nodule developing."

## Is BBL odor normal, or a sign of infection?

Usually it isn't infection, but it isn't something to expect either. Some people notice a smell from a damp garment, and washing the garment and showering should help. To tell the difference, look at where the odor comes from, whether it is fading or getting stronger, and what comes with it.

| What you notice | Less likely urgent | Call your surgeon |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where the smell comes from | Smell comes from the garment or pads | Smell comes from one incision |
| Over the next days | Improves after washing and showering | Gets stronger |
| Drainage | Some leaking from liposuction incisions for several days | Thick, cloudy white or cream discharge |
| Skin around the incision | Bruised, tender, mildly swollen | Redness spreading past the incision, warmth |
| Your temperature | Normal temperature | Fever above 101°F (38.4°C) |
| Under the skin | Firmness from swelling that slowly softens | A soft pocket of fluid, or a lump that hurts, grows or drains |

This table is general guidance, not a diagnosis. If you aren't sure, call your surgeon. Most signs in the right-hand column come from Cleveland Clinic's guide to [surgical wound infection](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/surgical-wound-infection). "A soft pocket of fluid" and "drains" are our own conservative advice: Cleveland Clinic's [seroma page](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/seroma) says to call if the fluid looks like pus, or if a seroma is getting bigger or causing discomfort, and its [fat necrosis page](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/24187-fat-necrosis) says to contact a provider if it causes pain or seems to grow. The middle column is general guidance, so when you aren't sure, call. Also call if a smell starts again after it had gone away, or if you have chills. Infection signs usually appear 3 to 7 days after surgery, according to Cleveland Clinic, so a new or stronger smell in that first week deserves a call rather than more deodorant.

## How long does BBL smell last?

There is no set number of days. No authoritative source gives a time for how long odor lasts. What matters is the direction: a smell that is fading is healing, and one that is getting stronger is not.

The Aesthetic Society says incisions may drain for several days, but that is a fact about incisions, not a timeline for odor. If the odor stays even though you shower as directed and change into a clean garment every day, ask your surgeon to look at the incisions. A smell that appears later along with other symptoms also needs a call.

## Do BBLs stink years later?

The sources we read describe BBL odor as a recovery issue, and none of them describes it lasting for years. No source we read covers a new smell 2 years after a BBL, so see a doctor, especially if it comes with a lump, drainage or redness.

One late issue to watch for is a lump. Cleveland Clinic says fat necrosis "may feel like a fatty lump or like a hard nodule," and that it usually doesn't hurt. See a doctor if a lump where fat was grafted hurts, grows, turns red or drains.

## What does BBL smell like?

ASPS's article describes it as "musty" or "rancid." A smell that comes from a damp garment should go once the garment is washed and your skin is clean.

If a smell is like stool, check the garment for soiling, which is easy to miss when you can't twist or sit to look. Change into a clean garment and wash the soiled one right away. A strong, foul smell from an incision itself, especially with thick, cloudy discharge, is different. That is the odor Cleveland Clinic lists among the signs of infection.

## How to prevent BBL smell during recovery

A routine that keeps the garment clean and your skin dry goes a long way. Follow your surgeon's written aftercare instructions first; these steps fit inside them.

![Flat lay of a BBL recovery hygiene kit: two folded compression garments, absorbent pads, breathable garment liners, a handheld bidet bottle, a gentle cleanser, a white towel and a mesh laundry bag](IMAGE:hygiene-kit)

1. **Buy two compression garments before surgery.** Dr. Keyes advises changing and washing garments "regularly, ideally every 24 hours," and suggests you "purchase a backup garment in order to have something to wear." Ask your surgeon how to take yours off and put it back on.
2. **Line the garment.** Clean, breathable dressings or liners inside the garment "can help absorb moisture and reduce bacterial buildup," according to Dr. Keyes. Change them whenever they are damp, more often while the incisions are draining.
3. **Shower when your surgeon allows it**, and ask whether to use an antiseptic wash. Dr. Keyes says, "I recommend that patients cleanse themselves with Hibiclens before and after their surgeries until the bottle is empty." Pat incisions dry instead of rubbing.
4. **Make the bathroom easier.** Dr. Keyes says, "I recommend that patients invest in a bidet, especially if they have difficulty wiping after a bowel movement." Keep flushable wipes or a handheld bidet bottle within reach, and change your garment if it gets soiled.
5. **Stay cool.** Loose, breathable clothes over the garment and air conditioning mean less sweat.
6. **Ask about lymphatic drainage massage.** Dr. Keyes says professionally performed lymphatic drainage can reduce fluid retention and swelling, "which may indirectly help minimize odor." Our guide to [massage after a BBL](/how-many-massages-after-bbl) covers what is known.
7. **Don't smoke or vape.** Cleveland Clinic says smoking, including e-cigarettes, increases the risk of a surgical wound infection.
8. **Keep your follow-up appointments**, so your surgeon checks the incisions in person rather than you guessing at home.

The [BBL compression garment timeline](/blog/bbl-compression-garment-timeline) explains how long to wear the garment at each stage, and the [week-by-week BBL recovery timeline](/how-long-to-recover-from-bbl) covers sitting, sleeping and exercise.

## Can the way a BBL is done lower the odds of odor?

Partly, in one surgeon's view. Dr. Keyes says "BBL smell is often associated with high-volume fat transfers." Moving less fat means injecting less fluid during surgery, which he says "usually results in less postoperative fluid drainage, therefore less opportunity for bacterial overgrowth or garment saturation." He offers this as his opinion.

The rest comes down to aftercare and access to your surgeon. Before you book, ask how drainage is handled, what the hygiene instructions are, and how quickly you can be seen if an incision looks or smells wrong in the first week. ## When should you call your surgeon about BBL odor?

<CalloutBox type="warning">
Call your surgeon the same day if you notice an odor coming from an incision, or if you have thick, cloudy discharge, redness spreading past the incision, warmth, pain that is getting worse, or a fever above 101°F (38.4°C).
</CalloutBox>

Also call if you notice a soft, swollen pocket that feels like it holds fluid, or a lump that hurts, grows or drains. Breathing trouble, chest pain or an unusual heartbeat mean you should seek medical attention immediately, so call 911.
