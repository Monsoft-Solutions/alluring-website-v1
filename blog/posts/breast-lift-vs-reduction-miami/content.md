## Do I need a breast lift or a breast reduction?

You likely need a lift if you like your size and your breasts have dropped, and a reduction if they feel too large or too heavy. The [American Society of Plastic Surgeons (ASPS)](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift) says a lift "does not significantly change the size of your breasts" and suggests combining it with a reduction if you want them smaller. If you want them fuller instead, see [breast augmentation vs breast lift](/blog/breast-augmentation-vs-breast-lift).

These signs come from ASPS's candidate pages for [a lift](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/candidates) and [a reduction](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/reconstructive-procedures/breast-reduction/candidates). The last row is our own reading of the two lists. Use this to start the conversation, not to diagnose yourself: your surgeon decides at consultation.

| What you notice | Points toward |
| --- | --- |
| Your nipples fall below the breast crease when unsupported | A lift |
| Your nipples and areolas point downward | A lift |
| Stretched skin and enlarged areolas | A lift |
| Back, neck or shoulder pain from the weight of your breasts | A reduction |
| Indentations from bra straps, or skin irritation under the crease | A reduction |
| Physical activity is hard because of your breast size | A reduction |
| Your breasts feel too large and also hang low | A reduction, often with a lift |

To check where your nipple sits against the crease, read [how to tell if you need a breast lift](/blog/breast-lift-candidate-checklist). The procedure page explains [who a breast lift suits](/procedures/breast-lift-miami#candidate).

## Breast lift vs breast reduction at a glance

A lift changes where the breast sits, and a reduction changes how much breast there is as well as where it sits.

| | Breast lift | Breast reduction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Goal | Raise and reshape | Make smaller and lighter, and reshape |
| Size | Doesn't significantly change (ASPS) | Smaller (ASPS) |
| What's removed | Excess skin (ASPS) | Excess breast tissue and skin (ASPS) |
| Scars | Permanent, and they fade ([ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/procedure)) | Permanent, and they fade (ASPS) |
| Surgery time | About 1 to 3 hours: Cleveland Clinic's pages give 1–2 and 2.5 to 3 | About 3 to 5 hours (Cleveland Clinic) |
| Insurance | Most plans don't cover it (ASPS) | Often covered if you meet your plan's criteria (ASPS, The Aesthetic Society) |
| Back to work | Desk work at about 1 week (Cleveland Clinic) | At least 1 week off, and some people need 2 (Cleveland Clinic) |
| Price at Alluring | $5,000 without implants | $6,000 small to medium, $8,000 large, $9,000 extra large |
| Real results | [Breast lift before and after photos](/gallery/breast-lift) | [Breast reduction before and after photos](/gallery/breast-reduction) |

Prices are list prices, set after an exam, and the surgeon confirms yours at consultation. Surgery and work times are the sources' figures, not a promise from any one practice. Your surgeon tells you yours.

## Does a breast reduction include a lift?

Usually in part. A reduction raises the breast as well as shrinking it, ASPS says, but Lee Daniel, MD, [writing on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/whats-the-difference-between-a-breast-reduction-and-a-breast-lift), says women with both concerns commonly do better with a combination.

ASPS [describes a reduction](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/reconstructive-procedures/breast-reduction) as "the removal of skin and tissue from the breast, followed by reshaping and elevating to create a smaller, more proportionate bust size." [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-reduction/detailed-procedure-information) says the surgeon can "move the nipple-areola complex to a higher position," and Daniel says women often have "a much more lifted appearance" afterward. So ask your surgeon how much lift to expect from your plan.

## When are a lift and a reduction done together?

They go together when you have both too much breast tissue and sagging, which Daniel calls "not unusual." [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/23298-breast-lift) says a lift "can be combined with breast augmentation or reduction." [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/detailed-procedure-information) says your surgeon will likely recommend an anchor incision if you are "getting a breast reduction with your breast lift." That technique "produces the greatest lift, but also leaves the most scars."

None of the pages we read describes a lift and a reduction as two separate operations, the way [a lift with implants](/blog/breast-lift-implants-miami) [can be split in two](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/safety). Ask your surgeon whether your plan is one surgery.

## Will insurance cover a breast lift or a breast reduction?

Insurance usually doesn't pay for a lift and often pays for a reduction, if you meet your plan's rules. [ASPS says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/cost) "most health insurance plans do not cover breast lift surgery or its complications," and [also says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/reconstructive-procedures/breast-reduction/costs) "breast reduction surgery is often covered by health insurance plans."

What coverage for a reduction usually takes:

- **Medical necessity.** [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-reduction/associated-costs) says your insurer must consider it a medical necessity, and you must meet criteria such as height, weight, body surface area and body mass index. Each insurer has its own.
- **Paperwork.** [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11025-breast-reduction) lists photos, height and weight measurements and a letter from your surgeon describing your symptoms. Your carrier also considers how much tissue will be removed.
- **Approval first.** ASPS says your surgeon may need your insurer's approval, and you schedule once it is granted. You may still owe copayments or deductibles.

Call your plan before your consultation and ask for its written breast reduction criteria. Then ask any practice you see whether it works with insurers, and what you would pay if your plan says no.

## How do the scars compare?

Both leave permanent scars that fade, and they use the same incision patterns, so the scar depends on how much needs to change. This comes from The Aesthetic Society's pages on lift incisions and [reduction incisions](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-reduction/incisions-and-scars).

| Incision | Where the scar sits | In a lift | In a reduction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Donut | Around the areola | Mild sagging; the least scarring | A minor reduction; the least scarring |
| Lollipop, or keyhole | Around the areola and down to the crease | Moderate sagging, without implants; moderate scarring | A moderate reduction with moderate sagging; moderate scarring |
| Anchor | Around the areola, down to the crease and along it | Severe sagging, or a lift with a reduction; the most scars | A significant reduction or severe sagging; the most scarring |

The Aesthetic Society calls the anchor "the most common technique" for a reduction. Breast lift scars are permanent but fade a lot, most of it in about 1 year, [Cleveland Clinic says](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/cosmetic-plastic-surgery/procedures/breast-lift). See [where breast lift scars sit and how they fade](/blog/breast-lift-scars-miami) and [the types of breast lift](/blog/types-of-breast-lift).

## How does recovery compare?

The two run close. Daniel puts both at "2 to 4 weeks of downtime," and no society or hospital page we read compares them directly.

| | Breast lift | Breast reduction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First days | Stiff and sore for 2 to 5 days ([The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-lift/aftercare-recovery)) | Tender, stiff and sore for a few days ([The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-reduction/aftercare-recovery)) |
| Lifting | No lifting, driving, laundry or cleaning for the first 2 weeks (The Aesthetic Society) | Limits on lifting and reaching overhead until your surgeon releases you (The Aesthetic Society) |
| Exercise | May not be cleared for up to 6 weeks (Cleveland Clinic) | None for at least 3 to 4 weeks (The Aesthetic Society) |
| Bra | A regular bra at about 8 weeks (Cleveland Clinic) | Still a support bra in weeks 4–5 ([ASPS](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/reconstructive-procedures/breast-reduction/recovery)) |

Call your surgeon right away for any of these, from Cleveland Clinic's lift and reduction pages:

- A breast that becomes red, hot, swollen or painful, or larger with pain and pressure
- Bleeding, drainage, redness or swelling at an incision
- Pus or other unusual discharge, or stitches that come apart early
- A fever (the lift page says higher than 100.5°F)
- Severe pain not helped by medicine
- Dizziness, nausea or vomiting

Call 911 or go to the emergency room for new or severe chest pain, trouble breathing, or pain, redness or swelling in one leg, which [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000132.htm) lists among the symptoms of a blood clot in the lung.

Our [breast lift recovery guide](/blog/breast-lift-recovery-guide-miami) goes week by week. After a reduction, [side sleeping](/when-can-i-sleep-on-my-side-after-breast-reduction) has its own guide.

## Can you breastfeed after a breast lift or a breast reduction?

Often yes after either one, but your supply may be lower and no surgeon can promise it. The [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/breastfeeding-special-circumstances/hcp/illnesses-conditions/breast-surgery.html) says most mothers who have had breast or nipple surgery can make some milk, though maybe not a full supply, and that incisions around the areola and surgery that fully detaches the nipple are more likely to lower production.

[ASPS says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/reconstructive-procedures/breast-reduction/safety) "most women can still breast feed after having a breast reduction," while warning that the surgery may limit it. For a lift, The Aesthetic Society says the nipple stays attached to underlying tissue to help "preserve sensation and the ability to breastfeed," and Cleveland Clinic still lists inability to breastfeed among the risks.

Feeling can change too. Feeling in the nipples and breasts gradually returns over a few weeks or months; some people are numb for up to 1 year, and rarely the loss is permanent, Cleveland Clinic says of a lift. With extremely large breasts, the nipple may have to be removed and placed higher, and [ASPS says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/reconstructive-procedures/breast-reduction/procedure) it then "will not have sensation after the operation." Tell your surgeon if you plan to breastfeed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a mastopexy the same as a breast reduction?

No. Mastopexy is the medical name for a breast lift, which ASPS says raises the breasts by removing excess skin and tightening the surrounding tissue. A breast reduction, or reduction mammaplasty, also removes breast tissue, so your breasts end up smaller. The two get mixed up because a reduction raises the breast as well.

### Will a breast lift make my breasts smaller?

Not significantly, but they can look smaller. ASPS says a lift does not significantly change breast size. Lee Daniel, MD, writing on the ASPS website, explains that because a lift repositions the breasts they "can appear smaller," even though "only excess skin is removed, not the actual breast tissue." If you want to go down a size, that is a reduction.

### Will a breast lift help my back pain?

It isn't meant to. A lift removes skin, not breast tissue, so the weight of your breasts stays about the same. ASPS ties the relief to size: "Women almost always report that having smaller breasts significantly reduces back pain." If you think breast weight is behind your pain, ask about a reduction, and have your doctor look for other causes too.

### Should I have a lift or a reduction after pregnancy?

Choose by size, as at any other time, and wait until you are finished having children if you can. [ASPS says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-lift/results) changes during pregnancy can minimize or reverse a lift's improvement. Shahram Salemy, MD, [writing on the ASPS website](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/better-breasts-after-pregnancy-and-breastfeeding), suggests waiting up to 6 months after breastfeeding stops. Our guide covers [when to have a lift after pregnancy](/blog/breast-lift-miami-post-pregnancy).

### How long do the results last?

A long time, but no source we read gives a number of years. ASPS calls the results of [both surgeries](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/reconstructive-procedures/breast-reduction/results) long-lasting, and Cleveland Clinic says a lift should last for several years. Your breasts keep changing with aging, weight changes, hormones and gravity. For a lift, ASPS says maintaining your weight helps you keep the result longer.

### Can liposuction alone reduce my breasts?

Sometimes. The Aesthetic Society says that if you have fatty breasts, need a minor to moderate reduction and don't need to correct sagging, "you may be able to have a breast reduction using liposuction alone." It says the technique may not suit you if you have significant sagging, a low nipple position or little skin elasticity. Ask your surgeon whether it applies to you.
