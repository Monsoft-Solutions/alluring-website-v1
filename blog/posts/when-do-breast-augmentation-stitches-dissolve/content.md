## When do breast augmentation stitches dissolve?

The stitch ends you can see go first, and the buried material takes much longer to absorb completely, from about 2 months to as long as 8 months depending on the suture. [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/15709-incision-care), writing about surgical wounds in general, says it usually takes one to two weeks before absorbable stitches dissolve, and that they can last for a few months, depending on how your body heals.

## When Do Breast Augmentation Sutures Start to Dissolve?

Usually in the first 1 to 2 weeks, when the ends you can see often fade, in line with Cleveland Clinic's figure for absorbable stitches. Your healing rate plays a part, but the bigger difference is the material. Deeper sutures keep absorbing for months, and that is expected.

The makers publish how long full absorption takes. These figures describe the suture material in general, not breast surgery, and they are examples, since your surgeon's choice may differ. It is a fair thing to ask.

## Which Stitches Are Used in Breast Augmentation?

Understanding what closes your incision gives you insight into your healing. [ASPS says](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/cosmetic-procedures/breast-augmentation/procedure) incisions are closed with layered sutures in the breast tissue and with sutures, skin adhesive or surgical tape to close the skin. So you may have more than one kind at once.

![Comparison card titled Stitches Used in Breast Augmentation. Dissolving stitches: buried under the skin, no removal needed, examples are Vicryl, Monocryl and PDS. Removable stitches, glue or tape: outer stitches come out in about 1 to 2 weeks, and tape or glue comes off in 1 to 2 weeks. Sources: The Aesthetic Society, ASPS and MedlinePlus. A note says to ask your surgeon which you have.](IMAGE:types)

Absorbable sutures, such as polyglactin 910 (Vicryl), poliglecaprone 25 (Monocryl) or polydioxanone (PDS), dissolve on their own over the times in the table above. They need no removal appointment.

Non-absorbable stitches need professional removal. [The Aesthetic Society](https://www.theaestheticsociety.org/procedures/breast/breast-augmentation/aftercare-recovery) says that if you have external sutures, your surgeon will remove them in about a week, and that tissue glue or tape will fall off on its own in a week or two. [MedlinePlus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/patientinstructions/000273.htm), writing about cosmetic breast surgery in general, says any stitches will be removed within 2 weeks. [A UW Medicine patient handout](https://healthonline.washington.edu/document/health_online/pdf/Breast-Augmentation.pdf) describes stitches under the skin with strips of white tape, and says to remove the strips after 2 weeks if they are still on.

We found no study comparing suture types for scarring after breast augmentation, so be wary of anyone who says one type always scars less. Your surgeon selects the types of stitches based on your anatomy and surgical technique. It is a fair question to ask at your follow-up.

## What Affects How Fast Breast Stitches Dissolve?

Several things influence how quickly your breast augmentation stitches dissolve, and the material is the largest.

![Tile card titled What Affects How Fast Stitches Dissolve? Suture material makes the biggest difference. Depth: buried stitches last longer than what you see. Your own healing varies from person to person. Clean and dry: follow your surgeon. Sources: suture makers' data and Cleveland Clinic.](IMAGE:what-affects)

The sutures used play the main role, as the table shows: from about 56 days for Vicryl to as long as 238 days for PDS. The makers of Coated Vicryl Plus and PDS describe them as absorbed by hydrolysis, meaning water in your tissues breaks them down slowly.

Your individual healing matters too. Cleveland Clinic says absorbable stitches can last for a few months, depending on how your body heals. We found no good data on how much age or skin type changes the timing.

Surgical technique matters as well, since deeper stitches last longer than surface closures. Keeping the area clean and dry protects your incision while this happens, though nothing we read shows that it or any other care routine speeds up absorption.

## How Do You Know If Breast Stitches Are Healing?

As your body begins healing after breast augmentation, a few signs can reassure you that your surgical sites are mending properly.

Stitch ends that fade in the first 1 to 2 weeks are normal progress. You want minimal inflammation at the incision site, and no discharge, which suggests the wound is closing appropriately. As weeks pass, incision lines should gradually flatten and lighten in color.

Keep an eye on your stitches. Persistent prominence or discomfort may signal something that needs attention.

## Why Does a Stitch Poke Out, and What If It Does?

A stitch end poking out is usually a reason to call, not to pull. Cleveland Clinic, writing about surgical wounds in general, says a healthcare provider may remove dissolvable stitches before they dissolve if they bother you, and not to try to remove these stitches at home. We found no reliable source on how often it happens after breast augmentation. Keep the area clean and let your surgeon's office look at it. It is more urgent if you also notice redness, drainage or increasing discomfort around it.

## Symptoms of Torn Internal Stitches After Breast Augmentation

No source we read lists symptoms of a torn internal stitch specifically, since you can't see one. What sources do list are signs that an incision or the tissue under it is in trouble. Cleveland Clinic names rupture of your stitches and abnormal discharge, such as pus, from your incision site among the symptoms to report immediately. MedlinePlus adds increasing pain, redness, swelling, yellow or green drainage, bleeding or bruising at the incision site.

If your incision opens, or one breast suddenly swells or changes shape, call your surgeon the same day. If one breast looks higher or more swollen without those signs, our [swelling and asymmetry FAQ](/blog/swelling-and-asymmetry-after-breast-augmentation-faq) can help you sort out what's normal. Sudden swelling has other causes as well, and your surgeon needs to see it.

## How Can You Help Stitches Dissolve After Surgery?

Proper care following breast augmentation protects your incisions while your dissolving stitches absorb.

Keep the surgical area clean and dry to help prevent infection. Wear your support bra around the clock for as long as your surgeon tells you, removing it only to shower. University instructions say 3 to 4 weeks, and [your surgeon sets your own length](/procedures/breast-augmentation-miami#recovery). This limits strain on your incisions as they heal.

Showers usually restart after about 2 days. Baths, pools and the ocean wait at least about a month, [an article on the ASPS website by Dr. Cindy Wu](https://www.plasticsurgery.org/news/blog/considering-breast-augmentation-for-beach-season-heres-when-you-should-plan-your-surgery) says, and until your incisions are fully closed and your surgeon agrees. See [when you can swim](/how-long-after-breast-augmentation-can-i-swim). Heavy lifting waits too: [Cleveland Clinic](https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/treatments/11024-breast-augmentation) advises none for at least 4 weeks.

Monitor your healing by checking for normal recovery signs: minimal redness, decreasing swelling and no unusual discharge. Complete absorption takes about 2 to 8 months, depending on the specific material used. Following your surgeon's post-operative instructions supports a smoother recovery.

## Frequently asked questions

### When do breast augmentation stitches start to dissolve?

Usually within 1 to 2 weeks for the ends you can see, in line with what Cleveland Clinic says about absorbable stitches. The buried material takes longer to disappear: about 56 to 70 days for Coated Vicryl Plus, 91 to 119 days for Monocryl and 182 to 238 days for PDS, by the makers' figures. Stitches that need removing come out in about a week to two weeks.

### What types of stitches are used in breast augmentation surgery?

ASPS says incisions are closed with layered sutures in the breast tissue, then sutures, skin adhesive or surgical tape on the skin. Absorbable sutures dissolve on their own over about 2 to 8 months, depending on the material. Non-absorbable stitches, if used, need removal, in about a week to two weeks.

### How can I tell if my breast augmentation stitches are healing properly?

Signs of proper healing include minimal inflammation, gradually flattening incision lines and no discharge at the incision site. If your stitches remain prominent or you have unusual discomfort, consult your surgeon to rule out a complication. Spreading redness, heat, pus or an incision that opens need a call right away.

### What affects how quickly breast stitches dissolve after augmentation?

The suture material matters most: makers' figures run from about 56 days for Vicryl to 238 days for PDS. Depth matters too, since buried stitches last longer than what you can see. Cleveland Clinic says timing also depends on how your body heals. We found no good data on age or skin type.

### How should I care for my stitches after breast augmentation surgery?

Keep your incision sites clean and dry, and wear your support bra around the clock for as long as your surgeon tells you, removing it only to shower. University handouts say 3 to 4 weeks. Follow your surgeon's tape instructions; one handout says to remove strips after 2 weeks if they are still on.

### Is it normal to still see stitches after a few weeks?

It can be. Cleveland Clinic says absorbable stitches can last for a few months, so a stitch you can still see isn't automatically a problem. A stitch end poking out is a reason to call, not to pull. If you notice increased redness, drainage or discomfort around it, contact your surgeon sooner rather than later.

### What are signs of complications with dissolving stitches after breast augmentation?

Warning signs include spreading redness, warmth, a new lump, pus or bad-smelling drainage at the incision site, or an incision that opens. These may mean infection or a problem with the stitches. Fever thresholds differ between hospitals, so use the number your surgeon gave you, and reach out promptly for evaluation.

### Can I do anything to help my breast stitches dissolve faster?

Nothing we read shows that anything speeds up absorption, and the makers of Coated Vicryl Plus and PDS describe the process as hydrolysis, which runs on its own timetable. What you can do is protect the incision: keep it clean and dry, wear your support bra as instructed, avoid strain on your chest and follow your surgeon's instructions.
